**Main:** Never write a Korean\nname in red
**Sub:** This one is worth actually following. It is not obscure superstition — it is widely enough held that a red-inked name on a card reads as unpleasant rather than quirky. Use any other colour.

## Card 05
mode: swap
kicker: THE EXPENSIVE MISTAKE
image: sources/seoul-market-stalls.jpg
image_label: A Seoul market street lined with produce stalls and Korean shop signage
avoid_label: A very expensive gift
avoid_why: It creates repayment pressure the other person did not ask for
instead_label: Something modest and well presented

**Main:** Generous can read\nas a debt
**Sub:** Korean gift-giving runs on rough reciprocity. A gift far above what the relationship calls for obliges the other person to match it, which is a burden rather than a kindness. Modest and neat beats large and awkward.

## Card 06
mode: swap
kicker: HOW YOU HAND IT OVER
image: sources/card_06-two-hands.jpg
image_label: Two people exchanging a wrapped gift box, both using both hands
avoid_label: Handing it over one-handed
avoid_why: Reads as casual when the moment is not
instead_label: Both hands, and take it back with both hands too

**Main:** The gesture matters\nas much as the box
**Sub:** Use two hands if the setting is formal or the other person is older than you — and do the same when receiving. It costs nothing and it is the single easiest piece of Korean etiquette to get visibly right.
