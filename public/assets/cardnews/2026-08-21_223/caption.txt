22 countries don't need K-ETA to enter Korea. Almost none of them are done. ✈️

Korea's Ministry of Justice (법무부) suspended K-ETA for 22 countries and regions, and the current extension runs to 31 December 2026. The exemption doesn't clear your desk, though. It moves the job.

📌 EXEMPT FROM K-ETA, through 31 Dec 2026
Asia — Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Macau
Americas — United States (incl. Guam), Canada
Europe — UK, Germany, France, Italy, Netherlands, Spain, Poland, Sweden, Finland, Norway, Belgium, Denmark, Austria
Oceania — New Zealand, Australia
Any passport — anyone aged 17 and under, or 65 and over

📌 You still owe the e-Arrival Card
Free, required of most foreign arrivals, and it only opens in the 3 days before you land. Korea retired the paper card in December 2025, so there is nothing to fill in on the plane any more.

📌 The trade nobody explains
Holding a valid K-ETA waives the e-Arrival Card. So the optional ₩10,000 cancels the free mandatory one — for three years.
→ One trip, no plans to return: do the free one.
→ Back within three years: the ₩10,000 is arguably the better buy.

📌 Timing
K-ETA — before boarding, allow 72 hours. It is permission to board, not to enter, and the airline is what stops you, long before Korea does.
e-Arrival Card — within the 3 days before arrival.

📌 The scam tell
There is no expedited K-ETA at any price, so anyone selling rush processing has told you exactly what they are. And the arrival card is free — any charge to submit one is not the government.
e-arrivalcard.go.kr · k-eta.go.kr — Korean government sites end in .go.kr

And one that surprises people: Korea stopped stamping passports in 2016. You get a printed entry confirmation instead, and its permitted-until date — not your paperwork — is how long you may stay.

Nothing has been announced for 2027. If you're planning next year, check the dates again rather than trusting any list, including this one.
