Every English guide to Korean kimchi containers compares the lid. Korean buying advice starts somewhere else. 🥬

It starts with a flat plate that goes INSIDE.

📌 누름판 (nureumpan) — THE PRESS PLATE
It sits on top of the packed kimchi and keeps it submerged in its own brine. That is the whole job, and it matters because of what kimchi does while it ferments: it rises. Gas lifts the mass, the top clears the liquid, and the exposed surface meets air.

Two things follow, and neither is what you want:
▪️ 골마지 — a white yeast film on the surface. Not mould, but nobody wants it.
▪️ The edges above the brine dry out and go leathery.

Press it under and neither happens. Korean buying guides rank this alongside capacity and seal. English listings barely mention it.

📏 THE FRIDGE PICKS THE SIZE
Korea has a second refrigerator that exists to hold one food, and it is fixed compartments rather than open shelves. So the containers are built to tile:
▪️ 7L — 26 × 17 × 22cm
▪️ 3.2L — 26 × 17 × 11cm

Identical footprint. Only the height changes. One deep container or two shallow ones fill the same drawer.

🔘 THE BUTTON ON THE LID IS NOT A GIMMICK
Kimchi keeps fermenting after you pack it, and fermentation makes carbon dioxide. A genuinely airtight box has two ways to fail: pressure builds until the lid pops, or brine forces its way out through the seal and into your fridge. The one-way valve lets gas out and does not let air back in.

🥄 STAINLESS OR PLASTIC
The honest answer depends on how long red kimchi sits in it. Stainless does not stain or hold the smell. Plastic is lighter and cheaper and eventually does both. Two-week turnover, plastic is fine. A 김장 batch meant to last through winter is what makes steel worth it.

Buying one? Check for the press plate before you check anything else.
