name_ko: 누름판
name_en: nureumpan — press plate

**Main:** It keeps the kimchi\nunder its own brine
**Sub:** Fermentation makes the mass **rise**. The top clears the liquid, meets air, and two things follow: **골마지**, a white yeast film, and edges that dry and toughen. Press it under and neither happens.

## Card 04
mode: stamp
kicker: THE BUTTON IS NOT A GIMMICK
image: sources/kimchi-container-air-valve.jpg
fit: cover
image_position: center 50%
image_opacity: 0.90
image_label: A hand pressing the round one-way air valve set into the clear lid of a stainless kimchi container

**Main:** Gas out, air not back in
**Sub:** Kimchi keeps fermenting after you pack it, and fermentation makes **carbon dioxide**. A truly airtight box either deforms until the lid pops, or forces brine out through the seal and into your fridge. **The valve is what makes sealing safe.**

**Main:** Two weeks, or six months
**Sub:** For a tub you finish in a fortnight the press plate barely matters. For a **김장** batch meant to last through winter it is the difference between kimchi that stays submerged and kimchi that grows a film on top.

**Main:** Stainless or plastic
**Sub:** The honest answer depends on **how long red kimchi sits in it.** Stainless does not stain or hold the smell; plastic is lighter and cheaper and eventually does both. Short turnover, plastic is fine. Long storage is what makes steel worth it.
