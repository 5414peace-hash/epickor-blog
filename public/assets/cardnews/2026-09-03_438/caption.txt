Korea tested 56 ramyun pots with acid. The uncoated ones leached thirteen times more aluminium. 🍜

The pot every Korean drama boils ramyun in is called 양은냄비 — "nickel silver pot." It contains no nickel silver. 양은 (洋銀) is copper alloyed with zinc and nickel, and modern ones are aluminium. The metal changed decades ago; the name never did.

So is it safe? Korea actually measured it, and English-language advice almost never cites the study.

🔬 THE TEST — Gyeonggi Institute of Health and Environment
56 pots sold in Korea, boiled with citric acid at pH 3.5.

▪️ 47 UNCOATED POTS — 23.90 mg/L average
Worst single pot: 115.21 mg/L.

▪️ 9 COATED POTS — 1.78 mg/L average
Worst: 8.72. About thirteen times less.

The coating is the whole decision. Not the shape, not the brand, not the price.

🌶 WHAT PULLS THE METAL IS ACID AND SALT, NOT HEAT
김치찌개 9.86 mg/kg (pH 4.6, 0.98% salt) · 피클 2.86 · 김치라면 2.34 · 된장찌개 1.64. Boiling plain water barely registers.

📏 NOW THE ARITHMETIC
식약처 puts the safe daily intake near 17 mg for a 60kg adult, and notes only about 1% is absorbed. A 300g serving of the worst dish, cooked in a bare pot, is roughly a sixth of that. A bowl of kimchi ramyun works out near 1.4 mg — if you drink all the broth.

The honest answer is conditional yes, not panic.

⚠️ THE ONE HABIT THAT UNDOES ALL OF IT
A steel scrubber strips the coating in a single pass. That turns a 1.78 pot into a 23.90 one, and nothing you cook afterwards undoes it. Soft sponge only. Don't long-simmer anything acidic in a bare pot. Replace it when the film goes.
