Seoul is not one city. Pick the neighbourhood first.

Most guides hand you a list of cafes. That is the wrong order — these places feel genuinely different from each other, and the reason is usually historical. Swipe for five of them, with the line you get there on.

• 연남동 Yeonnam-dong (Line 2 · Hongik Univ) — the long thin park everyone sits in is a railway line. Seoul buried the Yongsan track between 2012 and 2016 and kept the shape. Half a day.
• 을지로 Euljiro (Lines 2, 3 · Euljiro 3-ga) — machine shops and print works by day, the best drinking alley in Seoul after 7pm. The bars exist because the printers worked night shifts. An evening.
• 익선동 Ikseon-dong (Lines 1, 3, 5 · Jongno 3-ga) — Seoul's oldest surviving hanok village, and it was built as a property development in the late 1920s, not preserved as one. 1–2 hours.
• 해방촌 Haebangchon (Line 6 · Noksapyeong) — Liberation Village, built by refugees on the slope of Namsan after 1945. Walk it downhill, not up. 2–3 hours.
• 문래동 Mullae (Line 2 · Mullae) — artists' studios above working steel workshops. Nothing here was built for visitors, which is the reason to go. 2–3 hours.
