Most Korean homes run two refrigerators. One holds food. The other holds only kimchi. 🇰🇷

Because kimchi is alive. Fresh, it is crunchy and bright; weeks later, sour and deep. Every household has a favourite point on that curve — and the enemy is not time, it is temperature swing. A normal fridge cycles its compressor and swings several degrees. That is what pushes kimchi past where you wanted it.

The ancestor is a hole in the ground. Every autumn, households buried 옹기 onggi jars up to the neck. The jar was the container; the winter soil was the refrigerator, holding a steady low temperature no appliance was offering. Then Korea moved into apartments, and the yard went away.

김장 kimjang makes the problem acute. The communal kimchi-making that UNESCO lists as intangible heritage produces months of kimchi in a single November day. All of it needs the same cold, stable place at once. Apartments had none.

1995: a car-parts maker launched the 딤채 Dimchae — 4,000 units the first year, 20,000 the next, and the brand name went generic the way Kleenex did. Samsung and LG followed. Thirty years on, Dimchae's maker Winia has been through court-led restructuring, which is worth knowing before you buy on warranty.

Honest verdict: one jar a month? Your normal fridge is fine, and a $30 sealed container answers the question first. Making kimchi, or buying it by the kilo? The machine earns its floor space. 🥬
