In Korea the massage chair is not furniture. It is what adult children buy their parents. 💺

The flagship lists at $14,990 in the US. Bodyfriend's Pharaoh NEO. That is not an outlier price in this category — it is the top of a ladder that a lot of Korean families are actually climbing.

Japan invented it. Korea moved it into the living room. Japanese brands built the massage chair in the 1950s and kept it in bathhouses and hotel lobbies — coin-operated, public, transactional. Bodyfriend, founded in 2007 by two computer-industry veterans, overtook the originators within about a decade by making it domestic furniture.

Two gift seasons a year built the industry. Seollal and Chuseok formalise what children owe parents, and the massage chair sits at the top of that ladder — delivered to the living room, where every visitor sees it. The gift is partly the object and partly the fact that it is visible.

The category Japan invented is now a Korean duel. Ceragem took the revenue crown in 2021; Bodyfriend holds standalone revenue and the better margins. Roughly a trillion-won market, fought between two Korean companies.

Safety, and this one is not optional. Korea's consumer agency logged 631 injury reports in 44 months, and the largest injured group was children aged 0–6, caught in the motorised leg section. If someone is trapped, cut the power — do not pull. ⚠️

Honest verdict: a $150 massage gun proves whether your household actually uses mechanical massage every day, for about two percent of the flagship's price. Prove the habit before you give up the floor space.
