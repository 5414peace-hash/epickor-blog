## Primary Caption

Why do Koreans eat so much garlic?

Because in Korean food, garlic is not just a garnish.

It is part of the base flavor.
It goes into kimchi seasoning.
It wakes up jjigae.
It sharpens Korean BBQ wraps.
It adds punch to banchan and sauces.

And culturally, it has a funny mythic layer too.

In the Dangun foundation myth, the bear survives on garlic and mugwort before becoming human.
Modern Koreans do not literally eat garlic because of that story, of course.
But the joke works because garlic already feels deeply Korean.

The real reason is practical:
Korean food loves contrast.

Fatty pork belly needs sharpness.
Fermented flavors need body.
Rice and banchan need strong small bites.
Garlic helps connect all of that.

So garlic in Korea is not background flavor.
It is one of the voices at the table.
