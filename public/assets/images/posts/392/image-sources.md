# Image sources — Blog 392 (Kimchi refrigerators)

All three images show the article's actual subjects — the two form factors of the machine, and
the buried-jar system it replaced. No caption needs to argue for its image's relevance.

## 1. `kimchi-fridge-top-load-open.jpg` (hero, ogImage)

- **Source:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kimchi_refrigerator.jpg
- **Author:** **국립국어원 (National Institute of Korean Language)** — attribution required,
  in the caption.
- **Licence:** **CC BY-SA 2.0 KR**
- **Original:** 800x1200. Delivered 800x1200, **89 KB**.
- **What it shows, verified by opening it:** a red top-loading chest kimchi refrigerator with
  the lid open, **a bag of kimchi sitting inside the chilled well**, and a Korean energy-rating
  label on the front.
- **Why it is the hero:** the top-loading well is the design argument of the whole article —
  cold air sinks, a lid holds it, exactly like the buried jar. The photo shows the machine
  doing its one job, kimchi in place.

## 3. `lg-kimchi-fridge-drawer-type.jpg`

- **Source:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kimchi_refrigerator3.jpg
- **Author:** Jose Gabriel Marcelino — attribution required, in the caption.
- **Licence:** **CC BY 2.0**
- **Original:** 2736x3648. Delivered 1200x1600, 142 KB.
- **What it shows, verified at full size:** an **LG-branded** stand-type kimchi refrigerator
  with the lower drawer pulled out, sealed containers of kimchi and side dishes inside, and a

- **Why here:** the drawer stand-type is the form that now dominates the market, and the LG
  branding matches the article's point that Samsung and LG took over the category the inventor
  created. The caption names LG because LG is verifiably in the frame.

## Manufacturer route (0차) — attempted, and why Commons won

- `dimchae.com` — **SSL certificate verification failure**; unreachable from this environment.
- `dimchaeusa.com` / `winiaaid.com` — reachable but fully JS-rendered; zero extractable images.
- The manufacturer is also **in court liquidation as of 2026-07-02**, which the article covers;
  its web assets may not outlive the wind-up, making hotlink-era sourcing from it unwise anyway.
- The Commons hero is government-authored (국립국어원), Korea-shot, and shows the product in
  use — which for this article beats a studio packshot: the machine's *use* (kimchi in the
  well) is the story.

## Rejected

- **`KimchiStorageKoreanFolkVillage.jpg`** (public domain, 480x360) — a Korean Folk Village
  exhibit signboard titled "The pottery of kimchi and the refrigerator of kimchi!". Charming
  and directly on-topic, but it is a photograph of a text panel at 480px — unreadable at
  article width, and a picture of words is a weak image. The article makes the panel's point
  in its own prose instead.
- **LG DIOS promotional event photos** (Commons, 2012) — comedians at a marketing event for a
  general DIOS refrigerator; not a kimchi refrigerator, not informative.
