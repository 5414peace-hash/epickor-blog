## korea-hiring-target-system.jpg (1400x860, 117KB)
EpicKor original chart from the Ministry of Personnel Management's balanced-personnel annual
report: candidates added by the target system, 2003–2010 (women 1,095 / men 614), 2011–2020
(women 650 / men 1,725), cumulative across all grades (women 1,205 / men 2,332), national grade 7
(women 194 / men 20) and national grade 5 (women 43 / men 19).

**A first version of this chart was rejected and rebuilt.** It scaled every bar against a shared
maximum, which made 여성 194 at grade 7 render longer than 여성 1,205 in the cumulative row — a
reader glancing at it would have read the smaller number as the larger one. In an article about
people misreading a statistic, shipping a chart that invites the same misreading would have been
self-defeating. The rebuilt version normalises each row to its own split and prints the absolute
counts on the bars, so the visual claim and the numeric claim agree.

## Why charts and not photographs

Neither of these facts is a visual property of anything. "Which policies were repealed and when"
and "who the target system actually topped up" cannot be photographed, and a stock image of a
Korean street or a subway car would have been a mood photo attached to a numbers story — the
substitution the Blog Reference Image Standard names as insufficient. This is the same call
recorded in FACTS on 2026-08-18 and applied again on `417` and `418`.

## Rejected
- **Photographs of pink parking bays or pink subway seats.** Available on stock libraries, and
  actively misleading here: the article's point about Seoul is that those bays were repainted as
  family-consideration parking in 2023, so a pink-bay photograph would illustrate the claim the
  article is correcting. The paint outlasts the policy, which is precisely the trap.
- **Photographs of protests or political figures.** This piece reports what the rules say; putting
  a demonstration or a politician's face on it would signal a side the text does not take.
- **Ministry building exteriors and government seals.** Available via public sources, and empty —
  they identify the institution without carrying any of the information the article turns on.
