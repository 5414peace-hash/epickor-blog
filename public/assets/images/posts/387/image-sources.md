# Image sources — Blog 387 (Why Korean highballs cost more than beer)

Commons has **no Korean canned highball** at all. What it has is Japanese — Jim Beam
Highball, Torys, chuhai cans — and using any of those would be a country mismatch on
an article whose entire subject is Korean tax law. So the hero came from the 0차
route: the manufacturer's own material.

## 1. `earp-earp-highball-cu.jpg` (hero, ogImage)

- **Source:** BGF Retail / CU official newsroom, https://www.bgf.co.kr/bgflive/view/?id=339
- **Original:** 800x726, 356 KB. Delivered 800px mozjpeg, **71 KB**.
- **What it shows:** three EARP EARP cans — lemon tonic and Earl Grey — with the
  brand's bear character. **The label is legible: `ABV 9.0%`, `500㎖`.**
- **Why it earned its place twice over.** It is the exact product the article prices,
  and it **corrected the draft**: the text had been written with "~5% ABV" from a
  secondary source. CU's own page gives 9%, and the can in the photograph agrees.
  The launch date (23 November 2022), the ₩4,500 single price and the ₩3,700
  three-can price all came from the same official page.
- Editorial product identification. No sponsorship implied, and none exists.

## 2. `cass-fresh-bottle.jpg`

- **Source:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CassFresh.jpg
- **Author:** Miguel Andrade · **Licence: CC0** (credited anyway, as house practice)
- **Original:** 727x2324. Delivered 700px, 202 KB.
- **What it shows:** a real bottle of Cass Fresh on a table, label showing **4.5%**.
- **Use:** placed against the section explaining that beer has been taxed by volume
  since 2020 while spirits stayed on price — the mechanism that lets this bottle
  undercut the can beside it.

## Rejected: `Jinro soju 1.jpg`

Commons has a well-lit, KOGL Type 1 licensed Jinro bottle from the **National Museum
of Korea**, and it would have slotted straight into the soju row of the comparison
table. **It is a museum object** — a 70th-anniversary commemorative bottle, catalogue
number 55530, square clear glass, **30% ABV**.

The article's soju row is about the ₩1,900 green 360 ml bottle at 16–17% that people
actually buy. Captioning a commemorative artifact as "the soju you buy" would have
been the same error as the 2026-08-03 card-news incident and this morning's caption on
Blog 385. **Opening the file is what caught it** — the licence, the resolution and the
filename all looked fine.

## Checks run

- **Cross-post uniqueness:** first use of both on this site. Note that Blog 386,
  published the same day, uses a *different* soju photograph — no image is shared
  between the two alcohol posts.
- **Exact-subject rule:** the hero is the specific product named and priced; the beer
  is the specific brand named in the table.
- **Size:** 71 + 202 KB = **273 KB** for the post.
- **Captions:** written after viewing each file at full size. Both state what is in
  frame; neither apologises for its image.

## Gap worth noting

There is **no photograph of a Korean convenience-store highball shelf** — the wall of
thirty-odd cans that the article describes. That single image would do more than
either of the two used here, and neither Commons nor the chains' newsrooms appear to
have one under a usable licence. Worth shooting if anyone from EpicKor is in Korea.
