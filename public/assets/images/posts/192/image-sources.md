# Image Sources - Blog 192

Post: Olive Young Korea Guide: When to Shop, What It Costs, and What to Skip

## 2026-09-04 refresh — two real Olive Young photographs replaced two stock scenes

The article named Olive Young in its title and used four stock images: a generic Seoul shopping
street, a shopper reading a label, a cosmetics shelf, and a skincare flatlay. Every caption had to
explain what the picture stood for, which is the tell.

**The search that found the real thing was `oliveyoung` with no space.** Searching Commons for
`olive young` returns a 1920s Chinese-American film actress of that name and nothing else — eight
straight portraits. The solid-compound form returns the store: `OliveYoung store.png`,
`OliveYoung Skin-care zone.jpg`, `OliveYoung Make-up zone.jpg`, `OliveYoung Snack zone.jpg`, and
`Olive Young Myeongdong.jpg`. **Korean brand romanisations are often written solid, so try the
no-space form before concluding a brand is unphotographed.** New wrinkle on the romanised-search
rule established for 양은냄비 and 동묘.

**`OliveYoung store.png` was avoided** — Blog `388` already uses it as `olive-young-store-front.jpg`.
Checked before selecting.

## The hero photograph contains the article's correction

`oliveyoung-myeongdong-checkout.jpg` was picked as a store-scale shot and turned out to carry the
single most useful fact in the refresh. Enlarged from the 4000px original, the illuminated strip
along **every one of the fourteen checkout counters** reads:

> **PLEASE PREPARE YOUR PHYSICAL PASSPORT (FOR IMMEDIATE TAX REFUND)** 😊 (为了及时免税) 请准备好护照原件

The pre-refresh article told readers to bring *"your passport or a clear passport copy."* **A copy
does not work**, and the store says so in three languages on fourteen lanes because of how often
people try. The correction and the photograph proving it are the same object — the same thing that
happened with the sesame-oil mill window in `439`, the Dongmyo price cards in `242`, and the
presidential plaques in the same post.

Cropped and read at full resolution before it went in the caption. **Enlarge the text in a
photograph you chose for scale; it may be telling you something the body text has wrong.**
