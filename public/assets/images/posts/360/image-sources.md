## Why these, and why the manufacturer route was not needed

The 0차 rule sends packaged goods to the maker's site first. Lotte Chilsung's product pages
(`lottechilsung.co.kr`, `mall.lottechilsung.co.kr`) are JavaScript-rendered and return an empty
shell to a plain fetch, so no pack shot was retrievable. Commons turned out to hold better material
anyway: **Korean-market packaging photographed at 2988 px with the Korean label legible**, which a
marketing render would not have given us.

Both Milkis files were verified by looking at them rather than trusting the filename — the
`Ji Sung` / `Yoon Ji-sung` mix-up on Blog `167` earlier the same day is why.

## The labels are doing evidential work

- `milkis-bottles.jpg` — 500 ml Korean bottles. `밀키스` in Hangul, the slogan `사랑해요! Milkis`,
  `New feeling of soda beverage`, and `500 ml (250 kcal)` are all readable, and the cloudy
  milk-white liquid is visible, which is the drink's whole identity.
- `milkis-can.jpg` — the 250 ml can turned to its Korean nutrition panel: `250 ml (130 kcal)`,
  `탄산음료` as the food category, 0 g fat, 0 mg cholesterol, and whey powder in the ingredient
  list. The article's calorie and category claims are printed in the photograph.

## The Ambasa can

`File:Ambasa can (South Korea, 80s).jpg` is held by the **National Folk Museum of Korea** and
released under **KOGL Type 1**, which permits commercial use and derivative works with attribution.
It is a 1980s Korean-market can, so it illustrates the Milkis-versus-Ambasa section with a period
artefact rather than a current retail photo — appropriate, since the section is about which of the
two came first.
