# Image sources — Blog 389 (Korean bidets)

**Commons has no photograph of a Korean bidet control panel.** What it has is Japanese
— TOTO Washlet panels — and the manufacturer route failed too: `cowayusa-shop.com`
returned HTML instead of images for both product files. So neither image here is the
obvious one, and both are used for what they genuinely show.

## 1. `seoul-shop-bidet-water-purifier-2007.jpg` (hero, ogImage)

- **Source:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:August_2007,_Toilet_seat_for_sale_in_Seoul.jpg
- **Author:** Syced · **Licence: CC0** (credited anyway, as house practice)
- **Original:** 3072x2304, shot August 2007. Delivered 1300px, **107 KB**.
- **What it actually shows, verified by opening it:** a Seoul shop floor with a
  **Woongjin water purifier, an electronic bidet seat and a water softener standing in
  a row**, under Korean promotional signage with `신상품` stickers.
- **Why it is the hero.** The article's central claim is that the bidet spread through
  Korea because it was sold on the same rental counter as the water purifier, not
  because of anything cultural. This photograph *is* that claim — three rental
  appliances side by side — and it was taken in 2007, five years after the 2002
  Woongjin Coway `LooLoo` campaign that the article dates the takeoff to. A staged
  product shot could not have carried the argument this well.

## 2. `toto-washlet-control-panel.jpg`

- **Source:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wireless_toilet_control_panel_w._open_lid.jpg
- **Author:** Chris 73 / Wikimedia Commons — **attribution required, in the caption.**
- **Licence:** **CC BY-SA 3.0** (dual-licensed with GFDL 1.3+)
- **Original:** 800x750. Delivered 800px, 94 KB.
- **What it shows:** a TOTO Washlet wireless panel, 38 buttons, **all labelled in
  Japanese** — 止, おしり, やわらか, ビデ, 乾燥.
- **The care point.** This is a **Japanese** panel in an article whose main table
  teaches **Korean** button words. It is placed against the paragraph on TOTO's 1980
  invention, and the caption says "the ancestor, and it is Japanese" before mapping the
  four Japanese labels onto their Korean equivalents. Presenting it as a Korean panel
  would have been the same failure as the 2026-08-03 card-news incident.

## Rejected

- **`Coway Neo Plus White.jpg`** (CC BY 4.0) — a Coway **water purifier**, not a bidet.
  Correctly licensed, correct brand, wrong product. Opening it is what caught that.
- **`cowayusa-shop.com` product images** — both URLs returned `<!DOCTYPE HTML` at 4 KB.
  Blocked, not missing.
- **Kohler Novita product shots via bidetking.com** — a retailer rather than the
  manufacturer, with unclear reuse rights. The retailer page was still valuable: it
  **corrected the article's prices**, which had been drafted from a marketplace summary
  claiming "$70 to over $350". Real Novita seats list at $425–$999.

## Checks run

- **Cross-post uniqueness:** first use of both. Post 385 uses different Coway imagery
  and post 140 different toilet imagery; nothing is shared.
- **Size:** 107 + 94 KB = **201 KB** for the post.
- **Captions:** written after viewing each file at full size. The Japanese panel is
  labelled Japanese in its own caption.

## Gap worth noting

The single most useful missing image is **a Korean bidet control panel with Hangul
labels** — the exact thing the article's main table describes. Nothing openly licensed
appears to exist. Photographing one in any Korean hotel bathroom would take ten
seconds and would improve this post more than anything else available.
