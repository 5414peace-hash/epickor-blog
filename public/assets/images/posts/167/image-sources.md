## What was removed, and why

Three Pexels stock photos were deleted, not merely unlinked:

- `pexels-4009401.jpg` — "a couple watching Netflix in a cozy living room"
- `pexels-5202957.jpg` — "a person using a remote"
- `pexels-7991318.jpg` — "people in a cinema with red seats"

None of them showed Korea, a Korean drama, or anything the article discusses. Under the Blog
Reference Image Standard a mood photo is not sufficient for a post about specific named shows, and
these were not even Korea-shot. A press photograph of an actor who leads one of the named dramas is
a direct reference to the article's actual subject; a stranger holding a remote is not.

## Why actors rather than drama stills

Drama stills and posters are copyrighted by the broadcasters and streamers and cannot be used here.
Press-event photographs of the cast are the closest **licensable** image of the real subject, and
each caption names the specific drama that person leads — so the image is doing identification work
rather than decoration.

## One trap avoided

A Commons search for `Ji Sung` — lead of 판사 이한영, the ratings story of the year — returns
**Yoon Ji-sung**, a different person and a singer, not the actor. Nothing was downloaded from that
search. This is the same "the filename lied" failure recorded in `FACTS.md` from the Binggrae banana
milk case, and it is why files are checked against the pixels before use rather than trusted by
title. Ji Sung has no usable free-licensed photograph on Commons, so the article carries his section
without a portrait rather than shipping a photo of the wrong man.
