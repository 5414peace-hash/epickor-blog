# Image sources — Blog 388 (Is Korean sunscreen banned in the US?)

This article is about regulation, which photographs badly. Both images were chosen to
carry an argument the text makes rather than to decorate it, and both were opened and
viewed on a contact sheet before either caption was written.

## 1. `olive-young-store-front.jpg` (hero, ogImage)

- **Source:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:OliveYoung_store.png
- **Author:** Pkccccj — **attribution required, in the caption.**
- **Licence:** **CC BY-SA 4.0**
- **Original:** 1280x820 PNG. Delivered 1200px JPEG, mozjpeg, **87 KB**.
- **What it shows:** an illuminated Olive Young shopfront at night, signage reading
  `OLIVE YOUNG` and `HEALTH & BEAUTY STORE`.
- **Why:** the article is about where Korean sunscreen can and cannot be sold. This is
  the channel where it *is* sold, normally, at home.

## 2. `korean-skincare-shop-rotterdam.jpg`

- **Source:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Haru_Haru_(Korean_skincare_%26_cosmetics),_Rotterdam-Centrum,_Rotterdam_(2020)_01.jpg
- **Author:** Donald Trung Quoc Don — attribution required, in the caption.
- **Licence:** **CC BY-SA 4.0**
- **Original:** 4864x2736. Delivered 1200px, 122 KB.
- **What it shows:** a Korean skincare and cosmetics shop, `HARU HARU`, on a street in
  Rotterdam, its window marked "Korean skincare & cosmetics".
- **Why this and not a US photo:** it is placed against the section explaining why
  Korean sunscreen appears on Amazon despite not clearing the FDA's OTC drug route.
  The answer is that it reaches foreign customers through importers and specialist
  retail rather than the mainstream drug-store channel, and this is a photograph of
  exactly that. **The caption states Rotterdam explicitly** — it is not presented as
  American, because the article's regulatory argument is US-specific and implying
  otherwise would be a country mismatch.

## Rejected: `LRP sunscreen bottle.jpg`

Commons lists a La Roche-Posay sunscreen bottle under CC BY-SA 4.0, which would have
illustrated the filter discussion. **The download returned an HTML error page rather
than an image** (4 KB, `<!DOCTYPE html>`), so it was never usable. Recorded here so
the next session does not spend time rediscovering that.

It was also the weaker choice on merit: La Roche-Posay is French, and putting a
European bottle in an article about the Korea–US gap would have muddied the point.

## Checks run

- **Cross-post uniqueness:** first use of both on this site. Note that `192` and `028`
  also cover Olive Young; neither uses this photograph.
- **Exact-subject rule:** the hero is the actual retail chain the article names; the
  second is an actual Korean-skincare retailer abroad, which is the mechanism the
  section describes.
- **Size:** 87 + 122 KB = **209 KB** for the post.
- **Captions:** written after viewing. Both name what is in frame and carry the
  required CC BY-SA attribution. Neither apologises for its image.

## Gap worth noting

There is **no photograph of a Korean sunscreen label** showing `SPF 50+ PA++++` and a
filter list. That single image would carry the PA-rating section better than any
prose, and it is the obvious thing to shoot or source next time this post is touched.
