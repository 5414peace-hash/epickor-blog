# Image Sources — Blog 374 (Hangul Day in Seoul)

Four Wikimedia Commons files. Two are official Korea.net / KOCIS press photographs of an actual
Hangul Day event, one is a public-domain scan of the Hunminjeongeum Haerye itself, and one is a
current photograph of the Gwanghwamun statue. No generic "Seoul mood" stock was used.

Per the lesson recorded on `372`, each frame was opened and inspected before selection rather than
trusted from its filename — the Commons descriptions in the Korea.net series are identical across
all eleven files, so the titles carry no information about what is actually in the frame.

- `sejong-statue-hunminjeongeum-projection.jpg` — hero/OG.
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Korea_HangulDay_06_(8329125200).jpg
  License: **CC BY-SA 2.0**, Jeon Han / Korea.net / Korean Culture and Information Service.
  Original 3976×2488 → 1500px wide, 105KB.
  Chosen because it carries the article's whole subject in one frame: the King Sejong statue, the
  Hunminjeongeum text projected at scale onto the Sejong Center behind it, and the 세종대왕 pedestal
  legible in Hangul. Carries a small `KOREA.net` watermark bottom-right — this is the source's own
  credit mark, not a defect, and it is accurately attributed in the caption.
  **Date honesty:** the Commons metadata dates this event to 2012-12-28, and it is the ceremony
  celebrating Hangul Day's *restoration as a public holiday* (한글날 공휴일 재지정 축하 기념행사),
  not an October 9 observance. The article says so explicitly in the body rather than letting the
  reader assume it is an autumn photograph.

- `sejong-statue-gwanghwamun-daylight.jpg` — the "go here instead" section.
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:020250905_Statue_of_King_Sejong_the_Great.jpg
  License: **CC BY-SA 4.0**, Marsilar. Original 1201×1668 → 1000px wide, 129KB.
  Current daylight view. The open book in the statue's lap and the 세종대왕 pedestal are both clearly
  readable, which is what the surrounding text points at.

- `hunminjeongeum-haerye-opening-page.jpg` — the 1443/1446 section.
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hunminjeongeum_Haerye_02.jpg
  License: **Public domain** (Government of Joseon, 1446). Original 1920×1280 → 1300px wide, 91KB.
  The actual opening page of the document the October 9 date is derived from. Selected over the
  other 13 available page scans because this one shows the 訓民正音 title, the famous opening line
  國之語音異乎中國, and the phrase 二十八字 — the "twenty-eight letters" the article contrasts with
  today's 24. The caption points the reader at that specific column.

- `hangul-day-congratulations-projection.jpg` — the date-history section.
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Korea_HangulDay_08_(8329127118).jpg
  License: **CC BY-SA 2.0**, Jeon Han / Korea.net / Korean Culture and Information Service.
  Original 2880×1696 → 1300px wide, 92KB.
  축하합니다 projected with "congratulations" transliterated into Hangul in a dozen languages —
  a direct visual demonstration of Hangul as a phonetic system, which is the point of that section.
  Same 2012 event as the hero; dated honestly in the caption for the same reason.

Cross-post uniqueness: all four are first use on EpicKor. Note that the site-wide audit script keys
off Pexels-style `photos/{id}` URL patterns, which Commons `File:` URLs do not contain — these were
checked by filename against `public/assets/images/` instead.

Post total: 417KB across four images, well inside the 1MB-per-post ceiling.
