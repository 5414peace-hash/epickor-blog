# Image Sources — Blog 373 (Doenjang vs Gochujang)

Three Wikimedia Commons files, each showing the exact subject the surrounding text names. Per the
lesson recorded on `372`, the accompaniments were checked, not just the main item — the hero had to
show soybeans *and* chillies *and* all three products, because the article's argument is that the
two pastes come from two different raw materials by two different processes.

- `jang-trio-soybeans-chilies.jpg` — hero/OG.
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Soy_Sauce_Ganjang,_Gochujang,_Doenjang.jpg
  License: **KOGL Type 1** (attribution, commercial use and derivatives permitted),
  **Korea Agro-Fisheries & Food Trade Corporation** — an official Korean public agency, which is
  the strongest possible provenance for "this is what Korean jang looks like."
  Original 664×709 → 1200px wide.
  Chosen because it carries the article's thesis visually: raw soybeans and the doenjang/ganjang
  pair on one side, dried chillies and gochujang on the other.

- `meju-dried-block.jpg` — the 장 가르기 section.
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dried_meju.jpg
  License: **CC BY 2.0**, Sous Chef. Native 634×423, **not upscaled**.
  A real dried meju brick. Commons has several meju files; this one shows the block whole and in a
  working kitchen rather than as a museum object.

- `jangdokdae-naganeupseong.jpg` — the fermentation/UNESCO section.
  Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jangdokdae_in_Naganeupseong_01.jpg
  License: **CC0**, Bernard Gagnon. Original 4265×2843 → 1400px wide (quality 72 to hold the file
  near 300KB; the original is 4.5MB).
  Naganeupseong Folk Village, a real jangdokdae with dozens of jars under persimmon trees — the
  physical place where the 60-day separation described in the text actually happens.
