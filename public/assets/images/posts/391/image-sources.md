# Image sources — Blog 391 (Korean heated mattress pads)

Both images are **manufacturer product photographs of the two products the article compares**.
Neither needs a caption to justify its presence, which is the standard this post was written
to after the 388 and 390 image corrections on 2026-08-16.

**The 0차 manufacturer step was run first this time, before the article was written** — the
reverse of the order used on 388 and 390, where Commons was searched first and the manufacturer
route was reached late or badly.

## 1. `navien-water-mat-boiler-unit.jpg` (hero, ogImage)

- **Source:** Kyungdong Navien official site, sleeping-mat business page
  `https://www.kdnavien.co.kr/ko/business/living/sleeping-mat`, asset
  `/images/business/img_visual_sleeping_mat_mo.webp`, retrieved 2026-08-16.
- **Original:** 750x600 WebP. Delivered 750x600 JPEG, **38 KB**.
- **What it shows, verified by opening it at full size:** a bed with a quilted heating mat on
  the mattress, and **a white boiler unit standing on the floor beside it** — `navien` wordmark
  on the top face, a large round control dial, a ribbed/finned side panel, and a hose running
  out of the back.
- **Why it is the hero.** The article's whole subject is the difference between an electric mat
  and a water mat, and the difference *is* that box. A reader who has never heard of a 온수매트
  understands the product from this frame alone: mat on the bed, boiler on the floor, hose
  between them.
- **Note on the alternative asset.** The same page also serves a desktop banner at 3840x1040.
  It was downloaded and compared; at that aspect ratio the boiler unit is small and the mat
  dominates. The mobile asset is a tighter, different shot, not a crop, and is the better
  image despite the lower pixel count.
- **Rights basis:** manufacturer product image used editorially to identify the product the
  article names, the house practice already used for the Nongshim and Samyang packshots, the
  hy Mobility cart, the Happycall pan (post 390) and the Beauty of Joseon tube (post 388). No
  sponsorship implied; the caption credits Kyungdong Navien.

## 2. `ilwol-deomaru-electric-carpet-mat.jpg`

- **Source:** Ilwol official mall `ilwoulshop.co.kr`, product **`더마루 플러스 카페트매트`**
  (Deomaru Plus Carpet Mat), `product_no=31`, retrieved 2026-08-16.
- **Original:** 500x500 (the mall's `big` size is the maximum). Delivered 500x500, **36 KB**.
- **What it shows:** four nested mats in ascending sizes, **printed to look like wood
  flooring**, each with a white connector socket on one edge and a spec sticker in the corner.
  Clean cutout on white.
- **Why here:** it sits against the ondol section and carries the point the text makes — that
  Korean heating goes *under* you on the floor rather than *over* you in bed. A Western reader
  has no equivalent product; showing one is faster than describing it.
- **Type verified before the caption was written.** The caption calls it an electric mat, so
  that was checked against two independent sources rather than inferred from the photograph:
  the mall's own product name (`더마루 플러스 카페트매트`) and Danawa's listing for the same
  product, which names it **`일월 더마루 플러스 카페트 전기매트`** at ₩91,636. Guessing the
  heating type from a single visible connector socket would have been exactly the failure this
  repo recorded on 2026-08-03 and again on 2026-08-16.

## Rejected

- **`Baekje Ondol site.jpg`** (public domain, 1600x1200) — a reconstructed Baekje-era ondol
  excavation showing stone-lined flue channels and a firebox pit. It was downloaded, viewed at
  880px, processed, and then **discarded before publication**. It is a genuine and relevant
  artefact, and the caption would have read "this is the ancestor of the modern water mat" —
  which is precisely the tell that the image is wrong. **If a caption has to explain why the
  photograph is relevant, the photograph is wrong.** The article is about a product currently
  on sale; a 1,500-year-old excavation is context, not subject.
- **`Ondol.JPG`** (CC BY-SA 3.0 de) — the firebox at Unhyeongung, Seoul. Same problem, and dim.
- **`Ondol Chimneys in the Amisan Garden of Gyeongbokgung`** (CC BY-SA 4.0, 4416x3312) — the
  most attractive of the ondol candidates and the least informative: it photographs the exhaust.
- **Ilwol lifestyle shots** (7 others downloaded and viewed on a contact sheet) — mats on beds
  in styled rooms. One, `11.jpg`, appears at thumbnail size to show circulation tubing looped
  across the mat. **Enlarged to 900px it is a printed decorative pattern of circles.** A caption
  claiming visible water tubing was drafted and then discarded on that check. Recording it here
  because the thumbnail was genuinely convincing.
- **Commons `Korean apartment kitchen` / `Korean floor heating`** — returns Hong Kong shop
  interiors, Japanese material, and unrelated Korean interior series. Nothing usable.

## Gap worth noting

There is no photograph anywhere of **a water mat opened up so the tubing is visible**. That
single cutaway would explain the category in one frame and nothing in the manufacturer,
Commons, or Flickr pools has it. A Korean-side photo of a mat with its cover unzipped would be
the highest-value image this post could gain.
