## Korea is verifiable in both frames

The storefront photo carries three independent markers: the **N Seoul Tower on Namsan** on the
ridge behind, Korean **1+1 / 2+1 promotional tags with ₩ prices** in the window, and a Korean
`CESCO` pest-control sticker on the door. It also happens to show the **chest freezer on the
pavement**, which is exactly where ice cups are sold — so it is doing evidential work, not
supplying atmosphere.

The bottle photo shows four Binggrae pot-shaped milks with Korean labels legible: 바나나맛우유,
딸기맛우유, 바나나맛우유 Light, 메론맛우유. The banana one is the pairing Korean writing names
first for an ice cup.

## There is no free-licensed photograph of an actual 얼음컵. That was checked.

Searched and found nothing usable:

- **Wikimedia Commons** — `얼음컵`, `ice cup convenience store korea`, `Korean convenience store
  drink`, `pouch drink korea` all return **zero photographs**; the hits are digitised books and
  PDFs from unrelated centuries.
- **Pexels** — nine queries across the object and the setting. The closest results are iced
  coffee already made (`32972507`, a sealed plastic cup) and generic ice cubes. None shows the
  Korean product: a sealed cup of ice with no drink in it.
- **Chain sites (the 0차 manufacturer route)** — `7-eleven.co.kr` returns a **2 KB shell**, dead
  the same way `ht.co.kr` is. `gs25.gsretail.com` is server-rendered but its product list is
  behind an AJAX call that returns the page shell, and `cu.bgfretail.com` is the same.

So the article does not claim to show one. Rather than caption a lookalike as the subject — the
2026-08-03 카드뉴스 defect — it shows the freezer the cups come out of and the bottle that goes
into them, and describes the cup in text.

## The filename lied again, and it is the same file family as before

`File:Binggrae Banana Flavored Milk 01 (15723531232).jpg` is **not banana milk**. It is a
corporate interview portrait of a Binggrae executive with 투게더 and 요맘때 ice-cream tubs on the
table behind him. This exact trap is already in `FACTS.md`, and post `167` cites it as the reason
Commons files are checked against the pixels rather than trusted by title.

`03.jpg`, used here, was downloaded and inspected before use and does show the product.
