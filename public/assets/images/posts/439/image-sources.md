**The 방앗간 photograph is the one that earns its place, and the caption was verified by zooming in.**
The article claims the mill sells pressed sesame oil, so the caption names what the window says. Before
writing it, the right-hand window column was cropped and enlarged: the hand-painted red text reads
**고추가루 / 미숫가루 / 참기름**, over blue lettering spelling 부창 떡방앗간. **A caption that reports
text in a photograph has to be read at full resolution first** — at thumbnail size that column is
illegible and could have been anything.

**A chart is the hero here on purpose.** The article's argument is a legal distinction between two
words on a label; no photograph states it, and mocking up a fake bottle label to illustrate it would
be fabricating a product image. The two real photographs then carry the two sections a chart cannot —
the mill, and the table where 기름장 appears.

**⚠️ Manufacturer packshots were attempted first and failed.** Per the 0차 rule: `ottogi.co.kr`
301-redirects to `otoki.com`, whose product listing lives on `otokimall.com` and renders client-side,
so nothing is retrievable without a browser. `beksul.com` does not resolve. Commons' `Sesame oil`
category is almost entirely Indian and Middle Eastern; the one Korean file,
`Korean sesame oil-Chamgireum-01.jpg`, is **375 × 500** — too small for a body image, and upscaling it
would be worse than not using it. **This is the case where the waterfall genuinely runs out and the
honest move is a chart plus generic-but-true photographs**, not a stretched thumbnail.

**Cross-post uniqueness:** Pexels IDs `3338502` and `8896216` were checked against the used-ID list
before download; `Changnyeong_bangatgan` appears in no other post's `image-sources.md`.
