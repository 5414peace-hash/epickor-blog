## Why these three, and why two of them are daylight

The article's load-bearing claim is structural: **the Gwangan Bridge is the launch platform, not the
backdrop**, which is why viewing angle decides everything in Busan and barely matters in Seoul.

- The hero proves that claim directly. You can see pyrotechnics running along the span and
  cascading off the deck into the water — a photo of fireworks merely *near* a bridge would not.
  The foreground wall of silhouetted heads carries the crowd-density point at the same time.
- The other two are **deliberately daylight**. A night shot of a viewpoint shows you pretty lights;
  a daylight shot shows you the geometry, which is the thing a reader choosing between Gwangalli
  and Igidae actually needs to understand. Together they make the viewpoint comparison table
  legible: bridge dead ahead from the sand, bridge broadside from the headland.

## On the 2008 date

The hero is from 2008 and the visible cameras date it. It is used anyway because it is **the exact
subject** — this festival, this bridge, this firing pattern — and Commons holds nothing more recent
for the Busan festival. Under our standard the exact subject beats a newer generic substitute, and
the caption carries the year rather than implying it is current. The bridge and the deck-mounted
firing pattern are unchanged.
