# Image sources — Blog 376 (Korean convenience store 1+1)

All three are real photographs taken inside or outside Korean convenience stores, on
Wikimedia Commons, under licences that permit commercial use. No generated or editorial
graphic is used.

**Why photographs rather than generated visuals.** The representative approved generating
images to unblock the post. Two of the three images here carry the article's actual subject —
a `2+1` promotion strip and a wall of Korean shelf-edge price tags — and that is text and
brand signage, which is exactly what image generation renders as unreadable pseudo-Hangul and
invented logos. Sourcing real Korean stores produced a stronger result than generating would
have, so no generation was needed. Recorded here per the Blog Reference Image Standard.

## Processing

- Downloaded via the Commons thumbnail API, resized to 1100–1500px wide, mozjpeg q76.
- **262–288KB each, 832KB for the post.** Above the usual 150–250KB target and deliberately
  so: two of the three are detail-critical — if the shelf tags smear, the image stops making
  its point. Under the 400KB per-image gate and the 1MB per-post budget.
- `gs25-storefront-seoul-atm.jpg` is **cropped 9% off the bottom** to remove the uploader's
  `니나노데이` watermark. The removed strip is road surface only.

## Uniqueness

Checked against existing posts before selection. `GS25 alcohol selection seoul.jpg` appears in
`posts/343/image-sources.md` marked **"Not used"** — 343 rejected it because its shelves hold
alcohol rather than snacks, which was the wrong fit for a snack-scarcity article. For this post
the subject *is* the shelf-edge tag system, so the alcohol aisle is the better frame, not a
compromise: those tags are the largest and densest in the store. The other two files are not
referenced anywhere in the repo.
