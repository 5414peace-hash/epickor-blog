In 1989 a letter destroyed Korea's oldest noodle company. It was not true. 🇰🇷

On 3 November 1989, prosecutors received an anonymous letter claiming Samyang Foods fried its instant noodles in industrial-grade beef tallow. Executives were arrested. The press ran with it.

Within about three months the Dobong-dong plant had shut and Samyang's market share had fallen from roughly 60% to 15% — three quarters of the business, gone, over an allegation nobody had proven. Koreans still call it 우지 파동, the beef tallow crisis.

Investigators, and then the courts, found the tallow was edible all along. The Supreme Court acquitted the company on 26 August 1997, nearly eight years after the accusation. By then it did not matter.

Then, on 3 November 2025 — the same calendar date, thirty-six years later — Samyang launched 삼양1963, a rebuild of Korea's first instant noodle from 1963. And it is fried in beef tallow. The exact ingredient.

It is printed on the front of the bag: 우지 6.87%, on a 131 g packet, in the same size as the weight. Not buried in a panel on the back. About five months in, it was selling roughly seven million packets a month. 🍜
