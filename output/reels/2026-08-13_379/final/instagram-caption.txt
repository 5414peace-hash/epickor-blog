The Gwangan Bridge isn't the backdrop. It's the launcher. 🎆

Korea has two fireworks nights that matter, and they are not the same event twice. Seoul's is a river show. Busan's is a bridge show — pyrotechnics run along the 7.4km span of the Gwangan Bridge (광안대교) and cascade off the deck into the water, the effect Korean coverage calls the Niagara. That single structural fact decides where you should stand.

The 21st Busan Fireworks Festival is Saturday 7 November 2026, at Gwangalli Beach.

Now the timing problem nobody tells you about.

The show is in November. The thing you have to act on is in August. For the 2025 festival there were roughly 14,000 paid seats — R ₩100,000 with a table, S ₩70,000 chair only — laid out on the sand facing the bridge straight on. The early-bird block opened 22 August and sold out in about one minute.

For 2026, none of that has been published yet. The official site lists the date and venue and nothing else. If it follows last year's rhythm, that window falls in late August — and one minute is not enough time to discover the announcement and then decide.

Here's the part that should relax you. Fourteen thousand seats against a beach-wide crowd means the overwhelming majority of people at this festival watch for free, from sand facing exactly the same bridge. The paid seat buys you a guaranteed spot and a chair. Not a better angle. 🌊
