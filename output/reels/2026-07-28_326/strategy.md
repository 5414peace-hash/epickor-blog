## Creative Brief

- Target viewer: someone who has seen a Buldak "fire noodle challenge" video and assumes Korean spicy = extreme/dangerous.
- Misconception being corrected: viral packaging and challenge-video culture wildly overstate the actual heat.
- Hook archetype: **myth-bust**.
- First-frame promise (3-5 words): **"Not as spicy."**
- Tension/open loop: if the "extreme" noodle isn't that hot, what actually is?
- Payoff: cheongyang pepper (5x a jalapeno) is the real heat Koreans reach for — and it's not the sauce or noodle brand, it's a raw pepper sliced into everyday dishes.
- Why keep watching: real numbers stacked against a real reference point (jalapeno) the viewer already knows.
- Save/share reason: practical ordering knowledge before a Korea trip; corrects a widely-shared myth (comment-bait).
- Voice lane: **female_culture_travel** (food/travel, sharper warning angle fits the "you've been scared of the wrong thing" tone).
- Thumbnail directions:
  - Mystery: "The Spicy Lie"
  - Mistake: "You're Scared Of The Wrong Noodle"
  - Decision: "Which One's Actually Hot?"
  - **Selected: Mistake** — "You're Scared Of The Wrong Noodle" pairs directly with the myth-bust hook and creates instant stakes (viewer feels called out).
- Spoken opening variants:
  1. "Okay, this noodle went viral for being impossible to finish. It's really not that spicy."
  2. "Everyone's scared of this noodle. It's barely hotter than a jalapeno."
  3. "You've watched people cry over this noodle. Here's what it's actually rated."
  - **Selected: #2** — shortest, hits the contradiction in the first clause, pairs cleanly with the "Mistake" thumbnail.
- Motion card: **zero** (Reels 2.2 default — payoff is a visual reveal: the Scoville numbers appearing on screen as text over real footage, not a card).
- Funnel expectation: profile visit + EpicKor.com blog/326 for the full Scoville comparison table.
