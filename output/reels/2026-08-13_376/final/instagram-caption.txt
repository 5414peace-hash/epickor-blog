You can just take the second one. Nobody scans anything. 🏪

Half the fridge in a Korean convenience store is wearing a sticker — 1+1 (원 플러스 원) or 2+1. It means exactly what it looks like: buy one, walk out with two. No membership card, no coupon, no app, no minimum spend. The shelf-edge tag is the whole mechanism, and if the tag says it, you pick the second one up yourself and carry both to the counter.

Here's the part the guides get wrong.

Every English-language Korea guide tells you the same trick: don't carry the free one around all afternoon — leave it, save it in the chain's app, and collect it at another branch later. That is a real feature — GS25 calls it 나만의 냉장고, CU runs it inside 포켓CU.

And almost no visitor can use it. Signing up runs through Korean identity verification, which binds to a Korean phone line, bank certificate or card in your own name. Logging in with Apple or Google doesn't route around it — the gate is inside the app, not the login.

So take the second one now. 🧊
