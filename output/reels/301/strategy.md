# Reel 301 Strategy - Seoul Social Decoder

## Source Gate

- Source: Blog 301, `Ajumma Meaning: Is It Rude? Ahjumma, Ajumeoni, and Imo Explained`
- Public URL: `https://www.epickor.com/blog/301`
- Status: representative-final-reviewed, published, deployed, and publicly verified before Reels production, as recorded in `HANDOFF.md`.
- Fact boundary: the National Institute of Korean Language identifies `ajumma` as a lowered/impolite form used for an adult woman. The Reel must explain contextual risk without implying that the word is profanity or always hostile.

## Target Viewer

An English-speaking Korea traveler or K-drama viewer who learned `ajumma` from subtitles, assumes it is a neutral equivalent of `ma'am`, or copies `imo` from restaurant scenes without understanding relationship and setting.

## Hook System

Hook archetype: `mistake` plus `social rule`.

Thumbnail options:

1. Mystery: `WHY THIS WORD STINGS`
2. Mistake: `DON'T SAY AJUMMA`
3. Decision: `SAY THIS INSTEAD`

Spoken opening options:

1. `Ajumma can look like ma'am in subtitles and still offend the person in front of you.`
2. `If you call a stranger ajumma in Korea, you may be guessing her age out loud.`
3. `The safest Korean word for a woman you don't know is usually no title at all.`

Selected hook unit:

- Thumbnail: `DON'T SAY AJUMMA`
- Narration: option 1.
- First-frame visual requirement: real Seoul market or service context with an adult woman shown as a person at work, not as an age stereotype; the Hangul `아줌마` must be readable and correctly typeset.
- Reason: the pair creates immediate social stakes, then the second line softens the command by clarifying that context—not vocabulary alone—is the issue.

## Story And Payoff

- Misconception: `ajumma` is a friendly universal `ma'am` or `auntie`.
- Concrete surprise: the word can mark age and create familiarity that does not exist.
- Twist: it is not always negative when women self-identify or friends use it playfully; outside labeling is the risk.
- Practical payoff: use `jeogiyo` to get attention, an accurate role title such as `sajangnim` when known, and `imo` only when a casual restaurant context clearly supports it.
- Human line: `The translation is easy. The social distance is not.`
- Save/share reason: save the replacement phrase before eating or shopping in Korea; send it to the friend who learns Korean from subtitles.
- Funnel expectation: shares/comments first, profile and full language-guide visits second.

## Design System

- Name: `Seoul Social Decoder`.
- Palette: cobalt `#1557ff`, signal red `#ff3b30`, ivory `#f7f2e8`, ink `#111318`, safety green `#25b56a` used once for the neutral action.
- Visual vocabulary: live-caption brackets, Hangul word morphs, conversational distance rings, Seoul crosswalk ticks, and one human-centered decision board.
- Typography: wide grotesk for English, system Korean sans for Hangul, with no faux brush or tourist-poster styling.
- Rhythm: real human scene first, one clean semantic transformation per scene, no corporate-dashboard grid and no repeated cards.
- Sensitivity guardrail: no perm/visor/pushing stereotype, no estimating a woman's age from appearance, no comic reaction shot, and no framing that turns market labor into a caricature.

## Voice

- Lane: `female_culture_travel`.
- Delivery: confident, warm, and lightly cautionary; a culturally fluent friend correcting a subtitle, not scolding a learner.
- Pace: natural American English at 1.0x, with crisp pronunciation of `ajumma`, `ajumeoni`, `imo`, and `jeogiyo`.
- Audition: 8-12 seconds using the opening and `The translation is easy...` line before full TTS.

## Single Motion Card

- Count: exactly one.
- Placement: approximately 22-30 seconds.
- Role: a `social distance decoder` over real moving Seoul restaurant/market footage with a controlled blue veil and semi-transparent ivory panel.
- Synchronized rows:
  1. `STRANGER → JEOGIYO`
  2. `KNOWN OWNER → SAJANGNIM`
  3. `CASUAL RESTAURANT → IMO, ONLY IF THE ROOM DOES`
- Payoff line: `When you don't know the relationship, don't guess it.`
- Caption zone: dedicated lower band at least 410px above the bottom edge; rows end above that band.
