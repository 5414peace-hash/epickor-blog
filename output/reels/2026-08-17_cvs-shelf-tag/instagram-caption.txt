The price on a Korean convenience-store shelf tag is not the price. 🏪

Koreans do not read the tag. They read the little sticker next to it. Here is what four things actually cost, as of August 2026.

진라면 매운맛 컵 (Jin Ramen Spicy Cup) lists at ₩1,100. On a 3+1 or 2+1 it works out to about ₩825 — checked across emart24, CU and 7-Eleven on 3 August 2026. The mild and slightly-spicy cups list at the same price, so pick by heat, not by budget.

너구리 큰사발 (Neoguri Large Cup) lists at ₩1,900 at CU and lands near ₩1,267 on a 2+1. That is a ₩633 gap on one cup. And the stiff dark square you find inside is kelp — it goes in with the noodles, it is not a seasoning packet.

밀키스 250ml (Milkis) lists at ₩1,500 at GS25, ₩1,000 on a 2+1. It has been on sale since 1989, and Lotte Chilsung has publicly talked about the brand reaching ₩80 billion a year.

Then the one that is not a promotion at all.

포카칩 66g (Pocachip) is ₩1,700 at a convenience store. The same bag works out to roughly ₩1,100 in a supermarket multipack. No sticker, no deal — just a different shelf. For Korea's No.1 potato chip, the convenience store is simply the expensive way to buy it. 🥔

So there are two separate things happening. Some prices drop because a promotion is running. Others are only high because of where you are standing.
