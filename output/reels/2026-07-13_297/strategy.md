## Thumbnail and Opening Tests

### Mystery

- Thumbnail: `FIVE KINDS OF SWEET`
- Spoken opening: `Korean dessert makes more sense when you stop sorting it by shape.`

### Mistake — selected

- Thumbnail: `DON'T CALL IT MOCHI`
- Spoken opening: `If you call every Korean rice cake mochi, you miss most of the dessert table.`

### Decision

- Thumbnail: `PICK YOUR TEXTURE`
- Spoken opening: `Chewy, crisp, sticky, or drinkable—here's your Korean dessert match.`

Selected pair: Mistake. It creates a clear correction without insulting the viewer and leads naturally into the texture chooser.

## Story Arc

1. Correct the `all mochi` shortcut with direct Korean dessert proof.
2. Establish that `tteok` is a broad family, not one texture or shape.
3. Contrast chewy injeolmi with dense, syrup-soaked yakgwa.
4. Add crisp hangwa and the two drink paths: sikhye versus sujeonggwa.
5. Pay off with one center-filled `PICK BY TEXTURE` motion card around 60–75%.
6. Finish with a practical first tasting and a save/website CTA.

## One Motion Card

The only motion card uses four occupied choice rows:

- `CHEWY → TTEOK / INJEOLMI`
- `RICH + STICKY → YAKGWA`
- `CRISP → YUGWA / GANGJEONG`
- `DRINK → SIKHYE / SUJEONGGWA`

The red-and-cream split should feel like a designed editorial label system, not a corporate comparison slide. Real dessert imagery remains visible behind or beside the card. Narration captions use a separate lower safe band.

## Visual Guardrails

- Korea-specific dessert proof only; reject Japanese mochi, generic doughnuts, foreign tea ceremonies, or unrelated Asian sweets.
- Official VISITKOREA/Korea.net assets from Blog 297 are authoritative still proof and may be animated purposefully.
- Source real vertical Korean dessert/cafe/tea motion where truthful; use no generated visuals by default.
- No source family repeats, no adjacent near-duplicate dessert plates, and no thumbnail source reused later.
- Keep all thumbnail text and dessert focal points inside the conservative profile-grid safe area.

## Production Gate

Proceed only if the selected scene set can achieve a Visual Variety Score of at least 92/100 with no individual scene below 90, while keeping the first-frame hook readable and the motion-card center fully occupied.
