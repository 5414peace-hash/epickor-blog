## The brief

- **Hook archetype**: reversal of a number. Not a claim about a category — a measured share that
  moved, with the year attached. The winning reels in the 46-reel retention pull all opened on a
  judgment; the losers opened on a thesis.
- **First-frame promise**: a bright K-beauty aisle under a chyron reading `TWO PERCENT · 2022`.
  The viewer is told immediately that a number is about to be wrong.
- **Viewer misconception**: that Olive Young has always been a tourist destination. It has not —
  in 2022 foreign shoppers were a rounding error in its offline sales.
- **Payoff**: the physical consequence of the number. A checkout hall where every counter carries
  a lit strip telling you to bring the actual passport. It is a visual reveal — the viewer reads
  the sign themselves — not an information card.
- **Save/share reason**: the refund rules are actionable and easy to get wrong. Anyone planning a
  Korea trip saves this for the passport line alone.
- **Thumbnail**: the aisle frame with `TWO PERCENT / 2022`. The three directions considered were
  *Mystery* (the checkout hall with no context), *Mistake* (the passport sign) and *Decision*
  (the aisle plus the falsified number). Decision won: it is the only one that states stakes in
  the first frame without spending the payoff.

## The humour is structural, not written

The anchor never jokes. The joke is the descent: a trillion-won institutional figure, delivered
in the same flat register, lands on "bring the actual passport — a photo does not work." Nothing
in the script is funny by itself. Playing a store's checkout signage as national economic news is.

## What was cut, and why it matters

The post reports the 센트럴 명동 타운 branch at roughly 95% foreign sales — the single most
quotable figure in it. **It is not in the reel.** The only usable photograph is titled
"Olive Young Myeongdong" and names no branch, and there are nine branches in Myeongdong. A
sentence about *that* branch over *this* frame is a claim the picture cannot support. The reel
says "the checkout hall in Myeongdong" instead, which is exactly what the frame shows.

## Footage gate — run before any of this was written

Two passes, 1,020 candidates, 8 queries. **No Korean cosmetics-store interior exists in the pool.**

The instructive failure: four clips whose Pexels slug says `myeongdong`. Three of them show the
Bank of Korea's stone head office and one shows an empty paved plaza. Reading the slug rather
than the frame would have put a colonial-era bank building under a line about a beauty chain —
the same class of error as the European flea markets that nearly went into the Dongmyo reel.

**Selected**: `31801692` (3840x2160, 23.976fps) — a neon Seoul street, dense crowd walking toward
camera, shopping bags in frame. Carries the one generic sentence.
**Sourced and not used**: `32214385` (busy Jongno alley from above). Nothing needed it, and the
rule is that screen-speech agreement beats a video-share target.

**Video share is 175 of 736 programme frames, 24%.** That is below the 50% aim and it is
structural rather than lazy: every other sentence names Olive Young, and no video of Olive Young
is licensable. Filling to 50% would mean putting frames under sentences they do not match, which
is the exact reason the 2026-08-04 batch was rejected.

## Cadence

6 cuts + 8 beats + 1 figure = 15 discrete state changes across 24.5s of programme, about
1.6s per event. Same as the pilot. The Reels 2.3 aim is ≤1s and neither reel meets it; the
ticker and the lower-third wipes carry the gap. Worth revisiting if these two underperform.
