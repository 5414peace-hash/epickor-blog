In Korea a refrigerator drives down the road, and the law says it needs a driving licence. 🇰🇷

**1971** — 야쿠르트 arrives in Korea as a joint venture with Japan's Yakult Honsha, in a 65 ml bottle. The waisted polystyrene shape is not Korean and not new: it is a 1968 design by Isamu Kenmochi. Fifty-five years later it is still the same bottle.

**It is not yogurt** — and the difference is physical, not marketing. Korean fermented milk splits into two legal categories on a single production step. Homogenise the culture after fermentation and the curd breaks down into a liquid you drink: 액상발효유. Skip that step and it stays set, and you need a spoon: 호상발효유, 떠먹는 요구르트. Yakult is the first one. Same family, one step apart, a different physical object. 🥛

**March 2019** — at hy's 50th-anniversary event the 야쿠르트 아줌마 is officially renamed 프레시 매니저, Fresh Manager. Most Koreans still say the old name in conversation.

**April 2026** — about 11,000 of them nationwide, down from a peak of around 13,000 in 2021. Roughly 5,500 cover Seoul, Gyeonggi and Gangwon. Ninety are on Jeju. One industry forecast puts the point where delivery robots undercut people at 2028–2030.

**And the cart** — 코코 COCO, "Cold & Cool": a 220-litre electric refrigerator that does 4 or 8 km/h and runs ten hours-plus on a charge. Under Korea's 도로교통법, the Road Traffic Act, it is classified as a 원동기장치자전거 — the sub-50cc moped class. So a driving licence is mandatory, a helmet is legally required, and it may not use the pavement. A 4 km/h refrigerator is legally obliged to share a lane with cars doing 50. 🛵

Korean coverage says plainly that this does not work, and many Fresh Managers use the pavement anyway. It is a recognised regulatory grey zone, not individual rule-breaking. So if you see one on the pavement and one in the road on the same morning, you have not misread anything.

**And two of them are on 울릉도 Ulleungdo** — a volcanic island in the East Sea, several hours by ferry. Two people run that route. This network is not a Seoul marketing flourish; it reaches places that barely have a bus timetable. 🚢
