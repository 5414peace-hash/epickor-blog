## Why this topic gets the Spec Sheet frame

The load-bearing asset is `condition-sticks-human-trial-badge` — seven Condition sticks laid side by
side on white, from HK inno.N's own product page. **It is already a spec sheet.** Seven columns, each
printing a flavour, a weight, a kcal figure, `HANGOVER TECHNOLOGY`, and the badge
`숙취개선효과 인체적용시험완료`. The frame does not have to impose a structure on the picture; it has to
finish the one that is there.

That is also why this is the one topic in the batch where static pack shots are a *premise* rather
than a deficiency. A reel about a regulation printed on a package needs the package held still and
labelled, not motion.

### The badge cannot be read, and the frame does not pretend otherwise

Measured: in the 1106×1106 original from the manufacturer (fetched 2026-08-13, 1.25× larger than the
copy already in the post), one stick is ~140px wide and the badge ~106px. At 1080×1920 a crop tight
enough to make 8pt Hangul legible would be a 6× upscale.

So the frame **annotates instead of magnifying**: a leader line points at the badge and a callout
prints what it certifies in English. That is what a spec sheet is for. Asking a viewer to read Korean
small print would fail either way; telling them what it says is both honest and clearer.

## Hook archetype

**Named mistake.** The reel states within the first eight seconds that the viewer's instinct about
when to take these is wrong, then spends the rest earning a second, larger correction.

## First-frame promise

The seven-stick rack on white with the spec-sheet frame drawing its column rules, headline
`TAKEN BEFORE. / NOT AFTER.` The promise: this shelf has a rule you do not know.

## Viewer misconception

1. *"Hangover drinks are for the morning after."* Wrong for most of this shelf — Condition and
   Yeomyeong 808 are taken before drinking, and a Korean colleague will hand you one in the lift on
   the way to dinner.
2. *"It's an unregulated supplement, like anywhere else."* Also wrong, since 1 January 2025.

## Payoff

From 1 January 2025 the MFDS requires **인체적용시험** — a human trial — or a qualitative review of human
trial results, before a food may advertise 숙취해소, 술 깨는, or 음주 전후. Korean media reported **28
products reviewed, 25 cleared, 3 barred from the claim from 2026**. The reel lands that number on the
badge that carries it.

Stated honestly in the post and carried into the reel: this is **a floor on advertising honesty, not a
medical endorsement.** The reel must not imply the products are proven cures.

## Save/share reason

A viewer at a Korean convenience store counter can act on it immediately: take it before dinner, and
read the badge as "this claim has evidence behind it", not as "this works".

## Motion card

**Zero.** The spec-sheet blocks are the frame, not an information card, and the payoff is a reveal.
