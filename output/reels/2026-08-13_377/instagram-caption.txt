Koreans take these on the way INTO the night. Not the morning after. 🍶

That little shelf beside the till in every Korean convenience store is 숙취해소제 (sukchwi-haeso-je) — hangover relief. Korea buys about ₩350 billion of it a year. Most of what's on that shelf is meant to be taken before you drink, or with the first one: 컨디션 (Condition) and 여명808 both work that way, and a Korean colleague will hand you one in the lift on the way to dinner. Save it for the morning and you've used it backwards.

Condition launched in 1992, has been the number one seller for 33 straight years, and still holds roughly 44% of the market. ₩3,600–3,900 a stick as of August 2026.

Now the part that hasn't been written in English.

From 1 January 2025, anything sold in Korea that advertises itself with hangover language has to hold evidence for the claim — 인체적용시험, a human trial, or a qualitative review of human trial results. Without it, a product may no longer print 숙취해소, 술 깨는, or 음주 전후.

Korean media reported that of 28 products the ministry reviewed, 25 cleared the bar. Three could not produce evidence judged objective and valid, and are barred from the claim from 2026.

Read that as a floor on advertising honesty, not as a medical endorsement. A trial showing a measurable effect is a long way from a cure. 🧪
