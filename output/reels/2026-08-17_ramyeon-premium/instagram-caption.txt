A packet of Korean ramyun used to be ₩1,000. 🍜

Then two companies broke that line within nine months of each other, and the shelf split in two.

신라면 (Shin Ramyun) has been the reference price since 1986 — around ₩1,000, and the number the whole aisle is measured against.

신라면 골드 (Shin Ramyun Gold) arrived 2 January 2026 at ₩1,500, for Shin Ramyun's fortieth year. Half again the standard packet. A premium Shin had already been sold overseas before it came home, which Korean shoppers noticed.

Then there is the one with the story.

삼양1963 launched 3 November 2025 at ₩1,900 — roughly double an ordinary packet. It is a modern rebuild of Samyang Ramen, Korea's first instant noodle, from 1963.

The date was the point.

On 3 November 1989, prosecutors received an anonymous letter claiming Samyang fried its noodles in industrial-grade beef tallow. The accusation was false. The Supreme Court said so in 1997 — eight years later, by which time Samyang's market share had fallen from about 60% to 15%. Koreans call it the 우지 파동, the beef tallow crisis.

삼양1963 relaunched on the same calendar date, exactly thirty-six years later. And it is fried in beef tallow.

It is printed on the front of the bag: 우지 6.87%. The ingredient that nearly destroyed the company, stated as a number on the front of the pack, in the same size as the weight. 🐂

About five months in, it was selling roughly seven million packets a month.
