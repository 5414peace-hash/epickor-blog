## The one surprise this Reel proves

The stalls are not scattered around Seoul at random. They are parked on top of subway
exits on purpose, which is why the smell reaches you before you have even finished
climbing the stairs. Once you see that, you cannot unsee it.

## Narration — natural American English, mid-twenties, conversational

> Written to be *spoken*, not read. Contractions throughout, sentences that run into each
> other, no lecture cadence. Nothing here is clipped into staccato fragments.

Okay so — that smell. About ten seconds after you come up the stairs in Seoul it hits you,
and you're gonna stop walking.

It's hotteok. Brown sugar melting inside dough, pressed flat on a griddle right there on
the sidewalk, and it's like a dollar fifty.

And here's the part nobody tells you — these stalls aren't random. They're parked right
where you come out of the station. That's the entire business model.

Fish-shaped bread with sweet red bean inside. Steamed dumplings the size of your fist.
Fried stuff on sticks that you dip yourself. A pot of broth that's been going since morning.

None of it is a meal. All of it is about two bucks.

And you'll eat it standing up, right there, before you've even figured out which exit you needed.
