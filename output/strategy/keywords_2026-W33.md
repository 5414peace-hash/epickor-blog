Lane 1 (음식-구체). 31 seeds tested, 5 survive. Method follows the four changes W32c asked for:
duplicate audit first, coverage gate early, image gate inside the shortlist rather than after it.

**Order actually used:** body-level dedup (cheapest) → Two-Curl demand → query-shape read → coverage
gate (WebSearch, most expensive) → image gate.

### 1 — 썬칩 (Sun Chips) · the same name is a different chip

**The hook is a name collision, which is the pattern that just worked on 365 (Chilsung Cider).**
Orion Frito-Lay was formed in **April 1993** under a PepsiCo licence, and when that contract lapsed
in the 2000s the Korean product **had to be renamed 태양의 맛 썬**. The Korean chip is nacho-hard with
oats and a wavy cut; the flavours do not exist in the US — 갈릭바게트, 핫앤스파이시, 타코.

- **Query shape:** decision. `korean sun chips garlic baguette`, `korean sun chip flavors` — a
  reader picking a bag off a shelf.
- **Coverage:** namu EN, Grokipedia, a Creatrip company-history roundup, and English Wikipedia's
  page on the *American* Sun Chips. No dedicated English article on the Korean one.
- **To verify before writing:** whether 썬칩 or 태양의 맛 썬 is the current Korean shelf name, since
  export listings still say "Orion Sun Chips". The answer is the article's spine either way.

### 2 — 칸초 (Kancho) · there are words printed on every biscuit

Lotte prints names and messages on the biscuits themselves. The 2026 edition carries **500 Korean
names** and the search behaviour is people trying to find theirs — Naver builds `칸초 내이름 찾기`,
`칸초 이름 종류`, `칸초 이름 이벤트`. English autocomplete carries the same intent:
`kancho snack name list`, `kancho korean snack with names`.

- **Query shape:** action. The reader is looking for a specific name on a specific biscuit.
- **News hook without being news-dependent:** Jungkook bought boxes on a Weverse live hunting for
  "Ian". Sportskeeda covered *that*; nobody has written the product explainer.
- **Ephemerality check:** the 500-name run is a campaign, but printed messages on Kancho are decades
  old. Write the evergreen version and date-stamp the campaign.

### 3 — 바나나킥 (Banana Kick) · not banana, and Korean babies eat it

Nongshim, **1978**. It is a corn puff, not a banana chip, and it dissolves in the mouth — which is
why Naver's second branch is **`바나나킥 아기`**: it is a standard first snack for Korean toddlers.
That fact has no English page at all.

- **Query shape:** purchase. `banana kick snack near me`, `banana kick snack korea`, `ingredients`.
- **Coverage:** a Nongshim press release on GlobeNewswire, its Yahoo Finance syndication, and K-pop
  blogs on Jennie naming it on The Jennifer Hudson Show (2025-03-10). All PR or celebrity news.
- **Image gate — strongest of the five.** Commons holds three CC BY-SA 4.0 files up to **3000×2000**,
  and `nongshimusa.com` is the verified pack-shot source (FACTS, 2026-08-03).

### 4 — 뿌셔뿌셔 (Ppushu Ppushu) · the Korean flavours are not the export flavours

Ottogi. Crush the block in the bag, add the sachet, shake, eat dry. The English-speaking internet
knows that much — Amazon's own product titles say "Crush to Eat". **What it does not know is the
Korean shelf**: 제주마농, 구운양파, 떡볶이맛, 마요땅, 열뿌셔뿌셔. Export SKUs are BBQ, bulgogi and
grilled chicken.

- **Query shape:** decision + purchase. `ppushu ppushu flavors`, `ppushu ppushu hmart`.
- **Coverage — the weakest of the five.** theramenrater reviewed the bulgogi bag (2026-04) and
  SnackFever has a magazine post. Both are niche, but the basic instruction is genuinely taken.
  **This one wins on the flavour gap or not at all.**
- `korean crushed noodle snack` returns **0** English branches — the descriptive phrase is empty.

### 5 — 고래밥 (Goraebap) · nine sea animals, and a game printed on the box

Orion, **1984**. Nine different sea-creature shapes, six baked dough layers, and bingo and trivia
printed on the flip-top box. English autocomplete is small but entirely on-topic —
`goraebap flavors`, `goraebap ingredients`, `goraebap korean snack`.

- **Coverage — the cleanest result in this cycle.** Amazon, Weee, Feel in Seoul, NikanKitchen, one
  Flickr photo. **No editorial page in English exists.** (English Wikipedia's nearest entry is Turtle
  Chips, a different Orion product.)
- **Risk:** demand is the thinnest of the five (8 branches). This is a coverage-gap play, not a
  volume play — the same trade 꼬깔콘 lost on last cycle, but 고래밥's branches are all on-topic
  where 꼬깔콘's were 2 and polluted.

## What this cycle learned

1. **Romanisation pollution killed six seeds outright.** It is worth testing the romanisation *first*,
   before Naver, because a surname collision ends the candidate no matter how good the product is.
2. **The K-pop mention is a coverage risk, not a bonus.** 바나나킥 and 칸초 both have one, and in both
   cases the celebrity moment is already covered while the product is not. Write the product.
3. **Recipe blogs are the single biggest coverage killer in this lane** — three of eleven rejections.
   Any seed a home cook could make is probably taken; packaged products they cannot make are not.
