## Why the System Works
The Korean model is built on three layers:

1. technology adoption (bidet functions, privacy sounds),
2. policy modernization (flush-paper norm),
3. maintenance staffing and accountability.

This system-level approach matters more than any single gadget.

## The No-Trash-Can Transition
Many facilities now encourage flushing provided toilet paper. This reduced odor and improved hygiene outcomes compared with old bin-heavy practice.

## Free Access Advantage
Unlike many cities where paid toilets are common, Korea's public network is widely free, especially in transport hubs and parks.

## Traveler Etiquette Checklist
- leave stall clean,
- avoid long occupancy in busy nodes,
- respect quiet/public order norms,
- carry tissues only for rare edge cases.

## FAQ
### Where should I go in a bathroom emergency?
Nearest subway station is usually the most reliable first option.

### Is toilet paper always available?
Most of the time yes, with occasional exceptions in very old facilities.

### Are bidets common outside hotels?
Yes, especially in modern buildings and transit nodes.

### Do I need to pay for public toilets in Seoul?
Usually no.

## Final Takeaway
Korea's restroom experience feels premium because it is managed as public infrastructure quality, not an afterthought. For travelers, this dramatically reduces daily stress.
