**Seoul tech shopping** guides almost all begin the same way: go to Yongsan Electronics Market. That advice is now between out of date and actively wrong, and this article used to give it too.

Here is the state of things in August 2026. **용산전자상가** still exists. But the buildings have been coming down since June 2025, the trade that is left is largely business-to-business — parts, assembly, repair, used stock — and the retail browsing experience that made it famous has moved online. Meanwhile the reason young Seoul actually goes to Yongsan now is a street of restaurants and cafés on the other side of the station.

So this guide does two things: tells you what the electronics market is today and what it is still good for, and tells you where to buy tech in Seoul if that is what you came for.

## What Is Left of 용산전자상가

Construction on the replacement district is scheduled to begin in **2027** and finish around **2030**. Korean sources note something small and telling: once it is done, the **용산전자상가 intersection will be renamed**, because it will no longer be an electronics market.

Some shops in the **basement levels of 나진상가** are still trading and have said they will stay until their leases expire. Console-game specialists that used to be a draw have scattered — some PlayStation-authorised dealers moved toward **원효로2가**.

## Why It Emptied Out

Two forces, and the second one is not obvious.

**The first is the ordinary one.** Price comparison went online. Once a shopper could check every price in Korea from a phone, the whole basis of a haggling market — that the seller knew the price and you did not — was gone. Korean commentary is blunt that by 2025 there was little reason to go to Yongsan for straightforward shopping.

**The second is more recent.** The **2025 semiconductor shortage** hit small retailers unusually hard. Large technology firms bought up supply for AI data-centre build-outs, and the allocation ran through channels that bypass small shops entirely. Shelves in the market thinned out. A major retailer, **컴마왕**, gave up its premises. A market cannot function as a comparison venue when the stock is not there to compare.

That combination is why the honest description of Yongsan today is not "dying" and not "gone." It is **a working trade district that stopped being a shopping destination.**

## What It Is Still Genuinely Good For

Do not write it off. Go for the right reasons.

**PC parts and custom builds.** 선인상가 is still where Koreans go to have a machine assembled or to buy a specific component today rather than wait for delivery. If you need a part now, this is the fastest place in Seoul.

**Repair and A/S.** Screen replacements, board-level repair, connector work, obscure adapters, legacy cables. This is the market's most durable function and the one online cannot replace.

**Used and legacy stock.** Older components, discontinued cables, things that no longer have a retail channel.

**What it is not good for:** buying a new phone, laptop, tablet or camera as a visitor. You will not beat a brand store on price, and you will lose on warranty, language support and returns.

## The Yongsan People Actually Go To Now

This is the part no English tech guide mentions, and it is the more useful half of the answer.

Cross to the other side of the station and you are in **용리단길** — Yongridan-gil, the alleys of **한강로2가** between **신용산역** and **삼각지역**. It is a five-to-ten-minute walk from **용산역 Exit 1** or **신용산역 Exit 2**.

It was an old low-rise residential and light-industrial pocket. Then **아모레퍼시픽** put its headquarters there, the daytime population changed, and the alleys filled with small, heavily designed restaurants and cafés — places built around a specific reference, whether Hong Kong, San Francisco or Paris. The Michelin Guide has published a Yongsan neighbourhood guide. Korean media file it under 핫플레이스.

The practical point for a traveller: **if you are going to Yongsan, the food street is the reason, and the electronics market is now the curiosity you look at on the way.** That is the reverse of the itinerary this guide used to recommend, and it is the reverse of most guides still in circulation.

Popular places take walk-in queues at peak times. Koreans check **캐치테이블** before going.

<div class="table-scroll">

| You want | Go to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| New phone, laptop, tablet | Brand stores, **하이마트**, **전자랜드** | Warranty, English support, returns |
| PC parts today | **선인상가**, Yongsan | Immediate stock, assembly on site |
| Big electronics under one roof | **강변 테크노마트** (Gangbyeon), **국제전자센터** (Seocho) | Still functioning multi-floor markets |
| Cables, adapters, organisers | **다이소**, convenience stores | Cheap, everywhere, no negotiation |
| Camera accessories | Camera specialists, lifestyle shops | Inspectable, fairly priced |
| Used cameras | Specialist used dealers | Grading, warranty, returns |

</div>

Two Korea-specific things to hold in mind regardless of where you buy.

**Korea runs on 220 V, 60 Hz**, with the round two-pin **Type C/F** plug — the same shape used across most of Europe, but not the UK, US, or Japan. A device rated `100–240V` is fine with a plug adapter. A device rated for 110 V only is not, and no adapter fixes that.

**Warranty is usually domestic.** A Korean-market device generally carries a Korean warranty serviced in Korea. That is fine for a cable and a real problem for a laptop.

## Camera Shopping, Honestly

Seoul makes you want a lens. Neon alleys, palace roofs, Han River evenings, rain on a cafe window — the city is a permanent argument for more gear.

**Accessories are low-risk.** Straps, pouches, filters, cloths, small tripods, card cases. Inspect it, pay, done.

**Used bodies and lenses are not a market purchase any more.** The trade moved to specialists who grade condition and offer some form of return. If you are buying used, check the exact model, shutter count where it applies, fungus and haze, autofocus, stabilisation, mount compatibility, battery health, charger, menu language and the return terms. Take test frames and look at the corners.

If you cannot run those checks, the atmosphere of a shop is not a substitute for them.

## Tax Refund: The Numbers Changed This Year

This is the most concrete update in this guide, and it matters most on exactly the sort of purchase a tech shopper makes.

Korea's **사후면세** system refunds VAT to foreign visitors. Some stores can do it **on the spot** rather than at the airport, and **on 1 January 2026 the immediate-refund ceilings doubled**:

<div class="table-scroll">

| | Before 2026 | **From 1 Jan 2026** |
|---|---|---|
| Per transaction | ₩500,000 | **₩1,000,000** |
| Total per visitor | ₩2,500,000 | **₩5,000,000** |

</div>

Practical reading: a single purchase up to **₩1,000,000** — roughly a mid-range laptop, a camera body, a good lens — can now be refunded at the counter instead of queued for at Incheon. Above that ceiling you are back to the airport process.

Look for a **TAX FREE** sign and ask *before* you pay, not after. Carry your passport; it is required at the point of sale. Keep receipts flat, and photograph the receipt, box label and serial number.

## A Half Day in Yongsan That Makes Sense Now

The old version of this route sent you to browse the market. Here is one built on what is actually there.

1. **신용산역 Exit 2 or 용산역 Exit 1.** Start on the 용리단길 side.
2. **Walk the alleys of 한강로2가.** This is the part worth your time. Coffee, look at shops, notice how new the whole thing is.
3. **Cross to the market** if you are curious. Twenty minutes is enough to see what a Korean electronics market looked like and what is happening to it.
4. **선인상가** only if you need a part. Otherwise it is a working building, not an attraction.
5. **Buy accessories at 다이소 or a convenience store**, not at a counter where you have to negotiate.
6. **Eat back on the 용리단길 side.** Check 캐치테이블 first if it is a weekend.

## What Not to Buy in Korea

- **Bulky appliances.** Voltage, shipping and warranty all work against you.
- **Laptops and tablets**, unless the warranty situation is clear and you have accepted it.
- **Anything 220 V only** that you intend to use at home.
- **Used gear you cannot inspect** and cannot return.
- **Anything sold with pressure rather than information.**

The worst purchase is rarely a fake. It is usually a genuine product that is wrong for you: Korean plug only, Korean manual only, no overseas service, too heavy, no return, and cheaper at home once you have paid the airline for the weight.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is Yongsan Electronics Market still worth visiting?**
**A:** As a **shopping trip, no.** Half the Najin buildings have come down since June 2025 and the trade that remains is parts, assembly, repair and used stock. As a **twenty-minute look at a disappearing piece of Seoul**, yes — and the 용리단길 food street across from the station is worth the trip on its own.

**Q: Has Yongsan Electronics Market closed?**
**A:** No. **전자랜드** (buildings 1–3), **원효상가** (4–7) and **선인상가** (21–22) are still operating, along with basement shops in 나진상가 staying until their leases end. Redevelopment construction is scheduled for **2027–2030**.

**Q: Where should I buy a laptop or phone in Seoul?**
**A:** Official brand stores or a large electronics retailer such as **하이마트** or **전자랜드**. You will not beat them at the market on price once warranty, language support and returns are counted.

**Q: What is Yongridan-gil?**
**A:** **용리단길** — the restaurant and café alleys in **한강로2가** between **신용산역** and **삼각지역**, five to ten minutes from 용산역 Exit 1 or 신용산역 Exit 2. It grew after Amorepacific moved its headquarters there and is now one of Seoul's busier young-crowd food streets.

**Q: How much tax can I get refunded in Korea?**
**A:** From **1 January 2026** the immediate in-store refund limits doubled to **₩1,000,000 per transaction** and **₩5,000,000 in total** per visitor, up from ₩500,000 and ₩2,500,000. Above those ceilings you claim at the airport instead. Bring your passport to the till.

**Q: Will my Korean electronics work at home?**
**A:** Korea is **220 V, 60 Hz** with the round two-pin Type C/F plug. Anything rated `100–240V` works anywhere with a plug adapter. Anything rated 220 V only will not work on a 110 V supply, and an adapter does not convert voltage.

**Q: Should I buy a used camera in Seoul?**
**A:** Only from a specialist that grades condition and states return terms, and only if you can check the model, shutter count, glass, autofocus, battery and charger yourself. The market counters that used to serve this trade have largely gone.
