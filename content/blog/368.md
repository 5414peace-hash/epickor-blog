If you have bought "Orion Sun Chips" abroad, you have bought something that **does not exist under that name in Korea**.

The Korean product is called **썬**, and the full name on Orion's own catalogue is **태양의 맛 썬** — "taste of the sun, Sun." It lost the word *chip* for a legal reason, then it lost its factory to a fire, and then it came back because people kept asking.

Here is the sequence, which has never been written out in English.

## It Was a PepsiCo Product, Until It Wasn't

**Orion Frito-Lay** was formed in **April 1993** as a joint venture, licensing PepsiCo's snack technology for the Korean market. The chip launched that year as **썬칩** — a straight transliteration of Sun Chip.

The 1994 television campaign, starring **이병헌** and **박소현** off the back of the KBS youth drama *내일은 사랑*, is still what Koreans in their fifties picture when they hear the name.

Then the licence ran out. Orion could no longer use the name, so it did the smallest possible thing: it **deleted the final syllable**. 썬칩 became 썬, and the descriptor **태양의 맛** — taste of the sun — was added in front to carry the meaning the lost word used to carry.

This is why the export listings are confusing. Shops outside Korea still sell it as "Orion Sun Chips," which is the name Orion itself stopped using. If you search the Korean internet for 썬칩 you will find the product; if you look at the bag in a Korean shop, that word is not on it.

## Then the Factory Burned

In **January 2016**, a fire destroyed the production line. Sun had one line, and with it gone, Orion discontinued the product.

It stayed gone for over two years. Korean coverage of the period is full of the phrase **인생 과자** — "the snack of my life" — from people asking for it back, and Orion eventually rebuilt rather than let it go: production restarted at **오리온농협**, a joint venture with Nonghyup in **Miryang, South Gyeongsang Province**.

It returned in **April 2018** under the name **돌아온 썬**, "Sun, returned," which is still printed on the bag today. Within roughly three years of the relaunch it had sold **100 million bags**.

That is the difference between this and every other snack on the shelf next to it. Pocachip and Saewookkang have simply always been there. **Sun is the one Korea had to ask for twice.**

## What Two Years Without It Looked Like

The gap is the part worth understanding, because it explains why the bag still carries the word *returned* eight years later.

Korean snacks get discontinued constantly and almost none of them come back. What made Sun different was that the people asking were **specific about what they had lost**. The complaint was never "bring back the corn chip." It was that nothing else on the shelf had that particular deep-ridge bite — corn chips in Korea were either thin and flat or hard nacho triangles, and Sun sat between them.

Two things sustained it. The first was that Sun had been on shelves for twenty-three years, long enough that the people writing about missing it were adults remembering a childhood snack rather than a lapsed customer base. The second is that Orion had to build a **new production line to bring it back**, at the Nonghyup joint venture in Miryang, which is a far heavier commitment than restarting an idle machine. A company does not build a line for a snack it expects to sell modestly.

The 100-million-bag figure within three years is the answer to whether that bet worked. For scale, that is a country of about 52 million people buying it roughly twice each.

## How Koreans Actually Eat It

Two things that will not be on any export listing.

**It is a beer snack before it is a children's snack.** The deep ridges and the loud seasoning put Sun in the same category as Korean bar food rather than lunchbox food, and Garlic Baguette in particular is treated as an 안주 — something eaten *with* a drink. If you are assembling a Korean convenience-store drinking session, this bag belongs in it.

**Nobody dips it.** The ridge depth that would make a Western chip a scoop is, in Korea, simply where the seasoning sits. There is no salsa alongside. The chip is finished as it comes out of the bag, and adding a dip mostly buries the flavour you paid for.

One more practical note. The 80 g bag is nitrogen-filled and large for its weight, and the chips are structurally sturdier than a potato crisp but they are not indestructible. They travel better than [Banana Kick](/blog/367) and worse than a sealed biscuit.

## How the Korean Chip Actually Differs

Both products are wholegrain corn chips with ridges. They are not the same eating experience.

<div class="table-scroll">

| | **Korean 썬** (Orion) | **SunChips** (Frito-Lay) |
|---|---|---|
| Texture | Lighter, easier to chew | Harder, coarser bite |
| Grain | Wholegrain corn base | Wholegrain with added oats, giving a grittier particle |
| Seasoning | Stronger, more stimulating | Milder, grain-forward |
| Shape | Deep wavy ridges | Wavy, shallower |
| Flavours | Garlic Baguette, Hot Spicy, Original | Harvest Cheddar, French Onion, Garden Salsa |

</div>

The practical version: **the American chip tastes of the grain and the Korean chip tastes of the seasoning.** If you liked SunChips for being wholesome and slightly plain, the Korean bag will surprise you. If you found SunChips boring, this is the fix.

## Which Bag to Buy

Orion's own catalogue currently lists two variants, and Korean retailers carry a third.

**갈릭바게트맛 — Garlic Baguette.** Launched 2019 and the one to start with. 80 g, 403 kcal. Orion builds the flavour on a **발효종**, a fermented starter, which is why it lands closer to actual garlic bread than to the garlic powder you get on most chips. If you buy one bag, buy this.

**오리지널 — Original.** 80 g, 408 kcal. The plain wholegrain corn version, and the reference point for everything else. Worth knowing before you buy: the allergen line lists **밀, 달걀, 우유, 대두, 쇠고기, 닭고기** — wheat, egg, milk, soy, **beef and chicken**. The plain-looking bag is not vegetarian. Garlic Baguette's list is shorter: wheat, milk, soy.

**핫스파이시맛 — Hot Spicy.** Sold widely by Korean retailers and the flavour on most **돌아온 썬** packaging. Hot by chip standards, not by Korean standards.

Over the years the line has also carried Jalapeño Cheddar (2022), habanero, green onion cream cheese and a Mexican grill taco version, while **sweet onion, stir-fried onion, nurungji and injeolmi have all been discontinued.** If you find one of those on a shelf abroad, it is old stock.

### Reading the bag when you are not in Korea

The name situation makes this harder than it should be, so here is what to look for. Any listing that says **Orion** and shows a chip with deep waves is the Korean product, whatever the title calls it. The Korean text on the front is the reliable identifier: **태양의 맛** in a small arc, then a large **Sun** in Latin script, then **썬** in a red or dark badge beside it.

If the bag says **돌아온** above the logo, that is the post-2018 packaging and therefore current stock. Two flavour words are worth memorising because they are printed in Korean more often than English: **갈릭바게트** and **핫스파이시**. Anything listed as "Sun Chips Sweet Onion" or "Nurungji" is a discontinued flavour and has been sitting somewhere for a while — the shelf life is five months from manufacture, so that matters more here than on a biscuit.

## What It Costs

As of **August 2026**, Korean online grocers sell the 80 g bag mostly in multipacks — a six-pack of Garlic Baguette runs about **₩11,500**, which works out to roughly **₩1,900 a bag**. A larger **135 g** bag exists and is better value; a three-pack has been seen around ₩4,880, or about ₩1,630 each.

Convenience stores price single bags higher, as they do everything, and run 1+1 and 2+1 promotions often enough that the shelf tag is worth reading before you decide which flavour you want.

## Who Should Skip It

**Skip it if you want the SunChips you grew up with.** This is not a Korean version of that chip. It is a different recipe from a different company that briefly shared a name, and the seasoning is much louder.

**Skip Original if you avoid meat.** Beef and chicken are on the allergen line of the plainest bag in the range, which is not what anyone expects.

**Skip it if ridged chips are not your thing.** The deep wave is the whole design. If you prefer a thin flat chip, [Pocachip](/blog/366) is the Korean shelf's answer to that and it is a better bag for you.

**Do not skip it** if you like the idea of eating something a country talked a company into bringing back. That is a rare thing to be able to buy for two dollars.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is Korean Sun Chips the same as American SunChips?**
**A:** No. They shared a name between 1993 and the 2000s because **Orion Frito-Lay** made the Korean product under a PepsiCo licence. When the licence ended, Orion renamed it **태양의 맛 썬**. Today they are different recipes from different companies — the Korean chip is lighter to chew with much stronger seasoning.

**Q: Why is it called 썬 and not 썬칩?**
**A:** Orion dropped the final syllable when it lost the right to the licensed name. **썬칩 → 썬**, with **태양의 맛** added in front. Retailers outside Korea still list it as "Sun Chips," which is the name it no longer uses at home.

**Q: Was Sun really discontinued?**
**A:** Yes. A **factory fire in January 2016** destroyed the production line and Orion stopped making it. It returned in **April 2018** as **돌아온 썬**, produced at a new Orion–Nonghyup line in Miryang, and passed **100 million bags** within about three years.

**Q: Which Sun flavour should I buy first?**
**A:** **갈릭바게트맛 (Garlic Baguette).** It launched in 2019, it is the flavour Orion leads with, and the fermented-starter base makes it taste like garlic bread rather than garlic seasoning.

**Q: Is Sun vegetarian?**
**A:** **Original is not.** Its allergen declaration lists beef and chicken alongside wheat, egg, milk and soy. Garlic Baguette declares only wheat, milk and soy. Check the bag, because the plain one is the surprising one.

**Q: How much does Sun cost in Korea?**
**A:** About **₩1,900** per 80 g bag buying online in multipacks as of August 2026, and less per bag for the 135 g size. Convenience stores charge more and discount it often.
