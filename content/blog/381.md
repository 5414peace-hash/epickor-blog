Walk into a Korean department store in the four weeks before Chuseok and you will find an entire floor given over to gift boxes. Dried seaweed. Hanwoo beef. Red ginseng. Pears the size of a grapefruit, individually wrapped like jewellery.

And Spam. Stacked in navy presentation boxes, sold by the dozen, at prices that make Western visitors assume someone is playing a joke.

**Nobody is joking.** Canned ham is one of the most reliably welcome gifts in Korea, and CJ CheilJedang — which makes Spam under licence here — has held the number-one position in processed-food holiday gift sets for years. The company's own figures put roughly **60% of annual Spam revenue** in the two holiday gift seasons.

The part nobody explains in English is how to actually buy one, because they are not sold by name. **They are sold by number.**

## Why canned ham, and why it isn't a joke here

The short version is the Korean War. American military rations put canned meat into a country where protein was scarce and expensive, and it arrived attached to the most powerful army in the world. What was cheap in Ohio was luxurious in Seoul, and unusually for a wartime association, the status stuck.

Spam has been made in Korea by CJ CheilJedang since 1987. Korea is now the **second-largest Spam market on earth** after the United States, and cumulative domestic sales have passed **1.9 billion cans** — counted in 200g-can equivalents — for more than **₩4 trillion** in lifetime revenue. It is a normal ingredient in budae jjigae, kimbap, fried rice and the packed lunch that turns up in every other K-drama.

The holiday gift-set market it leads is worth over **₩1 trillion a year** on its own, and Spam has topped the processed-food share of it for years running.

What makes the gift work is not the ham. It is the **shelf life and the shareability**. A box of Spam is a gift that a household of any size can absorb, that will not go off, and whose value is legible at a glance — which is exactly what you want when you are giving something to your parents' neighbour, your team lead, or a client.

## The price on the box is fiction

Every set in the range is discounted, and not modestly. On CJ's own store on 13 August 2026, the discounts ran from **20% to 51%**, with the mainstream sets clustered around **40-45% off**.

That is not a sale you have caught by luck. It is how this category is priced: the number printed on the box is the **가격표 (list price)** that makes the gift look expensive when the recipient reads it, and the transaction price is something else entirely. Both numbers are doing a job.

**What this means practically:** never pay the listed price, and never assume a discount you see is unusually good. Compare the same 호 across CJ's own store, Coupang, the big supermarket chains and the department stores, and expect the spread to be small. If you find a set at full list price, you are in the wrong shop.

## 복합 means the box is not all Spam

Alongside the numbered sets there is a second family with 복합 (bokhap, "combined") in the name — 복합 특호, 복합 O-G호, 복합 K-2호, 복합 Y호 and others. These look more impressive and often cost more, and they contain **less ham**.

The bottles are CJ's own **백설 (Beksul)** pantry line: 카놀라유 (canola oil), 포도씨유 (grapeseed oil), 요리올리고당 (cooking oligosaccharide syrup, a standard Korean sweetener), plus herb salt and pepper grinders. They are perfectly good products and Korean households use all of them.

**But understand what you are buying.** A 복합 set is a hamper, not a bigger box of Spam. If the recipient wanted ham, a plain 호 set at the same price gives them substantially more of it. If the recipient is a household that actually cooks, the oils get used and the set is arguably the better gift. That is a judgement about the person, not about value — and it is exactly the calculation Korean buyers are making in that aisle.

There is also a growing collaboration range — a KakaoTalk edition of 12호, a "super moon" edition, a gold-bar edition, and co-branded boxes with the Spanish tuna brand Calvo. These are gift-presentation plays. Price them per gram like anything else.

## The yellow cap is gone, and only from the gift sets

For decades the Korean Spam can wore a yellow plastic lid, and Korean households famously kept them — for half-used cans, for organising small things, for no reason at all.

CJ began removing it in **2020**, starting with two Chuseok gift sets, after the cap became a symbol of unnecessary plastic in Korea's environmental debate. The cap was originally there to prevent damage in transit, not to reseal anything. Look at the boxes above and you will see the tan band reading **NO CAP FOR US** — 환경을 생각하여 스팸캡을 제거하였습니다, "we removed the Spam cap out of environmental consideration."

**Here is the detail that matters if you want one.** The removal is complete across gift sets, but **single cans sold on supermarket and convenience-store shelves still have the yellow cap**, because loose stock is more likely to get knocked about. So the famous lid is now a thing you get from buying one can at a CU, not from buying the ₩62,643 presentation box.

## Should you actually buy one?

Straight answer: **as a souvenir, no.**

- **Weight.** 1호 is over four kilograms before packaging. That is a meaningful chunk of a 23kg allowance spent on a product sold in most countries.
- **Customs.** Canned meat is a processed pork product, and rules vary sharply by destination. Australia, New Zealand and Taiwan are strict about pork; the EU restricts meat products from outside the bloc. Check your own country's rules before you buy rather than at the airport.
- **Availability.** It is Spam. The Korean version is made to the same licence and tastes the same.

**When it is genuinely a good buy:**

- **You live in Korea, or are staying long enough to eat it.** The per-gram maths above is real, and holiday season is the cheapest canned ham gets all year.
- **You are giving a gift to a Korean host, colleague or family.** Then it is not a novelty at all — it is a correct, conventional, well-received choice, and 8호 or 6호 is the normal register for someone outside your family.
- **You want to see the aisle.** Spend twenty minutes in the basement food hall of Lotte or Shinsegae in early September. The gift floor is one of the most revealing things about Korean social life you can look at for free.

## FAQ About Korean Spam Gift Sets

**Q: What does 호 mean in 스팸 1호?**

**A:** 호 (ho) is a counter used for numbered editions or model sizes. On Spam gift sets it functions as a **size rank**, and it runs backwards from what most shoppers expect: **1호 is the biggest box, 12호 one of the smallest.** It says nothing about quality — the ham is the same 스팸 클래식 in all of them.

**Q: Which set is the best value?**

**A:** **1호**, at roughly **₩15.4 per gram** at August 2026 prices, against ₩18.8 for 12호. The ladder is consistent: bigger box, cheaper per gram. If you are buying to eat rather than to give, buy the largest box you will get through.

**Q: How much does a Spam gift set cost?**

**A:** As of 13 August 2026, on CJ's own store: **1호 ₩62,643** (listed ₩109,900), **3호 ₩51,645**, **6호 ₩39,988**, **8호 ₩31,295**, **12호 ₩30,102**. Combination sets with cooking oil run higher — 복합 특호 is ₩55,449. Prices move through the season; confirm before buying.

**Q: What is the difference between a 호 set and a 복합 set?**

**A:** A numbered 호 set is **all Spam**. A 복합 (combined) set replaces some cans with CJ's 백설 cooking oils, syrup and seasoning grinders. The 복합 box often costs more and contains less ham, so compare by the weight of ham, not by the price of the box.

**Q: Can I take a Spam gift set home on a plane?**

**A:** Physically yes, in checked baggage — but canned meat is a processed pork product and import rules vary a lot by country, with Australia, New Zealand, Taiwan and the EU among the stricter destinations. Check your own country's biosecurity rules first. Given that Spam is sold almost everywhere, this is rarely worth the risk or the weight.

**Q: Why does my can have a yellow cap when the gift boxes don't?**

**A:** CJ started removing the plastic cap from gift sets in 2020 on environmental grounds and has now finished the job across the gift range. **Single cans on ordinary shelves keep the cap**, because loose stock is more prone to transit damage. If you want the famous yellow lid, buy one can at a convenience store.

**Q: When do the gift sets appear in shops?**

**A:** Roughly four to six weeks before the holiday. For Chuseok 2026 — **24 to 26 September** — the 2026 range was already live and discounted on CJ's own store by early August, and department-store gift floors fill up through late August and early September.

## The one thing to remember

**The number is the size, and it counts down.** 1호 is the big one. Everything else — the discount that looks generous, the 복합 box that looks more impressive, the collaboration edition with a cartoon character on it — resolves once you know that, and once you compare grams instead of cans.

And if you buy one for a Korean household rather than for your suitcase, you will not be making a joke. You will be doing something completely ordinary, which is a better souvenir than most things you can carry.
