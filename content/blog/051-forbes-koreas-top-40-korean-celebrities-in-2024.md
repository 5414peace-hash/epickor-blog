The **2026 Forbes Korea Power Celebrity 40** puts BTS first, followed by G-Dragon, BLACKPINK, Son Heung-min, and Lim Young-woong. That headline is easy to repeat. The more useful question is what the ranking measures.

It is not a permanent list of Korea's most beloved people, a pure fan poll, or a simple estimate of who earned the most money. Forbes Korea combines four weighted signals: estimated income, media mentions, social-media influence, and a fan vote. A person can therefore dominate one category without winning the overall ranking.

This guide gives international readers the 2026 top 10, explains the revised formula, and shows how to read the result without turning a one-year data snapshot into an absolute verdict on fame.

## Why BTS Returned to Number One

BTS did not appear as a group in the 2025 ranking while individual members were completing military service and pursuing separate activities. In 2026, the group returned at number one while all seven members also appeared within the full top 40.

That swing shows how responsive the index is to recent activity. The ranking rewards the economic and attention cycle inside its measurement window. It does not erase past cultural impact when someone drops out, and it does not guarantee that a current number one will hold the same position next year.

## G-Dragon: Fan Mobilization Versus Overall Rank

![G-Dragon performing in 2012](/assets/images/posts/051/gdragon-2012-commons.jpg)

G-Dragon placed second overall but first in the fan vote. Published vote totals put him narrowly ahead of BTS in that component. The final order changed because the vote was worth 20%, while estimated income accounted for half of the index.

This is the clearest example of why "won the vote" and "won the ranking" are different statements. A large, organized fandom can create a strong voting result, but the overall score still depends on three other categories. The system favors celebrities who combine commercial activity, coverage, online discussion, and mobilized fans.

That mix also helps explain why established names can re-enter strongly. A comeback year creates new music, performances, advertising, interviews, searches, and fan activity at the same time. The index is built to detect that concentrated burst.

## BLACKPINK and the Group-Plus-Solo Model

![BLACKPINK members at the Golden Disc Awards in 2018](/assets/images/posts/051/blackpink-golden-disc-2018.jpg)

BLACKPINK ranked third as a group, while Jennie, Lisa, Rosé, and Jisoo also appeared individually in the full 40. Like BTS, this demonstrates a modern K-pop business structure in which the group and each member can carry separate music, fashion, acting, advertising, and social-media value.

That does not mean an individual entry is "competing against" the group in an emotional sense. It means the ranking treats identifiable activity streams as measurable entities. For global readers, this is a useful reminder that K-pop's commercial footprint now extends far beyond group album cycles.

It also makes year-to-year comparisons difficult. A group tour, several solo releases, an acting project, and luxury campaigns can cluster in one period, then quiet down in another. A ranking jump may reflect scheduling as much as a sudden change in public affection.

## Why Athletes Rank Beside Entertainers

![Son Heung-min receiving recognition as an Asian footballer in 2024](/assets/images/posts/051/son-heungmin-bfa-2023.jpg)

Son Heung-min ranked fourth and Lee Jung-hoo ninth. The list has always included sports stars because "celebrity power" in Korea is not limited to music and television. Elite athletes generate salary income, endorsements, media coverage, social discussion, and national attention—exactly the signals the index is designed to combine.

International sports success can also work like cultural export. A Korean player appearing every week in a major overseas league becomes a recurring point of contact between Korea and global audiences. That creates a different type of Hallyu from K-pop, but it still carries brand and media value.

Readers should avoid reducing the athletes to symbols of national pride, however. Their position comes from measurable professional activity and exposure, not simply from being Korean abroad.

## Lim Young-woong and the Domestic Audience Lesson

Lim Young-woong placed fifth and reportedly led the social-media-buzz category. His presence is important because overseas readers often mistake international K-pop visibility for the whole Korean celebrity market.

Trot has a large, committed domestic audience, including older listeners who spend on concerts, albums, products, and organized fan activity. Korean television and advertising also reach far beyond the English-language social platforms international fans tend to watch.

The result is not a contradiction. A celebrity can be less familiar to a casual viewer in London or Los Angeles while having extraordinary recognition and commercial power inside Korea. Any useful guide to Korean influence has to leave room for both global and domestic markets.

## What Changed From 2025

The 2025 top five were IU, Son Heung-min, aespa, Jun Hyun-moo, and Lim Young-woong. In 2026, BTS, G-Dragon, and BLACKPINK occupied the first three positions, while IU moved to 19th.

It would be careless to read that as a collapse in IU's reputation or a permanent hierarchy between artists. The 2025 methodology emphasized recent activity and estimated economic impact; the 2026 method also introduced a 20% fan vote. Different release schedules and a changed formula can move the same celebrity substantially.

This is why the year belongs in every citation. "Forbes Korea ranked X at number one" is incomplete. The accurate version is "X ranked number one in the 2026 Forbes Korea Power Celebrity 40 under that year's methodology."

## How to Read Celebrity Rankings Responsibly

A ranking is most useful when you ask what produced it. Use this checklist:

- Confirm the year and publication date.
- Check whether the list covers Korea, global audiences, one platform, or one demographic.
- Separate estimated income from verified company revenue or personal earnings.
- Treat fan voting as participation data, not a scientific public-opinion survey.
- Do not assume media mentions are all praise.
- Compare several years before calling a trend permanent.
- Distinguish group entries from individual-member entries.

The list can show where money and attention concentrated during a measurement period. It cannot measure artistic quality, private character, universal popularity, or the lasting value of someone's work.

## FAQ About the Forbes Korea Power Celebrity 40

**Q: Who is number one on the 2026 Forbes Korea Power Celebrity list?**
BTS ranks first in the 2026 overall list, followed by G-Dragon and BLACKPINK.

**Q: Is the Forbes Korea Power Celebrity 40 only a popularity poll?**
No. The 2026 formula weights estimated income at 50%, fan voting at 20%, media mentions at 15%, and social-media influence at 15%.

**Q: Why are BTS and individual BTS members both ranked?**
The group and members have distinct group and solo activity. The list can therefore evaluate BTS as one entry and individual members as separate entries.

**Q: Does the ranking show exact celebrity income?**
No. Income figures are estimates based on reported or modeled activity such as music, performances, screen work, salaries, and advertising. They are not audited personal tax records.

**Q: Why did the 2026 list change so much from 2025?**
Recent activity changed, major groups returned, and the 2026 formula added a 20% fan-vote component. Rankings from different years should not be compared as if the method and schedule were identical.

**Q: Is the Forbes Korea list the same as the global Forbes celebrity rankings?**
No. Forbes Korea's Power Celebrity 40 is a Korea-focused annual ranking with its own candidate pool, data sources, and methodology.
