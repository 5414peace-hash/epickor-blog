If you have tried both, you already know they are not the same drink. A **Korean coffee mix** stick — almost certainly **Maxim** — makes something sweet, soft and milky. A **G7** stick from Vietnam makes something dark and bitter that tastes like coffee that has been argued with.

Both are 3-in-1 sticks. Both cost pocket change. English write-ups compare them on taste and stop there, usually landing on "depends what you like."

The more useful answer is that **the two products were designed by countries in opposite positions**. Vietnam is the world's second-largest coffee producer. Korea grows none at all — and drinks more of it per person than the United States.

## Korea Drinks 405 Cups a Year and Grows None of Them

This is the number that reframes everything else.

![Bar chart: South Korea 405 cups of coffee per person per year, United States 318, world average 152.](/assets/images/posts/429/korea-coffee-cups-per-person.jpg)

*Per person, per year, 2023. Chart: EpicKor, from Korean agricultural ministry figures.*

**South Korea drank 405 cups per person in 2023**, against a world average of **152** and the United States' **318**. That is **2.7 times the global rate**, in a country whose climate grows no commercial coffee whatsoever. Every bean is imported.

That combination — enormous demand, zero domestic supply, no inherited brewing tradition — is the condition Maxim was built for. Korea did not arrive at instant coffee because it was cheap. **It arrived at instant coffee because instant coffee is what a country with no coffee culture can standardise**, and then it stayed for forty years because the product got very good at being what Koreans wanted.

Vietnam's condition is the mirror image. The country is the **world's second-largest coffee producer and exporter**, its industry employs an estimated two to three million people, and it specialises in **robusta** — the harsher, higher-caffeine, more bitter species. G7 is what you build when the raw material is right there and it tastes like that.

## What Is Actually In the Stick

Both are "3-in-1": coffee, creamer, sugar. The ratio is where they part.

**Maxim's stick is a dairy-and-sugar product with coffee in it.** A Mocha Gold Mild stick is 12g and runs about **50 kcal**, which for a drink with no milk added tells you what most of the weight is. The coffee is there to flavour a sweet creamy base, not the other way round. That is a deliberate formulation choice, and it is why people who grew up on it describe black coffee as the strange-tasting one.

**G7's stick leads with the coffee.** It is a robusta-and-arabica blend, and the bitterness that English reviewers call "too strong" is not a defect — it is robusta doing what robusta does. Trung Nguyên's own positioning leans on that; the brand's blind-test claim from 2012 put its selection rate at **89%** in Vietnam's 3-in-1 category.

**The practical consequence for you:** if you dislike G7, adding milk fixes it. If you dislike Maxim, nothing fixes it, because the sweetness is structural. Buy a small pack of either before committing to a hundred sticks.

## One Company Holds 88% of Korea's Coffee Mix

This is the part no English article about Maxim mentions, and it is more interesting than the flavour notes.

**Dongsuh Foods held 88.3% of the Korean coffee mix market** in Nielsen's measurement, with more recent Korean reporting putting it around 90%. That is not market leadership. That is a category with one company in it and a rounding error behind.

**Maxim Mocha Gold has been on sale since 1989** and moved roughly **5.3 billion sticks** in a recent twelve-month period. For a country of about 52 million people, that is on the order of a hundred sticks per citizen per year, infants included.

**The price moved recently and the reason is worth knowing.** On **15 November 2024** Dongsuh raised shipping prices across instant coffee, coffee mix and coffee drinks by an average of **8.9%** — Mocha Gold coffee mix 2.16kg went from **₩23,700 to ₩25,950**, and the 500g refill from ₩17,450 to ₩19,110. The company's stated cause was **global abnormal weather cutting coffee production**, compounded by sugar and palm oil costs and the exchange rate.

That is the same climate pressure that has moved coffee prices worldwide. It reaches a Korean office kitchen as roughly ₩20 more per stick.

## The Decade Maxim Spent Losing, and the Year It Stopped

Korea's coffee market grew to **₩3.74 trillion in 2024**, up **35.6%** from 2018. But the growth did not go to coffee mix.

**Roasted coffee — beans, cafés, americanos — grew at about 15.8% a year** over that period. **Prepared coffee, the category holding coffee mix, shrank by about 0.5% a year.** Korea spent a decade moving from a desk stick to a café cup, and there are now over 100,000 cafés in the country to prove it. Our guide to [why Koreans drink iced americanos year-round](/blog/027) covers the drink that took those cups.

Then, in **2024, coffee mix sales rose 0.5% — the first increase since 2014.**

But it is the first time in a decade the line pointed the other way, and it arrived alongside reformulations like the zero-sugar version above — the category answering the specific objection that pushed people to the café in the first place.

## Which One to Buy

**Buy Maxim if** you want the drink Korean offices actually run on, you take coffee sweet and milky, or you want the thing that tastes like the country. Mocha Gold Mild is the default and the right first purchase. White Gold is lighter and creamier still; Supreme Gold is the fuller-bodied one; Zero Sugar exists if the sweetness is the problem.

**Buy G7 if** you want strength and bitterness, you drink coffee black, or Maxim tasted like dessert to you. It is the more conventionally coffee-like of the two.

For everything else in the Korean drinks aisle, our [Korea drinks guide](/blog/060) covers what to reach for — and if you are buying something to bring home in a jar rather than a box, [Korean yuja tea](/blog/430) explains why the big percentage on the front of that jar is not the fruit content.

**Buy neither if** you are chasing café quality in a stick. Neither product is trying to be that, and both are honest about it — which is more than can be said for a lot of instant coffee.

## FAQ

**Q: What is Korean coffee mix?**

**A:** A single-serve stick containing instant coffee, creamer and sugar, dissolved in hot water. In Korea it is overwhelmingly Maxim, made by Dongsuh Foods, which holds about 88% of the category. The flagship Mocha Gold has been sold since 1989.

**Q: Is Maxim or G7 better?**

**A:** They are built for different palates and the difference is structural, not a quality gap. Maxim leads with creamer and sugar and tastes soft, sweet and milky. G7 is robusta-forward and tastes bitter and strong. If you drink coffee black, G7 will suit you; if you take it sweet and white, Maxim will.

**Q: How much does Maxim coffee mix cost in Korea?**

**A:** About ₩19,680 for a 100-stick box of Mocha Gold Mild at Emart, roughly ₩197 a stick, checked August 2026. Nonghyup Hanaro lists ₩19,710. Convenience stores charge a large premium for small boxes, so buy at a supermarket if you can. Our [Korean convenience store food guide](/blog/059) covers what the premium buys you elsewhere in the shop.

**Q: Which Maxim should a first-timer buy?**

**A:** Mocha Gold Mild. It is the default in Korea and the reference point everything else is described against. White Gold is milkier and sweeter, Supreme Gold is fuller-bodied, and Zero Sugar drops from about 50 kcal a stick to about 30.

**Q: Why is Korean instant coffee so sweet?**

**A:** Because it was formulated that way for a market with no domestic coffee production and no inherited brewing tradition. Korea imports every bean it drinks and arrived at coffee through a sweetened, standardised product rather than through beans. Forty years later that is simply what a great many Koreans think coffee tastes like.

**Q: Why is G7 called G7?**

**A:** For the Group of Seven, the world's seven largest advanced economies. Trung Nguyên launched the brand at the end of 2003 with the explicit ambition of competing in those markets, and it now sells in more than 80 countries.

## The One Thing to Remember

Maxim and G7 are the same format solving opposite problems. **Vietnam had the beans and built a product around them. Korea had the demand and built a product around that** — a sweet, creamy, extremely consistent stick that one company sells 5.3 billion of a year.

Neither is the better instant coffee. They are two countries' answers to the question, and you can have both for about ₩200 a cup.
