A Korean restaurant menu says **cider**. You order it expecting something alcoholic and apple-flavoured, and a clear, colourless, fizzy lemon-lime drink arrives.

Nothing has gone wrong. **In Korea, 사이다 means clear lemon-lime soda.** There are no apples in it and there is no alcohol in it. The word means something different here, and the misunderstanding is common enough that Google's most-asked questions about Korean cider are *"is it alcoholic"*, *"is it halal"*, and *"does it have caffeine."*

Short answers: **no, yes, and no.** The longer answers are below, along with the thing every English article about this drink gets backwards.

## Alcohol, Caffeine, Halal: The Questions People Actually Ask

These are the top autocomplete branches for Korean cider in English, so they get direct answers.

**Does Korean cider contain alcohol?**
**No.** 칠성사이다 is a soft drink with no alcohol content whatsoever. The confusion comes entirely from the English word. If you want the alcoholic Korean drinks, that is a [different guide](/blog/060).

**Does it contain caffeine?**
**No** — and unusually, the bottle says so. The label carries **"PURE, CLEAN, CAFFEINE FREE"** in English, which is why the answer is visible in the photograph at the top of this page. Unlike dark colas it also contains **no phosphoric acid (인산) and no caramel colouring (캐러멜 색소)**, which is why it pours clear.

**Is it halal?**
There is no alcohol and no animal-derived ingredient in the standard formula, so the practical answer for most travellers is yes. **But certification is a separate matter from ingredients**, and it varies by market and production site. If you require certified halal rather than merely alcohol-free, check the specific can or bottle in front of you rather than trusting a general answer — including this one.

**Is it healthy?**
It is a sugared soft drink. A 500 ml bottle is around **220 kcal**, essentially all sugar. That is comparable to other full-sugar sodas. If that matters to you, the zero range below is the real answer.

## Why It Is Called Cider When There Are No Apples

The word did not lose its apples in Korea. It lost them in Japan.

In the late nineteenth century, English **cider** — the low-alcohol apple drink — reached Japan. What survived the crossing was not the apples but the sensation: sweet, strongly carbonated, fruit-ish. Japanese adopted the word as **サイダー (saidā)** and applied it to *clear carbonated fruit-flavoured drinks generally*, apple or no apple.

That sense is what passed into Korean as **사이다**. It is a loanword that arrived with its meaning already changed, which is why the English original is no help at all.

So when a Korean menu offers 사이다, the useful translation is not *cider*. It is closer to **"lemonade" in the British sense, or simply "Sprite."**

## The Thing Every English Article Gets Backwards

Search in English and you will be told, repeatedly, that Chilsung Cider is **"Korea's answer to Sprite."**

Check the dates.

<div class="table-scroll">

| | Launched |
|---|---|
| **칠성사이다** | **9 May 1950** |
| Sprite | **1961** |
| Coca-Cola, officially produced in Korea | **1968** |

</div>

**Chilsung Cider is eleven years older than Sprite**, and eighteen years older than legally bottled Coca-Cola in Korea. It is not an answer to Sprite. Sprite arrived in a market Chilsung already held.

The launch date is worth sitting with for another reason. **9 May 1950 is six weeks before the Korean War began** on 25 June. The company launched a soft drink into a country that was about to be destroyed, and the brand survived both the war and the seventy-five years after it.

## The Seven Stars Are Seven People

**칠성** means *seven stars*, and the label carries them.

The name came from the **seven founders** of 동방청량음료합명회사, the company that launched the drink in 1950: 최금덕, 박운석, 장계량, 주동익, 정선명, 김명근, and 우상대.

Their first choice was **七姓** — *seven surnames*, literally naming themselves. They changed it to **七星**, *seven stars*, for the Big Dipper, on the reasoning that the constellation is permanent and a company should aspire to the same.

The business changed hands repeatedly — 동방청량음료 to 칠성음료공업 in 1968, into 칠성한미음료 in 1973, and **acquired by Lotte in 1974**, which is why a drink named after seven Korean founders is sold today by 롯데칠성음료. The seven stars stayed on the bottle through all of it.

## The Zero Range Is Where the Action Is

This is where 칠성사이다 has diverged most from Sprite, and where Korean and English searches disagree completely.

English asks *what is it* and *is it like Sprite*. **Korean asks about variants and sizes** — the top Naver branches are `제로`, `제로 유자`, `500`, `캔`, `355`, `190`, `유자`, `병`. Koreans settled the identity question decades ago and are now choosing a flavour.

Two notes. **Zero failed the first time** — launched 2011, pulled in 2015, brought back in 2021 into a completely changed market, and it is now a core line rather than an experiment. The zero versions sweeten with **알룰로스 (allulose), 수크랄로스 (sucralose), and 아세설팜칼륨 (acesulfame K)**.

And **유자 (yuzu) is the one to try if you see it.** It launched in April 2026, it is genuinely Korean rather than a global flavour extension, and yuzu against a clean lemon-lime base works far better than it sounds.

## What Koreans Actually Do With It

Three uses that are not obvious from the bottle.

**It is the default drink at the Korean BBQ table.** After beer, 사이다 is the most-poured thing at a grill restaurant. It is ordered for the people not drinking, for children, and by drinkers as a palate reset between fatty cuts. If you want the one non-alcoholic order that will not get a second look, this is it.

**It goes into the cooking.** Korean home cooks use 사이다 in marinades and sauces — the sugar helps browning and the acidity tenderises meat. It turns up in 갈비 marinade, in cold noodle broth, and it is standard in [hwachae, the summer fruit punch](/blog/254).

**The word became slang.** In Korean internet usage, calling something **사이다** means it was satisfying — a blunt remark that cut through nonsense, a plot that finally resolved. Its opposite is **고구마**, sweet potato, for something so frustrating it feels like choking. A drama scene can be described as 사이다 or 고구마, and Koreans will know exactly what you mean. That metaphor exists because the drink's defining sensation is relief.

## Chilsung Cider vs Sprite: The Honest Comparison

They are close. Anyone telling you they taste nothing alike is overselling it.

<div class="table-scroll">

| | **칠성사이다** | **Sprite** |
|---|---|---|
| Launched | **1950** | 1961 |
| Profile | Lighter, cleaner, less sweet up front | Sweeter, sharper carbonation |
| Carbonation | Softer | More aggressive |
| Caffeine | None | None |
| In Korea | The default. ~80% of the category | Present, but not the one people reach for |

</div>

The practical difference is **sweetness and fizz intensity**. Chilsung reads as a lighter drink — a soda-water tang arrives first, then the lemon-lime sweetness behind it. Sprite front-loads the sugar and hits harder with the carbonation.

Whether that is better is a preference, not a fact. What is a fact is that **Chilsung got there first**, and the framing that treats it as a Sprite imitation has the history exactly reversed.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Korean cider alcoholic?**
**A:** No. **칠성사이다 is a non-alcoholic soft drink** — a clear lemon-lime soda. The confusion comes from English, where "cider" means a fermented apple drink. The Korean word arrived via Japanese **サイダー** already meaning "clear carbonated fruit-flavoured drink."

**Q: Does Korean cider have apples in it?**
**A:** No. It is lemon-lime flavoured. There is no apple anywhere in it, despite the name.

**Q: Does Chilsung Cider contain caffeine?**
**A:** No, and the bottle says so — the label carries **"PURE, CLEAN, CAFFEINE FREE."** It also contains no phosphoric acid and no caramel colouring, unlike dark colas.

**Q: Is Chilsung Cider halal?**
**A:** The standard formula contains no alcohol and no animal-derived ingredients, so for most travellers the practical answer is yes. **Formal halal certification is separate from ingredients** and varies by market — if you need certification rather than alcohol-free, check the specific product.

**Q: Is Chilsung Cider the same as Sprite?**
**A:** They are similar, not identical. Chilsung is lighter, less sweet at the front, and softer in carbonation. It also **predates Sprite by eleven years** — 1950 against 1961 — so it is not a Korean copy of Sprite.

**Q: How much does Chilsung Cider cost in Korea?**
**A:** As of August 2026, **₩2,500 for a 500 ml bottle**, raised from ₩2,300 on **1 August 2026**. Zero variants rose ₩100–300 at the same time.

**Q: What does 사이다 mean as slang?**
**A:** Something satisfying — a blunt truth or a resolution that clears the air, named after the drink's cooling relief. Its opposite is **고구마** (sweet potato), for something frustrating enough to feel like choking.
