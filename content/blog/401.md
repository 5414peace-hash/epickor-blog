Korea's best-selling toner is named after a date.

The **1025** on the bottle of **라운드랩 1025 독도 토너 (Round Lab 1025 Dokdo Toner)** is October 25 — **독도의 날, Dokdo Day** — and the label's little ink drawing is the islets themselves. The water inside is drawn from the deep sea off **울릉도 (Ulleungdo)**, the island next door. It is the rare skincare product whose entire identity — name, number, artwork, water source — is a map reference.

It is also, more practically, the toner that has won **Olive Young's toner category six years running**, built a company from ₩36 billion to ₩116 billion in revenue in four years with essentially no advertising, and was just bought — by the company behind [Beauty of Joseon](/blog/388) — for around ₩600 billion. This is the fourth stop in our K-beauty product series: what the bottle actually does, the quiet-growth story, the acquisition wave it just joined, and who should pick the other toner instead.

## The Name, Briefly and Honestly

For readers outside Korea: **독도 (Dokdo)** is a pair of small islets in the East Sea that Korea administers and holds with deep national attachment (Japan also claims them, as Takeshima — a long-running dispute this skincare article will not adjudicate). **October 25 is Dokdo Day**, and naming a mass-market toner *1025 Dokdo* — then putting the islets on the label and sourcing the water from neighboring Ulleungdo's deep sea — reads to Korean consumers roughly the way a "Yosemite" outdoor brand reads to Americans, with an extra patriotic charge.

The marketing genius is that the name gave an unadvertised product a story people repeat — exactly what you need when your growth plan is word of mouth. Which was, literally, the plan.

## What It Actually Does

Strip the geography off and the formula is a **low-irritation daily reset toner**: watery texture, mild exfoliation that clears dead surface cells without the sting of an acid product, and mineral content from deep-sea water doing light hydration work. It is fragrance-free and built for sensitive skin — the reviews that made it famous are overwhelmingly from people whose skin tolerates very little.

The honest placement, in the now-familiar frame of this series: **this is maintenance, not treatment.** It will not resurface like a real acid, brighten like vitamin C, or calm a flare like [heartleaf](/blog/396). What it does is make the *rest* of a routine work better — a clean, non-greasy first layer that most skin types can use twice a day indefinitely. Korean users treat it as the neutral default toner: the one you use when you want your toner to do its quiet job and get out of the way.

**The toner decision, since we now cover three:** reactive, flushed skin → [Anua's heartleaf](/blog/396). Dry, tight skin wanting a hydration layer → [Torriden's Dive-In](/blog/398) (a serum, but it occupies the same slot). Normal-to-oily skin wanting daily freshness and mild turnover → this one. One is enough.

## The Company: Growth With No Megaphone

**Round Lab** is the brand of **서린컴퍼니 (Seorin Company)**, founded in **2017** by co-CEOs 이영학 and 정서린. The Dokdo Toner's rise is the industry's favorite case study in unpaid growth: no celebrity, no TV, early years with barely any marketing spend at all — just review platforms doing the talking, then the **2019 Olive Young shelf placement** that lit the fuse.

The numbers after that: revenue of **₩36.4 billion in 2020 → ₩53 billion → ₩57.2 billion → ₩115.6 billion in 2023**, with Olive Young alone worth over ₩100 billion a year. And the streak that defines the brand: **first place in Olive Young's toner category six consecutive years through the 2025 awards** — the longest-running category lock in the awards, worn as a badge on the product cards themselves.

## The ₩600 Billion Twist

Here is the part that connects three articles on this site. In 2025, **Goodai Global — the company behind [Beauty of Joseon](/blog/388) — signed to acquire 100 percent of Seorin Company for a price reported at just over ₩600 billion**, with the deal closing that August, financed with acquisition loans and convertible bonds.

Zoom out and the pattern is unmistakable: [Amorepacific bought COSRX](/blog/395) for ₩935 billion; Goodai — itself an indie-turned-empire — is now rolling up Round Lab while Korean coverage openly debates whether it is building "Korea's L'Oréal." **The era of the independent K-beauty hit brand is consolidating in real time.** For a buyer, nothing in the bottle changes; for anyone reading K-beauty as an industry, the indie shelf you discovered five years ago is quietly becoming three holding companies.

## How Koreans Actually Use It

**Poured, not dropped.** The 500ml size exists because Korean users soak a cotton pad or pour it into palms twice daily — it is a volume product, closer to a liquid cleanser step than a precious serum.

**The 닦토 (wipe-toner) habit.** Applied on a pad and swept across the face after cleansing, it does a second gentle cleanse plus mild exfoliation — the specific use the formula is built around, and the reason the texture is this thin.

**Under everything, morning included.** No acid-style photosensitivity concerns at this gentleness; it is a morning product as much as a night one.

**Paired with its own lotion.** The Dokdo line's lotion is the standard seal; the duo exists because the two were formulated as a set.

## Who Should Skip It

- **Anyone wanting visible exfoliation.** This is maintenance-grade turnover. If you want glow-by-Friday, you want a real acid product used deliberately — different tool.
- **The redness-first face.** Calming is heartleaf's lane; the Dokdo Toner is neutral, not soothing-specialized.
- **Fragrance lovers, again.** Like the rest of this series' minimalists, it smells of nothing by design.
- **Anyone who already owns a daily toner they like.** The differences between good mild toners are small; the switch is rarely worth it mid-bottle.

## FAQ

**Q: What does 1025 mean on the Round Lab Dokdo Toner?**

October 25 — Dokdo Day in Korea. The toner is named for the Dokdo islets, carries a small drawing of them on the label, and uses deep-sea water drawn near neighboring Ulleungdo. The number is a date, not a formula code.

**Q: What does the Dokdo Toner actually do?**

It is a low-irritation daily toner: watery, fragrance-free, mildly exfoliating, with mineral-rich deep-sea water for light hydration. It maintains clear, smooth skin rather than treating anything — no acid-level resurfacing, no brightening, no targeted calming.

**Q: Is Round Lab still an independent brand?**

No. Seorin Company, Round Lab's operator, was acquired — 100 percent, for a reported price just over ₩600 billion — by Goodai Global, the company behind Beauty of Joseon, in a deal that closed in 2025. The formula and lineup continue unchanged; the ownership joined K-beauty's consolidation wave alongside Amorepacific's COSRX purchase.

**Q: How much is the Dokdo Toner in Korea?**

As of August 2026, the 200ml refill pack sells around ₩10,900–11,900, and Olive Young cycles bundle promotions on the 200ml and 500ml constantly — it is one of the chain's permanent flagship deals. On Amazon US the 200ml and 500ml list with routine discounts; check current prices rather than assuming.

**Q: Dokdo Toner vs Anua Heartleaf Toner — which one?**

Pick by complaint. Dokdo is the neutral daily-reset toner: mild exfoliation, no specialization, suits nearly everyone. Heartleaf is the calming specialist for red, reactive skin. If your skin is uncomplicated, Dokdo; if it flushes and stings, heartleaf.

**Q: Why did the Dokdo Toner win so many Olive Young awards?**

Six straight toner-category wins through 2025, and the boring answer is the honest one: it is inoffensive in the best sense — cheap enough to repurchase, gentle enough for the most sensitive reviewers, effective enough to notice. Products with no dealbreakers win longevity contests, and awards streaks are longevity contests.
