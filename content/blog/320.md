Most travelers planning a **Seoul observatory** visit only know about N Seoul Tower — the one from every K-drama rooftop scene. Here's what nobody tells you: Seoul actually has three real contenders right now, and the third one just came back from the dead. The 63 Building's rooftop, closed off to the public for roughly 16 years, reopened in June 2026 alongside a fully rebuilt indoor observatory as "63 SkyPicnic," which means the classic "Seoul Tower or Lotte Tower" debate is now genuinely a three-way decision — and each one is built for a completely different kind of visit.

This guide compares all three head-to-head: what they actually cost right now, how high you really go, what the view looks like, and which one fits your trip instead of just the postcard photo.

Prices and hours change — always confirm current tickets on each venue's official page or a trusted booking platform before you go, especially for 63 SkyPicnic, which is still settling into its post-reopening routine as of this year.

All three sit in genuinely different parts of Seoul, which matters more than most first-time visitors expect. N Seoul Tower is on Namsan, roughly central and walkable from Myeongdong. Lotte World Tower is in Jamsil, on the eastern side of the Han River, anchoring an entire retail-and-entertainment district. 63 Building is in Yeouido, west-central and closer to the National Assembly and the Han River parks than to the usual tourist core. None of them are a quick hop from the others, so picking one usually means picking a neighborhood for that part of your day, not just a building.

## N Seoul Tower: The Romantic Classic

N Seoul Tower sits on top of Namsan mountain, so its effective height is a combination of the hill itself and the tower structure — roughly 480 meters above sea level when you add them together, even though the tower alone is shorter than that number suggests. That elevation trick is actually part of the charm: you get skyline views without needing a 500-meter building to do it.

This is the tower every K-drama uses for a reason. The observatory faces central Seoul and the Han River, the love-lock fences outside are genuinely popular with couples (not just a tourist gimmick), and the surrounding Namsan park makes the whole visit feel like an outing rather than a quick elevator trip. It's also the most accessible after-dark option of the three — cable car and hiking trail access both stay lively into the evening, and the observatory itself runs until 23:00, later than either of the other two.

The tradeoff is that N Seoul Tower is the shortest of the three in usable observation height, and its deck, while renovated over the years, still feels more like a beloved classic than a cutting-edge attraction. If you want the most Instagrammed sunset-into-nightscape view in Seoul, this is still probably it — the skyline framed by Bukhansan's mountains in the distance is a view the taller towers simply can't recreate from their more central, flatter locations. ## Lotte World Tower: The Modern Giant

At 555 meters and 123 floors, Lotte World Tower is the tallest building in South Korea and one of the tallest in the world, and its Seoul Sky observatory — spread across floors 117 to 123 — makes sure you feel every bit of that height. The glass floor section is the signature thrill here: standing over a transparent panel with nothing but Seocho-gu traffic hundreds of meters below is a very different experience from Namsan's tree-lined park views.

Seoul Sky's other advantage is that it's almost entirely indoors, which makes it the strongest weather-proof option of the three — useful in Seoul's humid summers or during a sudden rain shower. The view itself trades intimacy for sheer scale: from this height, the Han River becomes a ribbon and Seoul's endless apartment towers turn into a genuine grid pattern, which is a completely different way of understanding the city's size than street level ever gives you.

It's also the most "attraction-like" of the three, sitting inside the massive Lotte World Tower and Mall complex — easy to combine with shopping, the aquarium, or Lotte World theme park itself, which N Seoul Tower and 63 Building can't match for one-stop convenience.

## 63 Building's 63 SkyPicnic: The 2026 Comeback

This is the one most guides haven't caught up to yet, and its backstory is more specific than "it reopened." The 63 Building's rooftop had been largely off-limits to the public for about 16 years — it was mostly occupied by air-defense and electrical equipment, with only a brief, partial 20%-open anniversary event in 2010 — while the separate indoor observatory, 63 Sky Art, kept operating on its own until it closed in mid-2024 for a full renovation. In June 2026, both stories converged: the indoor floors were rebuilt and the rooftop had its equipment relocated and glass barriers removed for the first time, reopening together as **63 SkyPicnic**, a genuinely different concept from a traditional single-floor observation deck.

Instead of one static viewing floor, 63 SkyPicnic is built around four connected zones: an immersive media theater, a "Sky Voyage Theater" simulating a flight over Seoul, a "Sky Stage" with holographic photo installations, and — the actual headline feature — a rooftop with its glass barriers removed entirely, making it a fully open-air space with direct views over the Han River, Namsan, and the surrounding Yeouido skyline.

That open-air rooftop is what sets 63 SkyPicnic apart from both other towers on this list, neither of which lets you feel actual outdoor air at height. The tradeoff is that it leans more into curated "experience" territory — theaters and photo installations — than pure observation, so if what you want is simply the clearest, longest, most unobstructed skyline view, it's not automatically the strongest pick. But as a novelty, and as the only place on this list you can stand genuinely outdoors above the city, it's worth treating as a real third option in 2026, not an afterthought.

## Which One Should You Actually Pick

- **Choose N Seoul Tower** if you want the classic, most romantic Seoul skyline, plan to visit at night, or want to pair the observatory with a park walk and the love-lock fences.
- **Choose Lotte World Tower** if you want the highest, most dramatic view, don't want weather to be a factor, or plan to combine it with shopping, the aquarium, or Lotte World in the same trip.
- **Choose 63 SkyPicnic** if you want the newest experience in Seoul right now, care about actually standing in open air at height, or are visiting Yeouido for the cherry blossoms and want to add a rooftop stop nearby.

If you can only do one, N Seoul Tower still wins for first-time visitors chasing the classic Seoul skyline photo. If you're a repeat visitor or a height-and-views completionist, 63 SkyPicnic's 2026 reopening is genuinely the more interesting story this year — it's the freshest thing to happen to Seoul's observatory scene in over a decade.

## Tickets, Timing, and Photography Tips

A few practical habits make the difference between a smooth observatory visit and a wasted afternoon at any of the three.

**Book online, not on-site.** All three towers sell tickets through their own apps or sites and through third-party platforms like Klook and Trip.com, and online prices are consistently lower than walk-up counter prices — sometimes by 20% or more, especially for Lotte World Tower's Seoul Sky. Skip-the-line options are worth the small premium on weekends, when queues at Seoul Sky in particular can run well past 30 minutes.

**Golden hour beats midday, every time.** All three observatories are dramatically better around sunset than at 2 p.m. — haze and pollution tend to be worse during the day, and Seoul's skyline genuinely comes alive once the building lights start turning on. If you only have time for one visit, aim to arrive 30–45 minutes before sunset and stay until it's fully dark to get both the golden-hour skyline and the night lights in the same trip.

**Weather changes the calculus.** N Seoul Tower's outdoor deck sections and 63 SkyPicnic's open-air rooftop are both weather-dependent in a way Lotte World Tower's fully enclosed Seoul Sky isn't. On a hazy, rainy, or extremely windy day, Seoul Sky is the safer bet; on a clear evening, the other two reward you with an experience Seoul Sky's glass walls can't fully replicate.

**Bring a lens cloth, not just a phone.** Observatory glass gets smudged constantly from hundreds of daily visitors pressing phones against it for photos — a simple microfiber cloth solves more blurry-photo frustration than any camera setting will.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Which Seoul observatory has the best view?**
It depends what you want. Lotte World Tower's Seoul Sky is the highest and most dramatic in scale. N Seoul Tower offers the most classic, romantic skyline framed by mountains. 63 SkyPicnic is the only one with a genuinely open-air rooftop.

**Q: Is the 63 Building observatory open in 2026?**
Yes. The indoor 63 Sky Art observatory closed in mid-2024 for renovation, and the rooftop — separately closed off to the public for roughly 16 years — had its barriers removed for the first time. Both reopened together in June 2026 under the new name 63 SkyPicnic, on the 60th floor with an open-air rooftop and interactive media zones.

**Q: How much does it cost to go up N Seoul Tower?**
Observatory-only tickets run around ₩29,000 for adults as of 2026, though third-party booking sites often sell for less than on-site prices. Combined cable car and observatory packages cost more.

**Q: Is Lotte World Tower's Seoul Sky worth it if I'm afraid of heights?**
The glass floor is optional to stand on, and the rest of the observatory is enclosed and stable, so most visitors with mild height discomfort are fine. If you have a serious fear of heights, the panoramic glass walls at 500 meters may still feel intense regardless of the floor.

**Q: Can I visit more than one observatory in a single Seoul trip?**
Yes, and many visitors do — they're in different parts of the city (Namsan, Jamsil, and Yeouido), so most people pair one observatory with whatever neighborhood they're already exploring rather than trying to hit all three in one day.

**Q: Do I need to book tickets in advance?**
It's not always required but is strongly recommended for Lotte World Tower's Seoul Sky, especially on weekends, and helpful for 63 SkyPicnic given it's a new, high-demand reopening in 2026. N Seoul Tower is the most flexible for walk-up tickets.

## The Bottom Line

Seoul's observatory scene stopped being a two-horse race in 2026. N Seoul Tower still owns the romantic classic view, Lotte World Tower still owns sheer height and drama, and the 63 Building's surprise reopening as 63 SkyPicnic gives the city its first genuinely open-air rooftop observatory in years. None of them is objectively "the best" — they're built for different trips, different weather, and different moods.

Pick based on what kind of view you actually want to remember, not just which tower is tallest.
