**Korea e-Arrival Card** and **K-ETA** sound like the same kind of travel paperwork, but they are not the same thing.

That confusion is exactly why tourists panic the week before flying to Seoul. One site says arrival card. Another says electronic travel authorization. A random agency site says it can "help" for a fee. A friend says they did not need K-ETA. Someone on social media says to fill out everything. Suddenly a Korea trip that was supposed to be about food, shopping, K-pop, and cafes becomes a browser-tab mess.

Here is the clean version: the Korea e-Arrival Card is an electronic entry declaration, while K-ETA is an electronic travel authorization for visa-free travelers who are required to apply. Some travelers may be exempt from K-ETA, but that does not mean you should ignore official entry checks. Use the official sites, avoid fake fee traps, and finish the paperwork before the airport day.

This guide explains the difference, when to do each step, what to prepare, and how to keep your Korea arrival day boring in the best possible way.

## Quick Answer: What Do Tourists Need Before Entering Korea?

Before flying to Korea, check three things:

1. **Do you need a visa?** That depends on your passport, purpose, and stay length.
2. **Do you need K-ETA?** If you are a visa-free traveler, you may need K-ETA unless your passport category is exempt.
3. **Do you need the Korea e-Arrival Card?** Use the official e-Arrival Card site and navigator to check whether you must submit an electronic entry declaration.

Use only official government sites:

- <a href="https://www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr/portal/main/index.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Korea e-Arrival Card official site</a>
- <a href="https://www.k-eta.go.kr/portal/newapply/index.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">K-ETA official site</a>

The official e-Arrival Card site says the declaration has **no fee** and should be completed and submitted **within 3 days before arrival in Korea**. The official K-ETA site says K-ETA applications are available only through `www.k-eta.go.kr` or the K-ETA mobile app, with a fee of **KRW 10,000**, approximately USD 7 to 8, and assessment generally within **72 hours**.

That means the simple travel rule is:

Do K-ETA early if you need it. Do the e-Arrival Card close to arrival if you are required to submit it.

## Korea e-Arrival Card vs K-ETA: The Difference

The names are easy to confuse because both are electronic and both relate to entering Korea. But they answer different questions.

K-ETA asks: **Are you authorized to travel to Korea without a visa under your passport category?**

The e-Arrival Card asks: **Can Korea collect your entry declaration details before you arrive?**

That is why one may not replace the other. A traveler could be exempt from K-ETA but still need to follow a separate arrival declaration process, depending on official rules. Another traveler may need a visa instead of K-ETA. A long-term resident or visa holder may have different steps again.

## What Is The Korea e-Arrival Card?

The Korea e-Arrival Card is Korea's electronic entry declaration system. It moves the old-style arrival-card logic online.

On the official website, the core promise is simple: no fee, online writing, and submission within 3 days before arrival in Korea. The site also has an e-Arrival Card Navigator that asks about valid entry-related documents such as visa, K-ETA, residence card, or none.

The form process shown on the official site includes:

- terms and conditions agreement
- passport information
- travel itinerary
- arrival and departure or stay information
- information check
- submission

For a normal tourist, the practical preparation is simple. Have your passport, arrival flight, departure plan, accommodation address, email, and travel dates ready.

Do not fill it out too early. The official site says within 3 days before arrival, so it belongs in the final pre-flight checklist, not the early dreaming stage of trip planning.

## What Is K-ETA?

K-ETA stands for Korea Electronic Travel Authorization.

It is for travelers who can enter Korea without a visa but are required to obtain electronic authorization before travel. The exact requirement depends on passport, stay purpose, stay length, and current exemptions. Because K-ETA rules have had temporary exemptions and updates, do not rely on an old blog, old video, or friend from last year.

Use the official K-ETA site and check your eligibility.

The official K-ETA page also warns travelers to be careful of similar agencies or phishing sites that charge high fees for application agency or expedited services. That warning matters because K-ETA searches often attract lookalike websites.

The official site lists:

- official channel: `www.k-eta.go.kr` or the K-ETA mobile app
- fee: KRW 10,000, approximately USD 7 to 8
- assessment time: generally within 72 hours
- member and non-member application options
- application result checks and travel information updates

If you need K-ETA, do not wait until the final night. A 72-hour estimate is not a promise that your exact case will finish comfortably before boarding.

## The Scam Risk: Free Form vs Paid Authorization

This is the part tourists really need to hear:

The Korea e-Arrival Card official site says **no fee required**. If a website tries to charge you for the e-Arrival Card, slow down and verify the URL.

K-ETA does have an official fee. But the official K-ETA site also warns about similar agencies and phishing sites charging high fees. Paying more does not automatically mean faster, safer, or more official.

Use this URL habit:

- e-Arrival Card: domain should be `e-arrivalcard.go.kr`
- K-ETA: domain should be `k-eta.go.kr`

The `.go.kr` domain is the key signal that you are on a Korean government site. Do not trust a site only because it ranks high in search, has a clean design, says "official-looking" things, or displays a Korean flag.

## The Best Timeline Before Your Korea Flight

Think of Korea entry prep in layers.

**One month before travel:** Check whether your passport needs a visa, K-ETA, or neither. If you need a visa, that is a separate process and this article is not enough.

**Two weeks before travel:** If K-ETA is required for your passport, apply through the official site or official mobile app. Do not wait just because the site says assessment is generally within 72 hours.

**One week before travel:** Save official site links, hotel address, arrival flight, departure details, and passport information in one safe notes document. Do not store sensitive scans casually, but keep the practical details easy to find.

**Within 3 days before arrival:** If required, submit the Korea e-Arrival Card through the official site.

**Before leaving for the airport:** Screenshot confirmation numbers, hotel address, and your first transfer. Make sure you can access your email without relying on airport Wi-Fi.

**At the airport:** Keep passport, boarding pass, entry confirmation details, and hotel address accessible. Do not bury them under snacks, chargers, receipts, and duty-free shopping.

## What Information Should You Prepare?

For most tourists, prepare:

- passport number
- passport expiration date
- nationality
- date of birth
- arrival date
- departure date or stay plan
- flight information
- accommodation name
- accommodation address in Korea
- email address you can access
- phone number if requested
- purpose of visit

Accommodation address is where many tourists get messy. A hotel may show a marketing name in English, a Korean address, a road-name address, and a neighborhood name. Save all useful versions. If you use Airbnb-style lodging or a guesthouse, make sure the address is complete enough for forms, taxi rides, and delivery if needed.

## What If Your K-ETA Is Exempt?

If your passport is currently exempt from K-ETA, good. But do not turn that into "I have nothing to check."

Temporary exemptions can change. Passport categories can differ. Stay purpose matters. Entry declaration requirements are a separate layer. Airlines may ask questions at check-in. Immigration officers have final authority at the border.

The safest wording is: "I checked the official site for my passport and travel date, and I saved the result."

That is better than: "I saw a TikTok that Americans do not need it."

## What If You Made A Mistake?

Do not ignore it.

If you made a mistake on K-ETA, use the official K-ETA site options for checking results and updating travel information where available. If you made a mistake on the e-Arrival Card, use the official check/edit function if the site allows it for your case.

Do not send passport information to a random agency chat. Do not pay a second site because it says "urgent correction" unless you have verified it is official. Do not assume the airline can fix immigration paperwork at the counter.

Most mistakes are less scary when you catch them early. The bad version is finding the problem at the departure airport with boarding time approaching.

Before leaving for the airport, check:

## Bottom Line

Korea entry paperwork is manageable if you separate the forms.

K-ETA is travel authorization for visa-free travelers who need it. The e-Arrival Card is an electronic entry declaration. K-ETA has an official fee. The e-Arrival Card official site says no fee required. K-ETA should be handled early. The e-Arrival Card belongs within 3 days before arrival if you are required to submit it.

The bigger lesson is simple: official sites first, random agency sites never by default, screenshots saved, passport ready, hotel address copied, phone charged.

Do that, and Korea can start the way it should: with airport signs, AREX or airport bus decisions, convenience-store snacks, and the satisfying feeling that the boring stuff is already handled.

## FAQ

### Is the Korea e-Arrival Card the same as K-ETA?

No. The Korea e-Arrival Card is an electronic entry declaration. K-ETA is an electronic travel authorization for visa-free travelers who are required to apply. Check both through official sites.

### Is the Korea e-Arrival Card free?

The official e-Arrival Card site says no fee is required. If another site asks for payment for the arrival card, verify the domain carefully before entering personal information.

### How early can I submit the Korea e-Arrival Card?

The official site says to complete and submit it within 3 days before arrival in Korea. It is a final pre-flight task, not something to finish months ahead.

### How much does K-ETA cost?

The official K-ETA site lists the fee as KRW 10,000, approximately USD 7 to 8. It also warns travelers about similar agencies and phishing sites that charge high fees.

### Do I need K-ETA if my country is exempt?

Maybe not, but you should verify your exact passport and travel date on the official K-ETA site. Temporary exemptions and eligibility details can change, and the e-Arrival Card is a separate check.
