Almost every guide tells you that **K-ETA** and the **Korea e-Arrival Card** are two separate layers of paperwork and you should check both. That is technically true and practically misleading.

Here is the shape nobody explains: **holding a valid K-ETA exempts you from submitting the e-Arrival Card.** And nationals of **22 countries and regions do not need K-ETA at all** through **31 December 2026**.

Put those two facts together and most readers of this page face a single choice, not two forms:

- **Do nothing extra**, and submit the free e-Arrival Card before *every* trip; or
- **Pay ₩10,000 once** for a K-ETA you are not required to have, and skip the arrival card for the next **three years**.

That is a real decision with a right answer that depends on how often you come back. This guide gives you the exemption list, the trade, the exact timing, and the scam pattern to avoid.

## Are You Exempt From K-ETA? The Actual List

The Ministry of Justice (법무부) suspended K-ETA — in Korean, 전자여행허가 — for 22 countries and regions from **1 April 2023**. It has been extended every year since; the current extension runs **1 January to 31 December 2026** (Korea Standard Time), confirmed on the official K-ETA portal on 23 December 2025 after being announced at the 10th National Tourism Strategy Meeting (국가관광전략회의).

If you want to check this yourself in Korean sources rather than trusting a travel blog, the search terms are **K-ETA 한시 면제** (temporary exemption) and **전자입국신고** (electronic arrival declaration). Korean-language embassy notices are updated faster than their English counterparts.

**Two more exemptions apply regardless of nationality.** Since 3 July 2023, travellers **aged 17 and under** and **aged 65 and over** are outside the mandatory K-ETA requirement. A family with grandparents and children may find that only the middle generation needs it.

**And the limit worth stating plainly:** this is a *temporary* suspension that has been renewed one year at a time. Nothing has been announced about 2027. If you are reading this planning a trip for next year, verify the date range again rather than trusting this list.

So the rule of thumb for someone from the 22:

- **One trip, no plans to return soon** → submit the free e-Arrival Card in the three days before you fly. Do not pay for anything.
- **Coming back within three years** — a second visit, business travel, family, a K-pop tour schedule → the ₩10,000 K-ETA is arguably the better buy. One application covers three years of entries and removes the arrival-card step every time.

The exemption does not block you from applying. Korea explicitly allows exempt nationals to apply anyway, and the arrival-card waiver is the stated benefit of doing so.

## Who Else Skips the e-Arrival Card

The waiver is not only for K-ETA holders. You do **not** need to submit an e-Arrival Card if you are:

- a **Korean national**
- a holder of a valid **Alien Registration Card (외국인등록증)**
- a **group or electronic visa** holder
- a holder of a valid **K-ETA**

Everyone else arriving as a foreign national submits it. That covers most short-stay tourists from the 22 exempt countries, which is exactly the group most likely to assume they have nothing to do.

## The e-Arrival Card: What It Replaced

Korea launched the e-Arrival Card on **24 February 2024**, moving the old paper arrival card online. For a transition period both were accepted — but **paper arrival cards were only available until December 2025**. In 2026 there is no paper fallback to fill in on the plane. If you are required to submit and you did not, you are sorting it out at the airport.

![A Korea e-Arrival Card form screen showing passport, stay and arrival information fields.](/assets/images/posts/223/e-arrival-card-detail-form-screen.jpg)
*The official flow is terms, passport details, itinerary, stay information, a check screen, then submit. It is a form, not a service — nothing about it should require a payment.*

Submission opens **3 days before arrival**, so this is a final pre-flight task, not something to knock out during trip planning. Have ready: passport number and expiry, nationality, date of birth, arrival and departure dates, flight details, accommodation name and address in Korea, an email you can actually open, and purpose of visit.

**Accommodation address is where people get stuck.** A Korean hotel may have an English marketing name, a road-name address (도로명주소), an older lot-number address, and a neighbourhood name. Save all of them. You will use them again for taxis and deliveries.

## K-ETA: The Numbers That Matter

K-ETA is authorisation to board a flight to Korea without a visa. If your passport is not on the exempt list and you are between 18 and 64, you need it before boarding — not on arrival.

- **Fee: ₩10,000**, roughly USD 7–8, plus a **3% online payment fee**. Non-refundable, including if your application is refused.
- **Validity: 3 years**, multiple entries, or until passport expiry if that comes first. Applications made before 3 July 2023 carry the old 2-year validity.
- **Processing: officially within 72 hours.** Most are approved the same day, but there is **no expedited service** — paying more anywhere does not buy speed, because speed is not sold.
- **Channels: `www.k-eta.go.kr` or the official K-ETA mobile app.** Nothing else.

Do not leave it to the final night. "Usually same day" is not a guarantee, and there is no counter at the airport that can fix a missing K-ETA.

## The Scam Pattern

Both systems attract lookalike sites, and they attack from different angles.

**The e-Arrival Card is free.** Any site charging you to submit an arrival declaration is not the government. There is no premium tier, no rush processing, no "assisted filing" that Korean immigration recognises.

**K-ETA does have a real fee, which makes it harder.** A paid agency site is not obviously wrong the way a paid arrival card is. The official K-ETA site itself warns about agencies and phishing sites charging high fees for application or "expedited" service — and since no expedited service exists, that offer is the tell.

One habit covers both:

- e-Arrival Card → the domain must be **`e-arrivalcard.go.kr`**
- K-ETA → the domain must be **`k-eta.go.kr`**

`.go.kr` is restricted to Korean government bodies. A clean design, a Korean flag, an "official" banner, and a top search ranking are all things a scam site can buy. The domain is not.

![A K-ETA official warning screen explaining non-official websites and agency fee risks.](/assets/images/posts/223/keta-official-warning-screen.jpg)
*The K-ETA operator publishes its own warning about agency sites. If a service offers to make K-ETA faster, that alone is disqualifying — no expedited option exists.*

## Situations Where the Simple Answer Breaks

- **Transiting airside without clearing immigration.** If you never enter Korea, entry paperwork does not apply — but confirm with the airline, because a terminal change or an overnight can turn a transit into an entry.
- **You already hold a visa.** A visa is not a K-ETA and does not need one. Check your visa conditions; a group or electronic visa also waives the arrival card.
- **Long stays and residence.** Registration rules take over past the tourist window. Tourist shortcuts stop applying.
- **Family and group travel.** Exemptions are per passport and per age. One person being clear says nothing about the others, and the 17-and-under / 65-and-over rule can split a family three ways.
- **You made a mistake on a form.** Use the official site's own check-and-update functions. Do not send passport details into an agency chat, and do not pay a second site claiming urgent correction.

## What This Guide Cannot Do

Two honest limits.

**The exemption is renewed annually and 2027 is undecided.** Every extension so far has come late in the preceding year. If your trip is in 2027, the list above may not apply to you.

**Immigration officers have final authority at the border.** K-ETA approval and an accepted arrival card get you onto the plane and through the queue faster. Neither is a guarantee of entry. Carry your return ticket and accommodation details; those are what get asked about.

The safest thing you can say to yourself is "I checked the official site for my passport and my travel date, and I saved the result." That is a much better position than "someone said Americans do not need it" — which happens to be true right now, through 31 December 2026, and was not true before April 2023.

## FAQ

**Q: Do Americans need K-ETA for Korea?**

**A:** No, through 31 December 2026. The United States, including Guam, is one of 22 countries and regions with a temporary K-ETA suspension that has been renewed annually since 1 April 2023. You will need to submit the free e-Arrival Card instead, unless you choose to apply for a K-ETA anyway.

**Q: Which countries are exempt from K-ETA?**

**A:** Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Macau, the United States (including Guam), Canada, the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands, Spain, Poland, Sweden, Finland, Norway, Belgium, Denmark, Austria, New Zealand and Australia — 22 in total, through 31 December 2026.

**Q: If I have a K-ETA, do I still need the e-Arrival Card?**

**A:** No. A valid K-ETA waives the e-Arrival Card. Korean nationals, Alien Registration Card holders, and group or electronic visa holders are also exempt from submitting it.

**Q: Is the Korea e-Arrival Card free?**

**A:** Yes. The official portal charges nothing. Any site asking for payment to submit an arrival declaration is not the government — check that the domain is `e-arrivalcard.go.kr`.

**Q: How much does K-ETA cost and how long is it valid?**

**A:** ₩10,000 plus a 3% online payment fee, non-refundable even if refused. It is valid for 3 years with multiple entries, or until your passport expires if that comes sooner.

**Q: How early can I submit the e-Arrival Card?**

**A:** Within 3 days before arrival. It is a final pre-flight task. There is no longer a paper arrival card to fall back on — those were withdrawn after December 2025.

**Q: Are children and older travellers exempt from K-ETA?**

**A:** Yes. Since 3 July 2023, travellers aged 17 and under and aged 65 and over are outside the mandatory K-ETA requirement, regardless of nationality.

**Q: Can I pay to get K-ETA faster?**

**A:** No. There is no expedited service. Any site offering one is not official, which makes that offer a useful way to spot a scam.

## Bottom Line

Do not think of this as two forms. Think of it as one question: **are you already covered?**

If you hold a K-ETA, a residence card, or a group or electronic visa, you are done — no arrival card. If you are from one of the 22 exempt countries and hold none of those, submit the free e-Arrival Card in the three days before you fly. And if you expect to return to Korea within three years, the optional ₩10,000 K-ETA quietly buys you out of the whole step until 2029.
