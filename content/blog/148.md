Daiso Korea is one of the easiest places to solve small travel problems. Need a laundry bag, cable organizer, sheet mask, notebook, gift wrap, or chopsticks before tomorrow morning? A large branch may have all of them on different floors.

Calling it simply a Korean “dollar store” misses two important details. Prices are not all identical, and the Korean chain is operated by Asung Daiso. Its stores mix basics, seasonal products, licensed characters, beauty, stationery, kitchenware, and travel supplies at clearly marked fixed price points. Quality and value still vary by item, so the best strategy is to shop with a short list and compare size, material, and ingredients.

## What Daiso Korea is good for

The official tourism listing for the Myeongdong Main Branch describes more than 30,000 products and identifies the store as tax-refund eligible. Branch size makes a major difference: a neighborhood shop may focus on daily necessities, while a destination branch can devote floors to beauty, stationery, home goods, and seasonal collections.

## Korean Daiso vs Japanese Daiso: Not the Same Company Anymore

This is the question shoppers arrive with, and the answer changed recently enough that most English pages still get it wrong.

**Korean Daiso (아성다이소) and Japanese Daiso are no longer connected.**

The Korean chain was founded as a Korean company and took an investment from Japan's Daiso Sangyo in **2001**, which is where the shared name and the long-running confusion came from. Then in **December 2023, Aseong Daiso bought back the entire 34.21% stake** Daiso Sangyo held — a transaction reported at roughly **₩500 billion**. Since then the two are separate businesses that happen to share a name.

That matters practically, because the product ranges are genuinely different. Korean Daiso stocks Korean cosmetics, Korean stationery brands, and Korean household goods that a Japanese branch does not carry.

Japanese Daiso built its identity on a single ¥100 price. **Korean Daiso runs a tiered system from ₩1,000 up to ₩5,000**, which is why the shelf-edge labels matter here in a way they do not in a true one-price store.

**The practical consequence for a visitor:** do not assume everything is ₩1,000. The ₩5,000 tier includes items that look identical in size to the ₩1,000 tier, and the difference is on the label rather than the product's appearance. If you are filling a basket quickly, check the tier colour before you reach the till.

## Understanding the price tags

Most items sit in familiar fixed tiers such as ₩1,000, ₩2,000, ₩3,000, and ₩5,000, with some categories at higher marked prices. Do not assume everything is ₩1,000. The shelf label and package price are the source of truth, and multi-packs can make two similar-looking products difficult to compare.

Use unit value rather than the headline price. A ₩3,000 travel bottle set may be useful for one trip, while a tiny beauty product at the same price may cost more per milliliter than its full-size counterpart elsewhere. Look at count, net volume, and durability.

At busy stores, put items back in the correct labeled bay if you change your mind. A product left under the wrong sign can create confusion at checkout.

## Daiso beauty: exciting, but compare the actual product

Daiso’s beauty section has become a destination because Korean brands release compact products and store-specific lines at accessible prices. Popular items can sell through quickly, and online trend lists may be outdated by the time you arrive.

Do not assume a Daiso version is identical to a brand’s full-size product just because the name or hero ingredient looks familiar. Check the ingredient list, concentration claims, net weight, applicator, and usage directions. Smaller packaging can be ideal for testing or travel even when the formula differs.

Patch-test unfamiliar skincare. If you have allergies, active irritation, or a dermatological condition, a viral product is not a substitute for professional advice. The stationery department is easy to browse because many products are lightweight and visually clear: memo pads, stickers, binders, pens, stamps, and gift bags. Seasonal collections change quickly, so treat a social-media product code as a lead rather than a promise.

## Travel supplies worth checking

Packing pouches, laundry nets, zipper bags, compact umbrellas, slippers, and simple organizers can be excellent emergency buys. Travelers staying in Korea for several weeks may also find small cleaning and kitchen items useful.

Check airline rules before transferring toiletries. Refillable bottles must still fit the liquid restrictions applied on your route, and an unlabeled bottle can be inconvenient later. Never transfer medicine into an anonymous container without keeping the original prescription or packaging information.

For power products, verify Korean voltage, plug type, safety certification, and device specifications. A cheap mechanical cable organizer is lower risk than an unknown charger. If the item could damage an expensive phone or laptop, buy from a reliable electronics retailer.

## How to shop a large multi-floor branch

Large tourist branches can be crowded after dinner and on weekends. Photograph the floor directory, then group your list by department instead of riding the elevator for each new idea. Use a basket until you understand how bulky the purchase will become in your luggage.

## Tax refunds and payment

Tax-refund eligibility depends on the branch, traveler status, purchase amount, and current rules. A sign at one large tourist branch does not guarantee the same service everywhere. Carry your physical passport if you intend to claim an immediate refund, and ask staff before paying.

Cards are common at major branches. Self-checkout interfaces may include English, but payment methods can differ by terminal and issuer. Keep a physical card or another backup; do not rely on a particular foreign mobile wallet unless that exact store confirms it.

## What not to buy automatically

Skip bulky storage bins unless you live locally. Avoid cosmetics with broken seals or unclear instructions. Do not buy a cooking tool without checking whether it is intended for food contact and the temperatures you will use. Be cautious with health claims and electronics whose specifications you cannot verify.

Trend-driven scarcity also creates bad decisions. If a viral color or skincare item is sold out, a random substitute is not necessarily equivalent. Save the product name and ingredient list, then compare at Olive Young, a brand store, or another Daiso branch.

## Choosing the right branch instead of the biggest branch

The largest tourist store is useful when you need several departments at once, but it is not automatically the fastest or best-stocked option. A neighborhood branch can be calmer for household basics, while a branch near an office district may carry practical stationery and lunch-hour supplies. Store assortment reflects available floor space and local demand.

Search the Korean or English store name in a current map app, then read recent photos and reviews for elevators, floor count, checkout lines, and tax-refund service. Reviews are clues, not official inventory. A post from last month cannot promise that a limited character collaboration remains today.

Build the stop into an existing route. Myeongdong works well with department stores and beauty shopping; Hongdae can combine character goods and fashion; a residential branch is convenient when you need laundry or kitchen basics near a longer stay. Crossing Seoul solely for one ₩3,000 item usually costs more time than the item saves.

If a product code is visible in a social post, save a screenshot with the Korean name and package image. Staff can identify it more easily than from a translated nickname. Be polite when a store is busy and accept that employees may not know future restock dates.

## Returns, defects, and getting purchases home

Inspect fragile items and sealed sets before leaving Korea. Keep the receipt and original packaging; return eligibility depends on item condition, time limit, and store policy. Cosmetics, opened hygiene goods, food, and seasonal clearance products may have restrictions. Ask the branch rather than assuming another country’s consumer policy applies.

Separate liquids in a leak-resistant pouch, protect notebook corners, and place ceramic or glass items inside clothing in the center of a carry-on when airline rules allow. A [Seoul stationery shopping guide](/blog/298) offers more packing advice for paper and pens. Photograph receipts for warranty or customs reference, but retain the paper copy until the trip ends.

Do not remove retail labels from items that your destination customs authority may need to identify. Check import rules for cosmetics, food, seeds, wood, batteries, and animal products before purchasing multiples as gifts.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Is Daiso Korea owned by the Japanese Daiso chain?**

The Korean business is operated by Asung Daiso. Travelers should use Korean branch information and policies rather than assuming another country’s assortment or service applies.

**Q: Is everything at Daiso Korea ₩1,000?**

No. Products use several clearly marked fixed price tiers. Always check the package and shelf label.

**Q: Are Daiso beauty products the same as full-size brand products?**

Not necessarily. Some are store-specific lines or smaller formats. Compare ingredients, quantity, directions, and claims product by product.

**Q: Can tourists receive a tax refund?**

Some tourist-oriented branches offer tax-refund service to eligible visitors. Requirements and thresholds apply, so bring your passport and ask before payment.

**Q: Which Seoul Daiso is best for visitors?**

The Myeongdong Main Branch is convenient and large, but the best branch is often the one near your route. Inventory changes, so no branch can guarantee a viral item.

**Q: Can I pay by foreign credit card?**

Major branches generally accept cards, but issuer or terminal problems can occur. Carry a backup payment method.
