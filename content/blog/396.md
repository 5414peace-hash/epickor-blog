The second-most-famous ingredient in Korean skincare right now is a roadside weed whose Korean name means, literally, **"fish-smell grass."**

**어성초 (eoseongcho)** — heartleaf, the plant botanists call *Houttuynia cordata* — earned that name honestly: crush the leaves and they smell like fish. Koreans have used it in folk medicine for skin trouble for centuries. And since roughly 2023, a clear bottle containing **77 percent of it** has been one of the best-selling toners on Amazon, made by a brand most buyers assume is a decades-old K-beauty house.

It is not. **아누아 (Anua)** belongs to a company started in 2017 by two Seoul National University classmates — an anthropology major and an economics major, both born in 1988 — who took no outside investment and rode this one bottle to **₩717.7 billion in revenue** last year.

This is the explanation the listing does not carry: what heartleaf actually is and does, what the 77 means (and the ingredient list that proves it), what Koreans pay versus what you pay, the margin problem the Korean business press is now writing about, and — as with every product this site covers — who should not buy it.

## The Weed Itself: 어성초

Heartleaf grows wild and low across Korea — damp ground, forest edges, the unglamorous margins of rice fields. Korean folk medicine used it for generations on inflamed skin, wounds, and heat rashes, and older Koreans know it less as a skincare ingredient than as something a grandmother boiled.

The fish smell is real and chemical — the plant's aldehydes are close cousins of the compounds in fish — and it vanishes in processing. What survives into a cosmetic extract is the part dermatology cares about: **quercetin and related flavonoids with measurable anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activity.** That is why heartleaf became Korean skincare's default answer for **redness** specifically, the way cica is for barrier repair and snail mucin is for hydration.

If you want the full map of these calming ingredients — cica, mucin, propolis, heartleaf — our [K-beauty ingredient decoder](/blog/312) places them side by side. And the philosophical opposite of this plant-based bottle is the one we covered last: [COSRX's snail essence](/blog/395), animal-derived where this one is marketed vegan. The two are the poles of the same Korean soothing shelf, and the choice between them is more about texture and ethics than efficacy.

## What "77" Means — and the Document That Proves It

Marketing numbers on K-beauty bottles are often garnish. This one is the recipe, and the proof is on the regulated part of the label.

![Anua's ingredient list card for the Heartleaf 77 toner, the INCI list beginning with Heartleaf Flower/Leaf/Stem Extract (77%), followed by purified water.](/assets/images/posts/396/anua-heartleaf-77-ingredient-list.jpg)
*Not the marketing panel — the ingredient list. The first line is `Heartleaf Flower/Leaf/Stem Extract (77%)`, which means the extract, not water, is the base of the formula. On most toners, water is the first ingredient. Manufacturer ingredient disclosure, Anua.*

Ingredient lists are ordered by concentration and regulated, so the first line is the honest one: **the heartleaf extract is the bulk of the bottle, with purified water second.** The renewed version layers hyaluronic-acid derivatives and panthenol on top of that base, at a skin-matching pH of 5.5.

What that composition delivers, used consistently: **visible calming of redness and reactive skin, light watery hydration, and a well-behaved first layer** under serums and moisturizer. What it structurally cannot deliver: exfoliation, acne treatment in the clinical sense, or pigment fading. Like the snail essence, its bad reviews are mostly the sound of the wrong expectation meeting the right product — it soothes the skin you have; it does not renovate it.

## Two Classmates, No Investors, ₩717 Billion

The company story is unusual even by K-beauty's standards.

**이창주 (Lee Chang-ju)** and **이선형 (Lee Sun-hyung)** — Seoul National University classmates, one from anthropology, one from economics, both born in 1988 — founded **더파운더즈 (The Founders Inc)** in 2017 and spent two years building Anua before launch. The premise was the same consumer-first instinct that built COSRX, aimed at a different niche: sensitive, redness-prone skin, treated with a traditional Korean herb rather than a lab novelty.

The growth curve bent at Amazon. Korean coverage records the brand's **2023 Amazon revenue at five times the prior year's**, a **537 percent surge in one Prime Day**, and by **2026, number-one rankings in major categories on both US and Japanese Amazon Prime Day events.** Consolidated revenue reached **₩717.7 billion with ₩129.5 billion in operating profit** — built, the Korean business press notes with some wonder, **without a won of outside investment.**

The honest counterweight, from the same press: the marketing that fueled this — Korean actress Suzy, then Kendall Jenner as global face — is expensive, and **operating margins have been compressing** as the company spends to hold its position against every K-beauty brand now launching a heartleaf line. None of that changes what is in the bottle. It does mean the brand premium you pay partly funds the billboard, which is true of every beauty giant and worth saying out loud about this one.

## How Koreans Actually Use It

**The 7-skin trick predates the product.** Layering a watery toner in multiple thin passes — 겹바르기 — is a Korean habit this bottle happens to suit perfectly. Two or three passes pressed in with palms beats one splashed-on pass.

**As a rescue mask.** Soak cotton pads, lay them on cheeks for three to five minutes after too much sun or an over-enthusiastic exfoliation night. This is the use case Korean reviewers rate highest, and it is where the calming is most visible.

**Fridge-cold in summer.** Like the snail essence, it needs no refrigeration but rewards it — cold heartleaf pads on a flushed August face are a small Korean-bathroom luxury that costs nothing.

**Not a treatment step.** Koreans run it as the quiet first layer under actives, not instead of them. If redness is severe, persistent, or medical (rosacea, dermatitis), the toner is comfort, not care — a dermatologist outranks any bottle.

## Who Should Skip It

- **Anyone expecting acne treatment.** Heartleaf calms the redness around breakouts; it does not treat the breakout. A BHA or benzoyl peroxide does different work.
- **Fans of rich, cushiony textures.** This is water-thin by design. If the snail essence's slip appeals to you, this will feel like nothing — that is the point, and it is not yours.
- **Anyone whose budget covers one product.** The permanent EpicKor answer: sunscreen first, always. A calming toner is the garnish of a routine, not its foundation.
- **Plant-allergy caution.** Rare, but heartleaf is a botanical extract at very high concentration — patch-test if your skin reacts to plant extracts generally.

## FAQ

**Q: What is heartleaf in Korean skincare?**

어성초 (eoseongcho), the herb *Houttuynia cordata* — a plant used in Korean folk medicine for inflamed skin, now processed into extracts rich in quercetin and flavonoids with documented anti-inflammatory activity. It is K-beauty's signature ingredient for redness, alongside cica for barrier repair and snail mucin for hydration.

**Q: Does the Anua toner smell like fish?**

No. The plant earns its Korean name — fish-smell grass — when crushed fresh, but the cosmetic extract is processed and the toner is fragrance-free and close to scentless. The name is the best trivia in your bathroom, not a warning.

**Q: Is Anua a big Korean brand?**

Bigger than most buyers guess and younger than they assume. Parent company The Founders Inc was started in 2017 by two Seoul National University classmates, took no external investment, and reached ₩717.7 billion in revenue with ₩129.5 billion operating profit — driven by Amazon, where the heartleaf line hit number-one category rankings on US and Japanese Prime Day in 2026.

**Q: What does the 77 in Heartleaf 77 mean?**

Heartleaf extract is 77 percent of the formula — confirmed by the regulated ingredient list, where `Heartleaf Flower/Leaf/Stem Extract (77%)` appears first, ahead of water. Current bottles are labeled 77+ HYALURON, the renewed version with added hyaluronic-acid derivatives at pH 5.5.

**Q: How much does the Anua heartleaf toner cost?**

As of August 2026: ₩25,000 list at Olive Young with promotions near ₩19,900, and $23 list on Anua's US store with frequent Amazon discounts. Watch the size — 250ml, 350ml and 500ml versions exist, and third-party listings sometimes price small bottles near big-bottle money.

**Q: Anua heartleaf toner vs COSRX snail essence — which one?**

Different jobs, different textures, different ethics. Heartleaf is plant-derived, marketed vegan, water-thin, and aimed at calming redness. Snail mucin is animal-derived, slippery, and aimed at hydration and barrier comfort. Reactive, flushed skin leans heartleaf; dehydrated, tight skin leans snail. Plenty of Korean routines simply run both, toner first.
