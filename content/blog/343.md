A soft cheese cookie has been sold out in Korea for most of 2026. Orion launched **촉촉한 황치즈칩** (chokchokhan hwang-cheese chip, "Moist Yellow Cheese Chip") on **26 February 2026**, sold through the entire first run of 380,000 boxes in about two weeks, ran a second batch, ran a third, and it is *still* not something you can count on finding.

At the peak, a box with a **₩4,480** list price was listed at **₩70,000** on a Korean resale platform. That is roughly 15 times list, for a cookie.

Here is what it actually is, why the shortage is structural rather than theatrical, and — the part most coverage skips — what Orion itself has said about why this can never become a regular product.

## First, it is not a chip

The English name is misleading and it trips up almost everyone. This is **a cookie, not a crisp.**

촉촉한 (*chokchokhan*) means moist or soft. The product is the cheese version of Orion's **촉촉한 초코칩**, a soft-baked cookie that has been on Korean shelves since **1997**. The format is identical: a soft, chewy cookie base with hard chips embedded in it. The only change is that the chocolate chips are replaced with hard, savory yellow-cheese chips.

So the eating experience is a soft sweet cookie that keeps hitting salty crunchy pockets. Korean coverage describes it as *"촉촉한 식감의 치즈 쿠키에 달콤 짭짤한 풍미의 황치즈칩을 더한 제품"* — a moist-textured cheese cookie with sweet-salty yellow cheese chips added. The package tagline is **"단짠 황치즈칩이 콕콕!"** — *dan-jjan*, sweet-salty, the flavor axis Korea has been chasing for a decade.

If you are picturing a cheese-dusted potato chip, recalibrate. Think closer to a cheddar-studded shortbread.

## What "황치즈" actually means

**황치즈 is not a variety of cheese. It is a flavor category** — literally "yellow cheese" — and it is built on **cheddar cheese powder (체다치즈 분말)**.

Korean food-industry reporting is specific about why manufacturers reach for it: cheddar powder delivers strong flavor and aroma in small quantities compared with actual cheese, and it produces a deep yellow color. It is flavor engineering, not a dairy category. The nearest Western reference point is the coating on cheese puffs, not a wedge of aged cheddar.

That matters for the reason the trend exists at all. As one industry source put it to *Maeil Business Newspaper*: *"최근에는 익숙한 맛을 얼마나 새롭게 구현하느냐가 중요해졌다"* — what matters now is how *newly* you can execute a *familiar* flavor.

Cheddar snacks are not new anywhere. What changed is that a strong yellow color plus a sweet-salty profile performs unusually well on short-form video.

The Daiso episode is the one worth understanding, because it is often reported wrong. Daiso received stock *late* — and by the time it arrived, every other channel was already empty. So for a matter of hours on 24 March, a ₩2,000 packet at Daiso was the only legitimately purchasable supply in the country. It sold out the same day. It was a window, not a channel.

Note the shape of that curve. **The premium collapsed from roughly 8–15x in March to about 2.7x by July, once the third run landed.** Anyone who paid the March price bought at the top.

One pricing note that confuses foreign readers: Korea uses **오픈프라이스** (open pricing), so retailers set their own. ₩4,480 is the Lotte Mart / Kurly price for the 320g box; Orion's own online mall lists it at ₩5,600, and most Korean articles quote that as the 정가. Both are real. The 160g size runs ₩2,000–2,800.

## The part nobody outside Korea has reported

Every English write-up of this product treats the shortage as marketing. Some Korean coverage does too — *Hankyung* attributed part of it to deliberate 헝거마케팅 (hunger marketing).

But Orion gave a concrete, checkable reason, and it has not appeared in English anywhere:

> **촉촉한 황치즈칩 shares a production line with 촉촉한 초코칩.** Without building additional capacity, Orion says it cannot run the product as a permanent SKU — *"생산라인을 공유하고 있어 생산능력 한계상 상시 제품으로 운영하기는 어렵다… 증설 없이는 한정판 형태를 유지할 수밖에 없는 상황."*

Every box of cheese chips is a box of chocolate chips not made. Orion is not withholding supply to build hype; it is choosing between two products on one line, and the 1997 cookie is the one that sells every week of the year.

There is a second Korean-source insight that explains why they will not simply build a new line. From *e-Today*, quoting the industry's own dilemma:

> *"트렌드를 반영한 제품을 제과업체가 출시하면 이제 트렌드가 끝났다고 판단하는 소비자도 있을 정도"* — it has reached the point where some consumers decide a trend is over *the moment* a confectionery company ships a product based on it.

Retooling a factory takes longer than a Korean snack fad lasts. That is the actual calculation.

## It became a whole category

By June this stopped being one product. Korean media named it — *EBN* framed 황치즈 as a contender to become **"제2의 두쫀쿠"**, the successor to the Dubai chewy cookie craze, alongside 버터떡 (butter tteok) and 봄동 (spring cabbage). Others reached for an older benchmark: **"제2의 허니버터칩."**

The hard number behind the trend, from GS Retail: **GS25's 황치즈 snack category grew 17.3% month-on-month in May 2026, then 115.4% year-on-year as of 22 June.**

## Can you buy it outside Korea? No — and be careful who tells you otherwise

Checked directly against Weee!, H Mart, Amazon US, Yami, eBay and Orion's own English catalog: **촉촉한 황치즈칩 is not sold through any legitimate international channel.**

This is structural, not a temporary stockout. The entire supply sold domestically, the product has **no export SKU**, and it does not appear in Orion's English catalog at all — limited editions never get one.

A note on sourcing, since this is exactly where English coverage of Korean snacks goes wrong: at least one English-language Korea site states this product launched in "late March." Every Korean source, and Orion's own launch announcement, says **26 February**. If a page cannot get the launch date right, treat its stock claims with the same suspicion.

## If you are in Korea and want to actually find one

The product moves in runs, so hunting it is a timing exercise rather than a location one. What has actually worked, based on how the three runs played out:

**Check convenience stores first, not marts.** The third run went into major convenience store chains sequentially from the third week of June — CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, Emart24. Convenience stores restock daily and turn over faster than a large mart's snack aisle, so a store that was empty yesterday is a different proposition today.

**Daiso is worth a look but do not build a trip around it.** Daiso carried the 160g size at ₩2,000, the cheapest legitimate price anywhere. But it received stock late and sold out within hours. If a Daiso near you has it, it is the best price you will find; it is not somewhere to travel to on the chance.

**Orion's own online mall and Naver Brand Store are the most reliable signal.** Both the third run and the June mid-year sale were sold through Orion's official channels first. If you read Korean, watching the brand store is more efficient than walking stores.

**Check the packet count before you pay.** There are two sizes and the resale market blurs them constantly: 320g is 16 packets, 160g is 8. A "₩12,000" listing means very different things depending on which one arrives.

**The realistic expectation:** if you are in Korea for a week and not specifically hunting, you may simply not see one. That is the normal outcome, not bad luck. The substitutes below are on shelves every day.

- **파리바게뜨 황치즈 페스츄리** if you would rather have it as a bakery item.

**Is the resale worth it?** Run the arithmetic before you decide. At list, ₩4,480 for 16 packets is **₩280 per 20g packet**. At March's ₩70,000 peak, that is **₩4,375 per packet** — roughly a full café dessert, for one cookie serving. At July's ₩12,000 it is ₩750.

The honest answer: this is a soft cookie with cheese chips in it. It is not rare in kind, only rare in stock. Paying a multiple buys scarcity, not a flavor you cannot otherwise reach — and the premium has already collapsed once.

The precedent is instructive. **허니버터칩** in 2014 listed at ₩1,500, resold above ₩5,000, and one seller notoriously offered the *smell* of an empty bag for ₩1,200. It is an ordinary permanent product today. Korean snack manias reliably deflate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is 촉촉한 황치즈칩?**
A soft-baked cookie from Orion with hard yellow-cheese chips in it — the cheese version of 촉촉한 초코칩, which has existed since 1997. Despite "chip" in the name it is a cookie, not a crisp.

**Q: How much does it cost in Korea?**
About ₩4,480 for the 320g / 16-packet box at Lotte Mart and Kurly, ₩5,600 at Orion's own mall, and ₩2,000–2,800 for the 160g size. Korea uses open pricing, so retailers differ.

**Q: Why does it keep selling out?**
It shares a production line with 촉촉한 초코칩. Orion has stated it cannot run it as a permanent product without expanding capacity, so it comes in limited runs — 380,000 boxes, then about 200,000, then a third undisclosed run.

**Q: Can I buy it in the US or outside Korea?**
No. There is no export SKU and it does not appear in Orion's English catalog. Available Orion cheese alternatives abroad include O!Karto Cheese, Yegam Cheese and O!Tube Cheddar Cheese.

**Q: Is 황치즈 a type of cheese?**
No — it is a flavor category meaning "yellow cheese," built on cheddar cheese powder. It is engineered for strong flavor and deep yellow color rather than being a dairy variety.
