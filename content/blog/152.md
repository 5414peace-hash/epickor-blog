## The Core Design Logic of Hangeul
Hangeul's letters are based on how speech organs move. This is why many designers call it “functional typography,” not just writing.

1. **Consonant forms** reflect tongue, teeth, and throat positions.
2. **Vowel axes** reflect sky, earth, and human symbolism.
3. **Block composition** creates visual rhythm and scanning stability.

This engineered structure gives Hangeul a rare balance of beauty and utility.

## Practical Learning Route for Non-Korean Readers
If you want both language and design appreciation, use a dual-track method:

- Track A: phonetic reading drills (10 minutes/day)
- Track B: signage + packaging observation (real-world scanning)
- Track C: short writing practice in block composition

This method is much more effective than memorization-only approaches.

## FAQ
### Is Hangeul really easier than other writing systems?
For many learners, yes. The phonetic consistency and modular structure reduce early-stage confusion.

### How long does it take to read basic Hangeul?
With focused daily practice, many beginners can decode basic text within a few days to a few weeks.

### Why do designers love using Hangeul in branding?
Because it offers geometric clarity and cultural uniqueness without sacrificing legibility.

### Do I need Korean grammar first to start?
No. Start with character recognition and pronunciation, then layer grammar gradually.
