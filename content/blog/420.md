**Book a flight to Korea today and roughly half the airline names in your search results are scheduled to stop existing.**

That is not a figure of speech. **Asiana Airlines is absorbed into Korean Air on 17 December 2026**, ending a 38-year-old brand. **T'way Air has already been renamed** — it is Trinity Airways as of this year, same aircraft, same routes, different name on the ticket. **Air Busan and Air Seoul disappear into Jin Air in March 2027.** And a carrier most travellers have never heard of, Parata Air, only started flying in September 2025.

Korean aviation is going through the biggest restructuring in its history, and it is happening right now, in the middle of your booking window. This guide is the map: who each airline actually is, what happens to the ones that are vanishing, and which one to book depending on what you care about.

## Tier 1: The Full-Service Carrier — Now Just One

For 38 years Korea had two full-service airlines. From December, it has one.

**Korean Air (대한항공)** absorbs **Asiana Airlines (아시아나항공)** on **17 December 2026**. The combined airline serves **more than 120 cities with over 230 aircraft**, which puts it in the same weight class as the major European and American network carriers.

If you are holding an Asiana booking or Asiana miles, three dates matter:

- **1 December 2026** — the last day to book Star Alliance award tickets on Asiana. Partner programmes such as Aeroplan, United MileagePlus and Singapore KrisFlyer stop displaying Asiana award seats about 16 days before the merger.
- **17 December 2026** — Asiana leaves **Star Alliance** and the brand ends. Korean Air is **SkyTeam**, and that is where the network goes.
- **Conversion rate**: Asiana Club miles fold into Korean Air's SKYPASS. **Miles earned from Asiana flights convert 1:1. Miles earned through partners convert at 1:0.82** — an 18% haircut that has not been widely reported in English.

That last detail is the single most valuable thing in this article for anyone with a Korean mileage balance. If your Asiana miles came from a credit card or a partner airline rather than from flying Asiana itself, they are worth less after December than before.

**Practical booking advice:** flights operate normally through the transition, and a ticket on Asiana today is a ticket on a functioning airline. But if you are choosing between the two for a flight *after* December, book Korean Air — you avoid a re-accommodation and your miles land in the right programme.

## Tier 2: The Hybrid — Air Premia

This is the airline most worth knowing about, because it occupies a category that barely exists elsewhere and is genuinely useful to a long-haul traveller.

**Air Premia (에어프레미아)** calls itself a **Hybrid Service Carrier** — priced near a budget airline, configured like a better one. It flies **Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners only**, and its pitch is literally its pitch:

- Economy is branded **"Economy 35"** — about **35 inches of seat pitch**, in a 3-3-3 layout with roughly 253 economy seats. Standard long-haul economy is 31–32 inches.
- Premium economy runs about **42 inches**.
- There is no first or business class. The whole fleet is one aircraft type in one configuration, which is how the economics work.

Its US network runs to **Los Angeles, New York (Newark), San Francisco, Honolulu and Washington DC**. For a traveller crossing the Pacific in economy, three to four extra inches of legroom for eleven hours is not a small thing, and it is the reason aviation reviewers keep writing about this airline.

**The honest caveat**: Air Premia is small and its ownership has been contested. AP Holdings holds about 46% with allied shareholders, JC Partners about 22%. Daemyung Sono Group bought into it, then sold its 22% stake to Tirebank in May 2025 to concentrate on T'way instead. A small airline with a shifting shareholder register is a different risk profile from Korean Air — book it for the seat, not for the certainty.

## Tier 3: The Budget Carriers, Mid-Reshuffle

Korea has an unusually crowded low-cost sector, and it is being consolidated into two big blocks plus stragglers.

### Jeju Air (제주항공) — the incumbent

Korea's largest LCC by passengers, and currently the healthiest.

- **42 aircraft**, average age 11.8 years and falling
- **3,311,358 passengers in Q1 2026**, up 24.2% year on year — first among Korean LCCs
- **91.9% load factor** against an industry average of 88.8%
- Two consecutive profitable quarters; Q1 2026 revenue ₩498.2 billion, operating profit ₩64.4 billion
- Modernising on the **Boeing 737-8**: 12 delivered, more due this year — though it cut its firm order from 40 to 32

For most short-haul trips within Asia, Jeju Air is the default LCC answer: most routes, most frequencies, best current operating numbers.

### Trinity Airways (트리니티항공) — this is T'way, renamed

**If you remember one thing from this article, make it this one.** T'way Air no longer exists as a name.

Daemyung Sono Group took control and renamed the airline **Trinity Airways (트리니티항공)**. The name was registered on **1 April 2026** and the brand formally launched on **6 August 2026** with new uniforms and a strategy the company calls **SSC — "Selective Service Carrier"**, meaning it unbundles and sells only the services passengers actually choose, tied into the group's hotel and resort business.

It is Korea's second-largest LCC with **48 aircraft**, and it is the only Korean budget airline flying long-haul to Europe — Paris, Rome, Barcelona, Frankfurt, Zagreb — routes it originally received as remedies for the Korean Air–Asiana merger's competition problems.

**But the European network is contracting, and this is the part to check before booking.** Despite load factors reported at 80–90%, Frankfurt bookings have been closed, Paris has gone from five weekly flights to roughly two or three, and Rome and Barcelona have been cut similarly. High oil prices and long-haul losses are the stated cause. **Verify your specific route is still operating before you plan around it.**

### Jin Air (진에어) — about to become the biggest

Hanjin Group's LCC, and the vehicle for the other half of the consolidation.

On **21 August 2026**, Jin Air, **Air Busan (에어부산)** and **Air Seoul (에어서울)** signed a merger agreement. Extraordinary shareholder meetings are set for December 2026, and the combined airline launches on **17 March 2027**.

The arithmetic: Jin Air's 32 aircraft plus Air Busan's 21 plus Air Seoul's 6 equals **59** — overtaking Trinity's 48 to become Korea's largest low-cost carrier by fleet.

**What this means for you**: if you are booking travel into 2027, Air Busan and Air Seoul are names with an expiry date. Air Busan matters particularly to anyone flying into **Busan (Gimhae)** rather than Seoul, since it is the carrier built around that hub.

### The smaller ones

- **Eastar Jet (이스타항공)** — rebuilt after bankruptcy and rehabilitation, targeting 27 aircraft across 25 airports in six countries.
- **Aero K (에어로케이)** — based at **Cheongju**, useful only if that airport suits you.
- **Parata Air (파라타항공)** — the newest and strangest. It is the former Fly Gangwon, bought in 2024 by the **air purifier manufacturer Winix** and relaunched on 30 September 2025. It is running a handful of A320s with long-haul ambitions. Korean financial press reports it back in complete capital erosion despite about ₩115 billion in support from its parent. EpicKor's [Winix company deep dive](/business/winix-air-purifier-company-deep-dive) covers how an appliance company ended up owning an airline. **Treat it as a startup carrier, not an established one.**

## What Actually Changes For A Traveller

Most of this restructuring is invisible from a passenger seat. Four things are not.

**1. Fewer full-service options means less price competition on long-haul.** Two full-service carriers competing on the same routes kept fares honest. One does not. This is exactly why regulators forced the remedy routes that Trinity now flies — and why Trinity cutting those routes matters beyond Trinity.

**2. Terminal logistics changed at Incheon.** Asiana moved to **Terminal 2** on 14 January 2026, joining Korean Air, Jin Air, Air Seoul and Air Busan. Thirteen airlines now use T2, including Delta, Air France, KLM and China Airlines. If your last trip had you at Terminal 1 for an Asiana flight, that is out of date. Check your terminal — the two are a shuttle ride apart.

**3. Names on your booking may not match anything you can look up.** A ticket issued as T'way, an aircraft painted Trinity, and a search engine that knows neither is a genuinely confusing experience. The same will be true of Air Busan and Air Seoul from March 2027.

**4. Miles are the one thing with a deadline.** Everything else in this article is inconvenience. The Asiana mileage conversion is money.

## What To Verify Before You Book

Airline restructuring moves faster than travel guides, this one included.

- **Confirm the operating carrier**, not just the marketing name. A codeshare booked as one airline may be flown by another, and during a merger that gap widens.
- **Confirm your Incheon terminal** on the day. T1 and T2 are separate buildings.
- **Confirm European routes are still scheduled** if you are flying Trinity. This is the live one.
- **Convert or use Asiana partner miles before December** if the 1:0.82 rate would cost you meaningfully.
- **Treat 2027 schedules as provisional** for Air Busan, Air Seoul and Parata Air.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Asiana Airlines safe to book right now?**

Yes. It is operating normally and will keep flying until the merger completes on 17 December 2026, after which the flights continue as Korean Air. The real considerations are mileage conversion and alliance change, not operations.

**Q: What happened to T'way Air?**

It was renamed. Daemyung Sono Group took control and the airline became **Trinity Airways (트리니티항공)** — registered 1 April 2026, brand launched 6 August 2026. Same aircraft, same fleet of 48, new name and a new "Selective Service Carrier" model. Its European routes are being reduced.

**Q: Which Korean airline has the most legroom in economy?**

Air Premia, by a clear margin. Its "Economy 35" cabin is about 35 inches of pitch against the 31–32 inches typical of long-haul economy, and its premium economy is around 42 inches. It flies 787-9s only.

**Q: What will happen to my Asiana miles?**

They convert into Korean Air SKYPASS. Miles earned from flying Asiana convert 1:1; miles earned through partners convert at 1:0.82. The final details were still being reviewed by Korean regulators as of August 2026, so confirm the current terms before acting.

**Q: Which is Korea's biggest low-cost airline?**

Jeju Air by passengers carried, with 42 aircraft and over 3.3 million passengers in Q1 2026. By fleet size, Trinity Airways leads with 48 — until March 2027, when the merged Jin Air, Air Busan and Air Seoul reach 59 aircraft.

**Q: Do all Korean airlines fly from Incheon Terminal 2?**

No. Thirteen airlines use T2 — including Korean Air, Asiana (since January 2026), Jin Air, Air Seoul, Air Busan, Delta, Air France and KLM. Jeju Air, Trinity, Eastar and most foreign carriers use Terminal 1. Always check, because the terminals are separate buildings.

## The Bottom Line

Korea's airline map in 2026 is a photograph of a country mid-consolidation: one full-service carrier where there were two, two large LCC blocks forming out of six, one genuinely interesting hybrid, and a couple of small carriers whose futures are open questions.

For the traveller, the practical version is short. **Korean Air** if you want the network and the miles. **Air Premia** if you want to arrive from across the Pacific able to walk. **Jeju Air** for everything short-haul. And check the name on your ticket still exists by the time you fly.
