**구운감자** — *gooun gamja*, "baked potato" — is a box of long, very thin, very crisp potato sticks that Haitai has sold in Korea since **1997**. It costs about a thousand won, it snaps rather than crunches, and the whole proposition is in the first word: it was **baked, not deep-fried.**

Koreans search it accordingly. The autocomplete branches around this product are calories, ingredients, and diet — people are trying to work out whether "baked" means what they hope it means.

So this article does the thing nobody has done in English: **reads the label.**

## Korea Built a Product Line Out of a Cooking Method

![The Haitai Gooun Gamja pack, a tall yellow carton printed with 구운감자 and the Haitai logo.](/assets/images/posts/415/gooun-gamja-pack.jpg)
*해태 구운감자 in its own packaging. The box copy makes two claims about texture — 똑! 소리나는 고소함 and 딱! 부러지는 깔끔함 — before it says anything about potato. Image: Haitai product listing.*

![Chart of Haitai's 구운 product family from 구운감자 in 1997 through 구운감자 슬림 in 2017, 구운고구마 in 2019, Texas hot barbecue flavour in April 2022, 구운옥수수 in 2022, hot grilled BBQ in 2023, 구운두유 black bean in 2023 and chipotle taco in 2024.](/assets/images/posts/415/gooun-gamja-product-line.jpg)
*EpicKor chart.*

Most snack brands are named after a thing. This one is named after a **verb**, and Haitai has spent nearly three decades finding out how far that goes.

**구운감자** arrived in 1997, first as a cracker resembling Haitai's own Ace, then as the thin stick in a tall box that everyone knows now. **구운감자 슬림** followed in **2017** — round, thin, closer to a potato chip than a stick.

Then the noun started moving. **구운고구마** (baked sweet potato) in **2019**. **구운옥수수** (baked corn) in **2022**. And in **2023**, **구운두유 까만콩맛** — *baked soy milk, black bean flavour* — at which point the family is no longer held together by a vegetable at all.

Meanwhile the original picked up flavours: **텍사스 핫 바베큐맛** in April 2022, **핫그릴드BBQ맛** in 2023, **치폴레타코맛** in 2024.

**The only thing all eight reliably share is the claim that they were not deep-fried.** 구운 stopped describing the snack and started naming the family.

## What "Baked" Actually Buys You

![Chart of the Korean label for 구운감자 27 g showing 32.6% potato starch and 5.2% dried potato powder, alongside the declared fats — mixed palm oil and margarine containing palm oil, processed fat, palm olein, rapeseed oil and processed butter — with 130 kcal per 27 g bag.](/assets/images/posts/415/gooun-gamja-label.jpg)
*EpicKor chart, from the Korean product label.*

**Haitai prints the two key numbers on the front of the box**, next to the product name, which is unusual and to the company's credit. Here is the fuller declaration, in order.

**감자전분 32.6%** — potato starch. **건조감자분말 5.2%** — dried potato powder. Then a potato-starch blend (German and Korean starch), a processed-starch preparation from Thailand (tapioca starch, sugar, refined salt), **혼합식용유** — mixed cooking oil, being palm olein and palm oil from Malaysia with rosemary oleoresin — more dried potato powder (American), a grain preparation of wheat flour, glutinous rice and starch, and **마가린**.

That margarine is itself palm oil, processed fat, palm olein, rapeseed oil and processed butter.

Two things follow, and they matter in opposite directions.

**First: there is no sliced potato in it.** The potato arrives as starch and dehydrated powder, which are combined into a dough and baked. That makes this a reconstituted snack, structurally more like a Pringle than a kettle chip — and it is why the sticks are so uniform and snap so cleanly.

**Second: not frying it did not remove the fat.** In a deep-fried crisp, oil is absorbed during cooking. Here it was **mixed into the dough beforehand**, along with margarine. The bag runs **130 kcal for 27 g**, about **480 kcal per 100 g** — lower than a typical fried potato chip, but not by the margin the name suggests.

**"구운" is a process claim, and it is true.** It is not a health claim, and the label never says it is.

## Why the Snap Matters More Than the Flavour

Haitai's own slogan for this product is two onomatopoeic lines and a name:
**뚝! 소리 나는 고소함 / 딱! 부러지는 깔끔함 / 구운감자.** Roughly: *nuttiness that goes* **뚝**,
*cleanness that snaps* **딱**.

That is unusual copy. Most snack advertising sells flavour. This one sells two sounds.

It is also accurate about what the product is for. The stick is very thin and very dry, so it
breaks in a single clean fracture rather than crumbling — and because there is no seasoning
powder and no oily surface film, **it does not leave anything on your fingers.** That is a
specific and underrated property. It is why the box lives in office drawers, why it goes into
children's snack boxes, and why Korean reviewers describe it as 담백 — plain in a way that is
meant as praise rather than a complaint.

The trade-off is that it is genuinely quiet in flavour. If you are coming from a heavily
seasoned chip, the first stick will read as underwhelming. That is the design, and it is why
Haitai eventually added barbecue and chipotle versions for people who wanted the opposite.

## The Pepero Day Loophole

Every 11 November, Korea observes **빼빼로 데이** — Pepero Day, named for Lotte's thin chocolate-coated biscuit sticks, on the date whose digits look like four sticks standing in a row. People give each other boxes of Pepero. It is the single largest confectionery day in the Korean calendar and it belongs entirely to one company's product.

구운감자 is also a long thin stick in a tall box.

So a small counter-tradition exists: **buying 구운감자 instead of Pepero on Pepero Day.** Sometimes it is a joke about not wanting to enrich Lotte, sometimes it is just for people who do not want chocolate, and sometimes it is a genuine preference — the snap of a baked potato stick is a different pleasure from a soft biscuit under chocolate.

If you are in Korea in early November and you want to take part in the ritual without buying the obvious thing, this is the local move.

## What to Buy, and What It Costs

**The small box is around ₩1,000 at a convenience store**, in 24 g and 27 g sizes. That is cheap even by Korean snack standards and is part of why it is a fixture in school bags and office drawers.

**슬림 is the one to get if you want a chip.** The 2017 round-cracker version behaves like a potato crisp — flat, wide, snappable — while the original is a stick you eat like a breadstick. They taste similar and feel completely different.

**Bulk exists and is popular with institutions.** 240 g and 405 g multipacks, and 24-count and 36-count boxes, are sold specifically into daycares, schools, after-school programmes and office pantries. If a Korean office has one snack in the cupboard, there is a decent chance it is this.

**The barbecue and chipotle versions are seasoning-powder variants** on the same stick, not different products. Try the plain one first — the whole point of it is being 담백, plain and clean-tasting, which the flavoured versions deliberately give up.

## Honest Notes

**The name will mislead an English speaker more than a Korean one.** 구운감자 in ordinary Korean means an actual baked potato, the vegetable. Nobody in Korea is confused about which one is in the box. Translated onto an export label as "Baked Potato Snack," it reads more like a claim than it does in Korean.

**It contains wheat, milk and soy.** The declared allergens are 밀, 우유 and 대두 — worth knowing, because "potato snack" does not imply any of the three, and the wheat is in there as a structural ingredient.

**It is not a low-calorie food.** About 480 kcal per 100 g. A 27 g bag at 130 kcal is a reasonable single portion; a 240 g bulk bag is not a single portion, and it is very easy to treat it as one.

**English-language search will fight you.** "Korean baked potato snack" drifts toward sweet potato products, which are a separate and much better-known Korean category. If you want this specific thing, search **Haitai Baked Potato** or 구운감자.

**The sister products are genuinely different snacks.** 구운고구마 and 구운옥수수 share the process and the branding, not the recipe. Liking one tells you very little about the others.

## FAQ

**Q: Is 구운감자 actually baked?**
**A:** Yes — it is not deep-fried, which is the product's founding claim. But oil and margarine are mixed into the dough before baking, so it is not low in fat. About 480 kcal per 100 g.

**Q: How much real potato is in it?**
**A:** The label declares 32.6% potato starch and 5.2% dried potato powder, plus a further potato-starch blend. There is no sliced potato — it is a dough, baked.

**Q: What does it taste like?**
**A:** 고소하고 담백 — nutty and plain, without a strong seasoning. The selling point is texture: it snaps hard and cleanly, which is what the slogan is about.

**Q: What is 구운감자 슬림?**
**A:** A 2017 version in thin round crackers rather than sticks. Same idea, chip-like format.

**Q: Why do Koreans buy it on Pepero Day?**
**A:** Because it is also a long thin stick in a tall box, so it works as a substitute for people who do not want chocolate or do not want to buy the Lotte product.
