Spend a year in Korea and you will accumulate towels you did not buy. A wedding, a first birthday, a shop opening, a company anniversary — each one hands you a towel, often with a name and a date printed on it. Nobody explains why.

Spend ten minutes on a Korean towel site and you hit the second thing nobody explains. Every product is listed as something like **최고급 40수 / 180g / 40×80cm**. Three numbers, no GSM, no "600 thread count", none of the vocabulary an English-language towel listing uses.

Meanwhile the *same brand's* towels on Amazon are sold as **"470 GSM, 20×40 inch"**.

**Both descriptions are of the same object.** This article translates between them, gives the current Korean prices, and explains the gift thing.

Korean towel listings lead with **수**, pronounced *su*. It is yarn count: **how fine the thread is.**

- **20수** — thick, coarse yarn. Cheap, hard-wearing, the kind used for gym and workshop towels.
- **30수** — the everyday middle.
- **40수** — finer thread. Softer hand, denser pile for the same weight, and what the "hotel" lines use.

Higher 수 does **not** mean thicker. It means *finer thread*, which is the opposite of what most English speakers assume when they see a bigger number. **Thickness is the second number.**

That second number is the **gram weight of one towel**. Songwol's own site sorts by it and labels the bands in plain Korean:

- **130–150 g** — 보통 두께, normal
- **160–180 g** — 도톰 두께, thick
- **190 g and above** — 고중량, heavyweight

The third number is the size, and for Korea it is usually **40 × 80 cm**. That is not a bath sheet. It is a face-and-body towel, and Korean bathrooms genuinely run on it — you dry off with something the size of what many countries would call a hand towel.

## Converting to GSM, Which Takes One Division

![Chart converting six Songwol towels from the Korean 수 and gram system to GSM, showing prices from 4,500 to 9,700 won and GSM values from 500 to 611.](/assets/images/posts/425/korean-towel-gsm-conversion.jpg)
*EpicKor chart, from Songwol's official mall, 25 August 2026.*

Here is the trap. **The gram figure on a Korean towel is the weight of that one towel.** GSM is grams *per square metre*. They are not the same number and they are not close.

To convert, divide the weight by the towel's area in square metres.

A **40 × 80 cm** towel is 0.4 × 0.8 = **0.32 m²**. So a 180 g towel at that size is 180 ÷ 0.32 = **563 GSM**.

Run that across Songwol's current hotel line and Korean towels land roughly between **500 and 611 GSM** — which, in the vocabulary of English-language towel shopping, is squarely in "luxury hotel" territory.

**This is why the conversion matters commercially.** Songwol's own Amazon listing abroad is sold as **470 GSM at 20 × 40 inch**. Twenty by forty inches is 50.8 × 101.6 cm, which is the same size as the 스퀘어50 sold in Korea at **250 g, i.e. 500 GSM**. The export set is a little lighter than the domestic one at the same size. Not dramatically — but if you assumed the Amazon listing was the identical product, it is worth knowing that the number moved.

## Why Weddings Hand Out Towels

![Four striped Songwol Hotel Collection towels laid in an overlapping fan, each with a small black woven HOTEL COLLECTION PREMIUM label at the corner.](/assets/images/posts/425/songwol-hotel-collection-labels.jpg)
*The Hotel Collection stripes, with the woven corner label. Around ₩9,700 for the 50×100 size. Image: Songwol official site.*

Korean celebrations run on a two-way exchange. Guests bring money in an envelope; hosts give something back. That return gift is **답례품**, and towels have been its default for decades.

The occasions are specific, and Korean search behaviour maps them exactly — the autocomplete branches under 수건 답례품 are **결혼** (weddings), **돌** (a baby's first birthday), and **칠순** (a seventieth birthday).

Why towels won this job comes down to four properties, none of them glamorous:

- **Everyone uses them and nobody has too many.** Unlike food or ornaments, a towel never has to be politely stored.
- **They print.** Songwol runs a 주문제직 custom-weaving service, and the standard is to weave or embroider the family name and the date. Reports on the service put the embroidery limit at around **20 characters**, Korean or English.
- **They stack.** Two hundred identical towels transport and hand out cleanly.
- **The price is legible.** A ₩5,000 towel signals the right amount of effort. Nobody is embarrassed on either side.

The consequence is that Korean households run a rotating stock of towels commemorating other people's events, and the printed date is how you know how old the towel is. **If you are handed one, that is what the writing says: whose event, and when.**

## How to Buy One Without Guessing

![Four folded Songwol towels stacked — cornflower blue, cream, grey and dark brown — each with a woven decorative border band.](/assets/images/posts/425/songwol-solid-towel-stack.jpg)
*The plain hotel line, 40수 and 30수, ₩4,500–₩6,200 depending on weight. Image: Songwol official site.*

The short version, for a shop in Korea or a Korean site:

- **Start at 40수, 180 g, 40 × 80 cm.** That is roughly 563 GSM, costs about **₩5,100**, and is the honest middle of the range.
- **Go up in grams, not in 수, if you want it plusher.** 190 g and above is the 고중량 band.
- **Only go up in size if you actually want a bigger towel.** 50 × 100 cm nearly doubles the price and does not improve the weave.
- **코마사 means combed cotton.** It is a real quality marker and appears in the product name when it applies.
- **뱀부얀 is the bamboo-yarn line**, a separate branch from the cotton grades, not a higher tier of it.
- **Ignore the list price.** Compare selling prices.

Where to buy: Songwol's own mall ships within Korea, Daiso carries cheap towels at its own price points, and department store basements and 생활용품 shops stock the branded lines. Abroad, the hotel line is on Amazon under the same brand name.

## The Honest Part

Three things this article is not claiming.

**Korean towels are not magically better.** They are well made, densely woven, and cheap for what they are. The 500–611 GSM range is genuinely good, but it is not unique to Korea — it is what any well-made cotton towel weighs.

**The small size is a real trade-off.** A 40 × 80 cm towel is not a bath sheet, and if you are used to wrapping yourself in one after a shower, the Korean standard will annoy you. Buy the 50 × 100 if that matters, and accept the price.

**And the cheap ones are fine.** Daiso sells towels for a fraction of these prices and they dry you perfectly well. The difference shows up over years of washing, in how quickly the pile flattens — which is a real difference, and also one you will not notice for eighteen months.

## FAQ

**Q: What does 수 mean on a Korean towel?**
**A:** It is yarn count — how fine the thread is. 20수 is coarse, 30수 is the everyday middle, and 40수 is the fine thread used in hotel lines. A higher number means a finer thread, not a thicker towel. Thickness is set by the gram weight printed next to it.

**Q: How do I convert a Korean towel's grams to GSM?**
**A:** Divide the weight by the towel's area in square metres. A 40 × 80 cm towel is 0.32 m², so a 180 g towel is about 563 GSM. Korean hotel-line towels generally land between 500 and 611 GSM.

**Q: How much does a good Korean towel cost?**
**A:** About ₩4,500 to ₩6,500 on the maker's own mall as of August 2026. Above that you are usually paying for a larger size rather than a better weave — the 50 × 100 cm items run to about ₩9,700.

**Q: Why did someone give me a towel at a Korean wedding?**
**A:** It is a 답례품, the return gift Korean hosts give guests who bring a money envelope. Towels are the default for weddings, first birthdays and seventieth birthdays, and are usually woven or embroidered with the family name and the date.

**Q: Are Korean towels smaller than Western ones?**
**A:** Yes. The Korean standard is 40 × 80 cm, closer to what many countries would call a hand towel. Larger 50 × 100 cm options exist and cost roughly double.

**Q: What is 코마사?**
**A:** Combed cotton — the yarn has been combed to remove short fibres before spinning, which makes it smoother and less prone to shedding. Songwol names it in the product title when it applies, so it is a spec rather than marketing language.

## Final Take

Korea did not invent a better towel. It built a **different vocabulary** for buying one, and that vocabulary is more informative than the English one: yarn fineness and actual weight, rather than a thread count that means little and a GSM figure most shoppers cannot picture.

Once you can read **40수 / 180g / 40×80**, you can price any towel in Korea in about two seconds — and you can check whether the export version on Amazon is the same cloth.

As for the drawer full of towels with strangers' names on them: that is not clutter. **It is a record of every event you were invited to.**
