Order 떡볶이 at a Korean spicy-food shop and something arrives that you did not ask for and cannot identify. It is pale, slightly cloudy, sweet, and served cold in a cup or a squat bottle. Nobody explains it. Everyone at the next table is drinking one.

That is **쿨피스** (*Coolpis*), and it has been the standard-issue fire extinguisher on Korean tables since 1980. It is not lemonade, it is not yogurt, and — this is the part almost every English list gets wrong — **it is not actually neutralising the chilli.** Understanding why is the difference between drinking it correctly and wondering what the point was.

**If you only remember one line: it works, but not the way you think, and milk would work better if most Koreans could drink it.**

## Korea's First Lactic-Acid Drink, and Not Quite a Lactic-Acid Drink

쿨피스 launched in **1980** under 해태유업 and is routinely described in Korean coverage as the country's first 유산균 음료 — lactic-acid bacteria drink. 동원F&B bought the brand in **2006** and still makes it, which makes 2026 its **46th year on the shelf**.

The classification is looser than the nickname suggests. 쿨피스 is a **과일향 음료** — a fruit-flavoured beverage — that contains **유산균 배양액**, a lactic-acid culture, rather than being a fermented milk drink in the way [Yakult is](/blog/362). That distinction matters for what it can and cannot do to your mouth, and we will come back to it.

The formulation has moved with the times. It was originally sugar and aspartame; the current product is sweetened with **stevia and sucralose**.

## Why It Is on Every Spicy Table

The commercial logic is simpler than the culinary one.

Korean spicy-food restaurants — 떡볶이 shops, 엽기떡볶이 branches, 찜닭 places, fried-chicken joints — need something to hand a customer whose mouth is on fire, and they need it to be cheap, shelf-stable and safe to leave in a jug. 쿨피스 is all three. It is concentrated, so a small amount of syrup makes a lot of drink, and it keeps far better in a warm dining room than milk does.

Korean coverage now frames the brand explicitly as **"K-매운맛의 영원한 단짝"** — the eternal partner of Korean spicy food — and it has ridden the last decade of escalating heat, the 엽떡 era in particular, the way a shovel manufacturer rides a gold rush.

## The Honest Part: It Is Not Neutralising the Chilli

Here is the thing nobody tells visitors, and it is worth knowing because it changes what you order.

**Capsaicin is fat-soluble.** It does not dissolve in water, which is why drinking water after something spicy spreads the burn around rather than stopping it. What actually removes capsaicin from your tongue is **casein**, the protein in dairy: it binds the capsaicin molecule and carries it away, and the fat in whole milk coats the mouth on top of that. This is also why **skim milk barely works** — take the fat out and you lose most of the effect.

쿨피스 contains a lactic-acid culture, but it is not a fatty dairy drink. What it has in quantity is **sweetness**, and concentrated sweetness is genuinely effective at *masking* heat — Korean writing about the drink says exactly this, that its dense sweetness is good at "catching" the spicy taste. Masking and removing are different mechanisms with different results: masking works while you are drinking and fades quickly, which is why you keep refilling the cup.

So why does the restaurant not just put out milk, which would work better?

**Because most Korean adults cannot drink it.** Lactose intolerance is very common in Korea, and handing a table of six a jug of milk with their 엽떡 would send some of them home unwell. 쿨피스 is the drink that solves the restaurant's problem — cheap, stable, safe for everyone, and sweet enough to feel like relief — rather than the drink that solves the chemistry.

That is not a criticism. It is the actual reason it is on the table, and it is more interesting than "Korean sweet yogurt drink."

## The Flavours, and the One That Took Fifteen Years

Three flavours carry the brand, and the ranking is not close.

**복숭아 — peach.** The original and, by consensus, the one people mean when they say 쿨피스. If a restaurant stocks one flavour, this is it.

**자두 — plum.** Sharper, more sour, a real second place rather than a novelty.

**파인애플 — pineapple.** The tropical option, and the one most likely to turn up in a convenience-store frozen cup.

Then in **2026, banana** — and the interesting part is the gap. It is the brand's first new flavour since **2011**, a fifteen-year pause, and 동원F&B launched it explicitly on the back of the global K-spicy wave rather than as an ordinary line extension.

## Why 2026 Was the Year It Got a New Flavour

A fifteen-year gap between flavours is not caution. It is a brand that had settled into being furniture — the thing on the table, unremarked, like the water jug — and then found itself standing next to a global story.

The story is Korean spice going worldwide. Buldak carried it first, and behind it came an entire export category built on how hot Korean food can be. 쿨피스 sits in a peculiar position inside that: it is not the spicy product, it is **the thing you need because of the spicy product**, and Korean industry coverage in 2026 started describing the brand in exactly those terms — the partner to K-매운맛 rather than a drink in its own right.

That is a genuinely good place to be. Every bowl of 엽떡 sold abroad is a person discovering, usually too late, that water does not help. The brand does not have to win a beverage category; it has to be the answer to a question the food is already asking.

Hence banana in 2026, the first new flavour since 2011. It is a mild, creamy, low-acid flavour — which is a deliberate choice for this job, because sharp and sour reads as *more* stimulation to a mouth that is already overstimulated. Peach and plum both have acidity. Banana does not.

Whether it displaces peach is doubtful; 46 years of muscle memory is a lot to move. But it tells you how the company reads its own product: not as a juice competing with juices, but as equipment.

## Where You Actually Meet It

Three formats, three completely different experiences.

**The restaurant jug or cup.** Free or nearly free, poured from a dispenser, usually peach, usually watered to the shop's own taste. This is the default encounter and you do not order it — it appears.

**The convenience-store frozen cup.** A 200 ml cup sold from the freezer, which you let soften and then drink as slush. Genuinely good in August, and it is the version most visitors miss because it is not in the drinks fridge.

**The 450 ml bottle.** The one to buy if you are cooking something spicy at home. 동원's own online store lists the peach 450 ml at **₩550** as of August 2026, dropping to **₩490** for members — which tells you what the drink actually costs to make, and why restaurants can give it away.

Convenience stores charge more than that, as they do for everything, and 쿨피스 is a frequent 2+1 item there. If you are buying more than one bottle, the promotion cycle is worth waiting for; if you are buying a case, the manufacturer's own store is cheaper than any convenience store will be.

## Honest Notes Before You Buy It

**It is sweet, and that is the entire product.** If you dislike sweet drinks you will dislike this one, and no amount of cultural context will fix that. Order water and accept the burn.

**It is not a health drink.** The lactic-acid culture in the name does real work in the marketing and very little in your gut compared with an actual fermented milk drink. Buy it because it is pleasant with spicy food, not because it is doing something for you.

**Whole milk genuinely works better** if you can drink milk. If you are not lactose intolerant and you are eating at the extreme end of Korean spice — 엽떡 단계별 매운맛, buldak — a cold full-fat milk is the more effective choice and the shops near those restaurants sell it.

**It attracts insects.** A minor but real point Korean writing makes about leaving an open cup around: it is a sugar solution, and outdoors in summer it does what sugar solutions do.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Coolpis yogurt?**
**A:** No. It is a fruit-flavoured drink containing a lactic-acid culture — 유산균 배양액 — rather than a fermented milk drink. It is closer in category to a cordial than to [Yakult](/blog/362), which is a genuine liquid fermented milk.

**Q: Does it actually stop spicy food burning?**
**A:** It masks the heat with concentrated sweetness rather than removing capsaicin. Whole milk removes it, because casein binds the molecule and fat carries it off. Coolpis is what restaurants serve because it is cheap, keeps in a warm room, and does not trouble lactose-intolerant customers.

**Q: Is it free at restaurants?**
**A:** Usually included or nearly so at spicy-food shops, poured from a jug or dispenser. Convenience-store bottles are a normal paid product.

**Q: Which flavour should I try first?**
**A:** Peach. It is the original, it is what restaurants stock, and it is what Koreans mean by the word. Plum if you want it sharper, pineapple if you are buying the frozen cup.

**Q: Is it sugar-free?**
**A:** The current formulation is sweetened with stevia and sucralose rather than the sugar and aspartame it used decades ago. It still tastes emphatically sweet — that is the mechanism, not a flaw.
