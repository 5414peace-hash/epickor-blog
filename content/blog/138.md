A good Seoul safety guide should do two things at once: reassure you that most visits are straightforward and prepare you for the small number of situations where attention matters. Seoul has extensive public transit, busy streets late into the evening, tourist assistance services, and a nationwide police lost-property system. In January 2026, the former LOST112 service moved into the new Police Civil Service 24 portal. The city also has taxi overcharging complaints, persistent street recruiters, nightlife risks, and etiquette rules that visitors can misread.

The goal is not to make Seoul sound dangerous or perfectly safe. No large city deserves either description. The useful approach is to know what normal service looks like, keep records when something feels wrong, and use Korea's official help channels rather than arguing on the street.

## The Short Answer: Your Seoul Safety Setup

Before leaving the airport, save four numbers: police 112, fire and ambulance 119, the Korea Travel Helpline 1330, and the Seoul city information line 120. Install or bookmark a map app that works well in Korea, keep your accommodation address in Korean, and use an in-app taxi booking when possible.

Most everyday problems are not violent emergencies. They are misunderstandings about fares, a lost phone, an unwanted conversation, a missed last train, or a card that does not work. The table below shows the fastest first response.

## Seoul Taxi Scams: What Is Actually Against the Rules

The old warning to avoid every black taxi is wrong. Color does not tell you that a taxi is a scam. Seoul has regular sedans, deluxe taxis, large taxis, international taxis, and app-based premium services with different legitimate fare structures. Some deluxe or premium vehicles are black and cost more by design.

The official Seoul taxi guide states that ordinary taxis must charge by the meter unless an approved separate fare plan applies. In-app services may use predetermined or flexible pricing, and international taxis can offer approved airport section fares. A quoted flat fare is therefore not automatically illegal; the question is whether you booked a service that clearly disclosed it.

Red flags include a curbside driver soliciting you aggressively, refusing the meter without showing an approved fare, changing the agreed app price, or demanding cash while refusing a receipt. At an airport, use the official taxi stand or a confirmed app pickup point. Do not follow a person who approaches inside the terminal claiming to have a "special taxi."

![A Korea Tourist Police booth in central Seoul.](/assets/images/posts/138/korea-tourist-police-booth-seoul-pexels.jpg)

For a normal street taxi, check that the meter starts and that the route on your map generally makes sense. Seoul fares include legitimate late-night and out-of-city surcharges, so a higher total is not proof of fraud. Traffic also affects time-based meter charges. If you suspect overcharging, stay calm, pay in a traceable way if possible, request a receipt, and photograph the license plate after exiting safely.

The 1330 Korea Travel Helpline accepts tourist complaints by phone and provides real-time chat and interpretation options. Seoul has also continued targeted taxi enforcement in airport and tourist areas. A documented complaint is more effective than trying to win a roadside debate.

## Street Recruiters, Donations, and Unwanted Invitations

Visitors in Hongdae, Myeongdong, university districts, and palace areas sometimes report being approached by friendly pairs who ask about Korean culture, ancestry, personality, or "good energy." Some are ordinary students practicing English. Others are religious recruiters, salespeople, unofficial tour promoters, or people seeking donations.

You do not need to identify which category they belong to. The safety rule is based on the request, not the person's appearance. Do not follow a stranger to a second location, hand over your passport, withdraw cash, or participate in an unplanned ceremony. A firm "No, thank you" while continuing to walk is sufficient.

Persistent does not mean violent, but politeness can trap travelers into a long conversation. Korean social manners do not require you to keep engaging with an unknown person who ignores a refusal. Move into a convenience store, hotel lobby, subway information area, or police box if someone follows you.

Treat donation requests the same way you would at home. If a cause interests you, take the organization's official name and donate later through a verified channel. Do not rely on a clipboard, costume, or printed badge as proof.

## Subway Etiquette Is a Comfort Rule, Not a Silence Law

Seoul subways can feel unusually quiet, especially during commuting hours. That does not mean conversation is forbidden. The expectation is to keep calls, videos, music, and group conversation at a volume that does not take over the carriage. Seoul Metro has run public campaigns encouraging earphone use because speaker noise is a recurring passenger complaint.

Priority seats should remain available to people who need them, including older riders, disabled passengers, pregnant passengers, and adults with small children. Pink pregnancy seats have a specific purpose, and visitors should not treat an empty one as ordinary seating during a busy ride.

![Passengers wait behind platform screen doors at a Seoul subway station.](/assets/images/posts/138/seoul-subway-platform-gongdeok-pexels.jpg)
*Platform markings, screen doors, and orderly queues do much of the work; follow the local flow rather than stopping at the doorway. Photo by Theodore Nguyen via Pexels.*

On escalators, follow posted guidance rather than assuming a universal standing side. Let passengers exit before boarding, move away from the train doors once inside, and remove a large backpack on a crowded car. Eating a full meal on the subway is a poor fit even when no employee stops you.

## Nightlife, Drinks, and Getting Home

Seoul's nightlife districts can stay active late, but crowds do not eliminate risk. Watch your drink being prepared, do not accept an opened beverage from a stranger, and keep your group connected. If a venue changes the price or adds unexplained charges, ask for an itemized bill before paying.

The last subway time varies by station, direction, weekday, and service pattern. Do not build a plan around a generic "midnight" rule. Check the route on the day, and budget for a taxi if you will stay out late. Surge or late-night pricing can make that ride more expensive than expected.

Korea's legal and cultural environment around drugs is strict. Do not carry controlled medication without checking documentation requirements, and never accept unknown pills or substances. If you need prescription medicine on a trip, consult the relevant Korean embassy or customs guidance before flying.

Walking alone requires the same judgment as any major city. Use bright main streets, avoid isolated riverbanks or alleys when intoxicated, and share your live location with someone you trust. "Seoul is safe" should never be used to pressure yourself into ignoring discomfort.

## Lost Wallets and Phones: Korea's Current Recovery System

If you lose something, identify the last controlled location first: taxi, subway, cafe, airport, or street. Contact that operator directly when possible because items may remain in a local office before entering the national system.

For taxis, a card receipt can help identify the vehicle. For rail and subway, note the line, direction, boarding and exit stations, carriage number if known, and exact time. For a cafe, call the branch rather than only the national brand account.

The Korean National Police Agency now provides found-property search and loss reporting through Police Civil Service 24. The former LOST112 site displays a notice that the service moved on January 26, 2026. The police non-emergency contact 182 can also guide lost-property inquiries during its service hours. If the missing item contains bank cards, freeze them immediately; do not wait for the physical wallet to appear.

Create a small record before the trip: phone IMEI or serial number, passport copy stored securely, insurer contact, and photos of important items. Never keep the copy and original passport in the same bag.

## A Five-Minute Safety Checklist

Save your hotel's Korean address as a screenshot. Add 112, 119, and 1330 to your contacts. Enable device tracking and a strong screen lock. Carry one backup payment method separately. Download an offline copy of your insurance details. Tell a trusted person when you are going somewhere alone late at night.

When moving between Incheon Airport and the city, use official transport and verify the pickup point. ## FAQ

**Q: Is Seoul safe for solo travelers?**

Many solo travelers visit without serious problems, but safety varies by situation and no city is risk-free. Use normal precautions, especially around alcohol, late-night transport, and isolated areas.

**Q: Are black taxis in Seoul scams?**

No. Some legitimate deluxe and premium taxis are black and charge higher published rates. Verify the service type, meter, or disclosed in-app fare rather than judging by color.

**Q: What number should a tourist call for help in Korea?**

Call 112 for police emergencies, 119 for fire or ambulance, and 1330 for travel information, interpretation, or tourist complaints.

**Q: What should I do if a street recruiter will not leave me alone?**

Stop engaging, move into a staffed public place, and call 112 if you feel followed, threatened, or prevented from leaving.

**Q: Is talking on the Seoul subway illegal?**

No. Quiet conversation is normal. Loud calls, speaker audio, blocking doors, and occupying priority seats create the problems visitors should avoid.

**Q: Where do I search for lost property in Korea?**

Use the National Police Agency's Police Civil Service 24 portal and contact the relevant taxi, subway, railway, airport, or business directly with exact times and identifying details.
