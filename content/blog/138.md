## Taxi Scam Pattern: What Actually Happens
Most drivers are honest. The issue is a small minority in high-traffic tourist zones proposing flat, inflated fares.

Defense protocol:
1. use official ride-hailing apps,
2. confirm meter activation,
3. request receipt when fare feels unusual.

Documenting car ID and route details can resolve most disputes quickly.

## Subway Etiquette: Silence Is a Social Contract
Rush-hour Seoul subway culture prioritizes low-noise coexistence. Loud calls or speaker audio trigger instant social friction.

- keep voice low
- avoid speaker playback
- move backpacks in crowded cars

## Street Recruiters: Safe Exit Script
Common opening lines include "Can I ask about Korean culture?" or "You have good energy." They are usually persistent recruiters.

Best response sequence:
- do not stop walking,
- keep reply short,
- avoid further eye contact.

A calm, consistent exit prevents escalation.

## FAQ
### Is Seoul safe to walk at night?
Generally yes. Crime rates are low compared to many global capitals, but standard urban caution still applies.

### What if I lose my wallet?
Visit a nearby police station and check LOST112. Korea's lost-and-found recovery is strong.

### Are street recruiters dangerous?
Usually not physically dangerous, but they can be persistent and time-consuming.

### Should I avoid all black taxis?
No. Color alone is not a scam indicator. Focus on meter usage and booking transparency.

## Final Takeaway
Seoul travel success is about process, not luck. Use the prevention checklist, respect transit etiquette, and keep decisions simple when tired. That system protects both your budget and your travel energy.
