The most-awarded serum in Korean beauty is not a miracle ingredient story. It is a leaving-things-out story.

**토리든 (Torriden)** launched in 2015 with no celebrity model, no conglomerate behind it, and a formula philosophy Korean coverage sums up as 덜어내기 — *subtracting*: no fragrance, no alcohol, no essential oils, vegan-certified, and one job per bottle. The bottle that carried it, the **Dive-In Low-Molecular Hyaluronic Acid Serum (다이브인 저분자 히알루론산 세럼, 2019)**, has now sold **over 13 million units**, won Hwahae's moisture-serum award three years running, and taken **Olive Young's serum award four consecutive years** — then three category number-ones at once in the 2025 awards.

This is the third stop in our K-beauty product series, and it completes a triangle: [snail mucin](/blog/395) is the animal-derived hydrator, [heartleaf](/blog/396) is the plant-derived calmer, and Dive-In is the pure-chemistry option — water engineering, no organism involved. Here is what the chemistry actually does, what the awards do and do not mean, both countries' real prices, and who should not buy it.

## What "Low-Molecular 5D Hyaluronic Acid" Actually Means

Hyaluronic acid is a humectant — a molecule that binds water. The catch every chemist knows: **standard HA molecules are too large to sit anywhere but the very surface** of skin. Fine, but shallow.

Dive-In's pitch is size-sorting. The formula carries **five different molecular weights** of hyaluronic acid — from large film-formers that hold water at the surface down to low-molecular fragments small enough to settle into the upper layers of the stratum corneum. The result is layered hydration rather than a single wet film: the surface glow plus a longer-lasting plumpness underneath, supported by **panthenol and allantoin** for calming.

That is the whole trick, and the honest reading is that it is a *good* trick rather than a deep one. Multi-weight HA is real formulation science, and the texture — watery, instant-absorbing, zero tack — is why Korean reviewers made it the default first serum and makeup-prep layer. But hydration is hydration: it is comfort and appearance, renewed daily.

**What this bottle deliberately is not:** an exfoliant, a brightener, a retinoid, or anything that changes skin structure over time. Torriden's minimalism means the one-star reviews are, once again, mostly the sound of the wrong expectation — someone bought "the most-awarded serum in Korea" expecting treatment, and got excellent water management. If you want change, you need actives; if you want your actives to stop stinging, this is the layer that goes under them.

## The Company: Growth Without a Face

Most K-beauty megabrands buy their breakout — a celebrity face, a drama placement. Torriden's rise ran on a different engine, and Korean business coverage keeps pointing at it: **review infrastructure.**

The brand launched in **2015** and put the Dive-In serum out in **2019**, priced mid-range and formulated for the sensitive-skin checklists of Korean review platforms. On 화해 (Hwahae) — Korea's ingredient-scanning review app — it took the moisture-serum award **three years straight (2019–2021)**. Olive Young shelved it everywhere, and the awards compounded: **serum category winner four consecutive years (2021–2024)**, then the **2025 Olive Young Awards with first place in three categories at once** — serum, mask, and lip care. CJ's own retail coverage calls Torriden the model case of a brand built inside Olive Young's ecosystem — five years from shelf entry to mega-brand.

The receipts are corporate now: Korean reporting puts Torriden past **₩200 billion in annual revenue in 2025, up 45 percent in a year**, with the serum alone past 13 million bottles. All of it without a face on a bus ad — the product photograph *is* the brand, which is also why this article's images are just the bottle and the liquid.

August 2026, both sides — and this one has a twist:

At street prices the two markets are surprisingly close, which is rare in K-beauty; this is a mid-priced product everywhere. The buying note that matters more than price: the lineup is a family — serum, toner, cream, mask, sun serum all carry the Dive-In name — and bundles often price better per item than singles. The serum is the one the awards are about.

## How Koreans Actually Use It

**On damp skin, always.** A humectant needs water to grab. The Korean habit is toner (or just not-fully-dried skin) first, Dive-In pressed in immediately after.

**Under actives, as the buffer.** The most common Korean routine slot: Dive-In first, then the vitamin C or retinal that would otherwise sting. Hydrated skin tolerates actives better, and this texture layers under anything without pilling.

**The 7-skin variant.** Like the heartleaf toner, it suits the multiple-thin-layers habit — two or three passes on very dry days rather than one thick one.

**Sealed, or it backfires.** In dry air, a humectant with nothing over it can pull water outward. Cream on top is not optional in winter — the same physics we flagged for [snail mucin](/blog/395).

**Morning shortcut.** Because it doubles as makeup prep, plenty of Korean users run it as the entire morning routine between cleanser and sunscreen. That minimalism is on-brand — literally.

## The Dive-In Family, Decoded

The serum's success spawned a full line, and the names confuse buyers abroad. The map, briefly:

- **The serum (세럼)** — the awarded one. Start here; everything else is optional.
- **The toner (토너)** — the same HA logic in a thinner, splashable form. Redundant if you own the serum; useful if you prefer the 7-skin toner habit instead.
- **The soothing cream (수딩 크림)** — the seal for the serum's water. The most defensible second purchase, and why the duo bundle exists.
- **The mask (마스크팩)** — one of Torriden's three 2025 award categories; a single-use version of the serum for post-sun or pre-event days.
- **The sun serum (선세럼)** — a hybrid sunscreen with the Dive-In texture. Popular in Korea, but buy sunscreen on protection first, texture second — our [Korean sunscreen guide](/blog/160) covers how to judge that.

The honest ranking for a first order: serum alone, or serum plus cream if your climate is dry. The rest is enjoyment, not necessity.

## Who Should Skip It

- **Anyone expecting visible change.** Fine lines, spots, texture — those are active-ingredient jobs. This is maintenance, done excellently.
- **Anyone whose routine already has a humectant they like.** A second hydrating serum adds cost, not moisture. Snail, heartleaf, or Dive-In — pick the texture you enjoy; you do not need two.
- **The single-product budget.** The standing EpicKor answer: if you own one skincare product, make it sunscreen. A hydrator is the second purchase, not the first.
- **Fragrance lovers.** It smells like nothing, on purpose. If unscented reads as "clinical" to you, this line will feel joyless.

## FAQ

**Q: What does the Torriden Dive-In serum actually do?**

It hydrates in layers — five molecular weights of hyaluronic acid hold water from the surface down into the upper stratum corneum, with panthenol and allantoin calming alongside. It absorbs like water, preps makeup well, and does not exfoliate, brighten, or treat aging. Hydration is the entire job, by design.

**Q: Why is Torriden so popular in Korea?**

It won on review infrastructure instead of advertising: three straight Hwahae moisture awards from 2019, four consecutive Olive Young serum awards from 2021, and three category wins at once in 2025 — with over 13 million bottles sold and revenue past ₩200 billion in 2025. The fragrance-free, alcohol-free, vegan formula matched exactly what Korean review platforms score highly.

**Q: How much is the Dive-In serum in Korea?**

As of August 2026: ₩25,500 list, about ₩22,000 in standing Olive Young bundle promotions, and ₩16,900 on Torriden's own Korean mall — the manufacturer undercuts the retailer. In the US it lists at $22.50 with routine Amazon discounts. Street prices in the two countries are unusually close.

**Q: Torriden Dive-In vs snail mucin vs heartleaf toner — which one?**

They are the three routes to the same destination. Dive-In is pure chemistry: unscented, vegan, watery. Snail mucin adds barrier-comforting slip but is animal-derived. Heartleaf is the pick when redness, not dryness, is the complaint. Pick one by texture and ethics; stacking all three adds nothing but cost.

**Q: Is Torriden vegan and fragrance-free?**

Yes — the Dive-In line is vegan-certified, fragrance-free, alcohol-free and essential-oil-free, which is the brand's stated subtraction philosophy and a main reason sensitive-skin reviewers scored it highly enough to build the streak of awards.

**Q: Can I use Dive-In serum with retinol or vitamin C?**

That is its most common Korean use — as the hydrating buffer under actives. Apply Dive-In to damp skin first, let it absorb for a minute, then the active, then a cream to seal. Hydrated skin tolerates irritating ingredients better.
