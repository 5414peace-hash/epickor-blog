For ten years Korea has had two high-speed rail operators pretending to be one railway. If you have researched a Seoul-to-Busan trip, you have run into it: KTX from Seoul Station, SRT from a station called Suseo that no guidebook explains, two apps, two accounts, two sets of prices for the same 400 kilometres.

**That ends on 1 September 2026.** Korail and SR become one operator, the SRT is renamed **KTX-산천 (KTX-Sancheon)**, and KTX fares come down to the cheaper SRT level on the same day.

The reason this is worth your attention right now rather than in September is timing. Chuseok — Korea's biggest domestic travel week — falls on **24-26 September**, three weeks after the merger. The holiday seats are sold in a separate advance release, and the booking system they will be sold on is being replaced weeks before it happens.

## What actually changes, and what does not

Most coverage of this has been on business desks, where the story is a corporate combination approved by Korea's Fair Trade Commission on **2 August 2026**, putting the two operators back together twelve years after they were split. That is accurate and almost useless if you are a person trying to buy a train ticket.

Here is the traveller's version.

**Changes on 1 September:**

- The name on the train. **SRT becomes KTX-산천**, which is the name of a train type Korail already runs, so you will see KTX-Sancheon services from both Seoul Station and Suseo.
- The price of a KTX ticket, downward.
- Where you book. One app searches and sells both, including departures from Seoul and Suseo in the same results list.
- How much there is to book. Korail is adding roughly **116,000 high-speed seats per week** — about 16,000 a day. Weekday high-speed trips go from **379 to 402**, weekends from **431 to 457**.

**Does not change:**

- The tracks, the trains, or the journey times. Suseo-line services still run on the Suseo-Pyeongtaek high-speed line and still take about the same time to Busan.
- Which station you leave from. Seoul Station and Suseo remain two different places, roughly an hour apart on the metro, and picking the wrong one is still the single most expensive mistake on this trip.
- The fact that Korean holiday seats sell out in minutes.

## The fare cut lands all at once — this part gets reported wrong

There are two numbers circulating and they describe different things.

Korail's commitment to the FTC covers **three years**, during which KTX fares are held at the lower SRT level. Some coverage compressed that into "fares fall 10% over three years," which reads as though you would see roughly 3% now.

That is not how it works. **The reduction is applied on 1 September**, and Korail has published the actual figures:

<div class="table-scroll">

| Route | Before | From 1 September | Saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seoul - Busan | ₩59,800 | **₩54,400** | ₩5,400 |
| Yongsan - Gwangju Songjeong | ₩46,800 | **₩42,000** | ₩4,800 |
| Average across KTX routes | — | **about 10% lower** | — |

</div>

The three years is the *commitment window* — the period over which the lower level is guaranteed — not a phase-in schedule.

**Now the honest part: ₩5,400 is not a reason to move your trip.** It is roughly two convenience-store coffees. If your dates are fixed, book the seat you want and stop optimising. The saving matters if you are travelling several intercity legs, or if you are a family of four, where the same trip quietly returns about ₩21,600.

## Suseo is the station most visitors have never used, and it is about to matter more

If you have only ever booked KTX, you have probably never heard of **수서역 (Suseo Station)**. It sits in Gangnam-gu in southeast Seoul, on metro lines 3 and Suin-Bundang plus the GTX-A line, and it opened as SR's high-speed terminus at the end of 2016.

![The concourse of Suseo SRT station, seen from above an escalator. A large banner reads "수서역 SRT 개통을 축하합니다," departure boards and START SRT signage are visible, and travellers cross the polished floor below a steel-truss roof.](/assets/images/posts/380/suseo-station-hall.jpg)
*Suseo's concourse, with the banner celebrating the SRT's opening still up. That launch is what is now being folded back into Korail. Photo: Minseong Kim, Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0).*

Suseo's role grows with the merger. Korail expects seats on Suseo departures and arrivals to rise by about **30%**, and the KTX sets it is putting on the route carry up to **955 seats per train** — considerably more than the SRT sets they supplement.

**Whether that helps you depends entirely on where you are staying.** Suseo is convenient if your base is Gangnam, Jamsil, Songpa or Seongnam. It is a bad idea if you are in Myeongdong, Hongdae or near Seoul Station, because you would spend an hour crossing the city to save nothing.

Two smaller benefits arrive with the integration and neither has been reported in English: a mileage scheme worth **5% of the ticket payment**, and a **30% discount on the connecting conventional-train fare** for passengers transferring off a high-speed service. If your final destination is a small city that a KTX does not reach, that second one is real money.

## Do this before you book anything: the account migration

This is the part that will actually cost people seats, and it is invisible from the English-language internet.

Merging two railways means merging two membership databases, and Korail has been running a conversion process since **14 July 2026**. What you must do depends on which accounts you hold:

<div class="table-scroll">

| If you have | What happens | What you must do |
|---|---|---|
| **Korail account only** | Converted automatically | Nothing |
| **Both Korail and SR accounts** | Converted automatically | Consent to the data transfer in the SR app or site, **or you lose your SR booking history and discount coupons** |
| **SR account only** | Nothing happens on its own | **Register with Korail, then run the transfer yourself.** Skip this and your SR history and coupons are gone |
| **No account at all** | — | Create a Korail account now, not on the morning tickets go on sale |

</div>

The conversion is reachable through the existing Korail Talk and SR apps and sites. **Do it on a quiet weekday evening, not at 08:55 on a booking morning.** Korean holiday ticket sales are decided in the first three or four minutes, and an account you are still setting up is an account you are not booking with.

If you are visiting Korea for the first time and have never registered with either, this section is a non-issue — just make a Korail account in advance. **Log in and confirm it works before the day.**

## Chuseok is the stress test, and the dates are now official

**Chuseok 2026 is Friday 25 September**, with the public holiday running **Thursday 24 to Saturday 26**. There is no substitute holiday this year — Korea grants one when the holiday falls on a Sunday, and none of the three days does. In practice, though, Sunday 27 follows immediately, so the country is effectively shut for **four days**.

Holiday train tickets are not sold on the normal rolling schedule. They are pulled out and sold in a **separate advance release**, and popular departures are gone within minutes of the window opening.

**The widely-repeated dates were wrong, and the merger is why.** Korean travel blogs had been circulating 20 August for KTX and 25 August for SRT, matching the usual five-weeks-ahead pattern. Korail's official notice, posted **13 August 2026** (<a href="https://www.korail.com/ticket/guest/notice/25563" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">2026년 추석 연휴 승차권 예매 안내문</a>), put the sale much later and stretched the general window from three days to five: **priority passengers 3-4 September**, **everyone else 7-11 September**, covering travel on 23-27 September.

**And the open question about Suseo has been answered.** Historically, holiday tickets were the one category *not* cross-sold between the two operators online — SR's own Lunar New Year notice this year said exactly that. On the merged system there is no longer a separate SR sale at all. Instead, **Suseo departures get their own day inside the Korail sale: Thursday 10 September**, covering the Gyeongbu, Gyeongjeon, Donghae, Honam and Jeolla lines. If you were planning to catch what used to be an SRT, that is your morning, and it is one day *before* Seoul-Busan opens on the 11th.

Two details that decide whether the account work above was worth doing. The advance sale is **online and members-only** — Korail's notice says `역 창구 예매 없음`, no station-window sales — and payment runs **12–15 September**, separately from the booking itself. An SR-only account that has not been converted cannot book on any of those mornings. ## What this means for a normal trip

Strip out the corporate story and the practical advice is short.

**If you are travelling before 1 September:** nothing has changed for you. Book as you always did, at current fares.

**If you are travelling on or after 1 September:** those tickets have been on sale since **5 August at 10:00**, through 코레일+ and the Korail website. You will see Seoul and Suseo departures in one list for the first time, which makes it much easier to notice that a Suseo train leaves twenty minutes sooner — and much easier to book it without realising Suseo is nowhere near your hotel. **Check the station, not just the time.**

**If you are travelling over Chuseok:** the seat matters more than the fare. Have a working, logged-in unified Korail account days beforehand, and **look up your route's own sale morning** — Suseo departures on 10 September, Seoul-Busan on 11 September, conventional trains as early as 7 September. Getting the account right and the date wrong costs you the same as doing neither.

**If you were choosing between KTX and SRT:** that decision no longer exists in the form our older guide describes. It has become a simpler question — which of the two Seoul stations is closer to you.

## FAQ About the KTX and SRT Merger

**Q: Is the SRT being discontinued?**

**A:** No. The trains, the route and the Suseo terminus all continue. Only the name changes — from 1 September they run as **KTX-산천 (KTX-Sancheon)**. If you have an SRT ticket for a date after the merger, it is the same physical train either way; confirm the booking in the Korail+ app rather than assuming.

**Q: Do I need a new app?**

**A:** You need **코레일+ (Korail+)**, which went live on 3 August. If you already had Korail Talk, updating it is enough — it becomes Korail+. If you only ever used the SRT app, install Korail+ and migrate your account.

**Q: How much cheaper is Seoul to Busan?**

**A:** **₩59,800 → ₩54,400**, a saving of ₩5,400, from 1 September. Yongsan to Gwangju Songjeong goes ₩46,800 → ₩42,000. The average KTX reduction is about 10%, and it applies on the day rather than gradually.

**Q: Should I delay my trip to September to get the cheaper fare?**

**A:** Almost certainly not. ₩5,400 is a small fraction of what a day in Korea costs, and late September is Chuseok week, when trains, hotels and intercity buses are at their most contested and expensive. The fare cut is a nice side effect, not a reason to move.

**Q: When do Chuseok train tickets go on sale?**

**A:** Korail's official notice of **13 August 2026** sets it out by route. Priority passengers book **3-4 September**, everyone else **7-11 September**, 07:00-13:00. Conventional trains open on the 7th, KTX from Seoul or Cheongnyangni on the 8th, KTX from Yongsan on the 9th, **anything to or from Suseo on the 10th**, and Seoul-Busan on the Gyeongbu line on the 11th. Remaining seats go on open sale from **11 September at 15:00**, which is the only stage sold at station counters. Ignore the 20 and 25 August dates still circulating on travel blogs — those were the pre-merger pattern and they did not happen.

**Q: I have an SR account and no Korail account. What happens if I do nothing?**

**A:** Your account is not carried over automatically. Register with Korail and run the transfer through the conversion process, or you lose your SR booking history and any discount coupons attached to it.

**Q: Is Suseo or Seoul Station better?**

**A:** Whichever is closer to where you are sleeping. Suseo suits Gangnam, Jamsil, Songpa and Seongnam. Seoul Station suits almost everywhere else a visitor stays. Seat availability at Suseo improves with the merger, but not by enough to justify an hour of metro.

## The one thing to do this week

If you already hold a Korean rail account of any kind, **open it and check whether it needs migrating.** That takes five minutes now and is impossible to fix on a booking morning.

If you do not have one, create a Korail account, log in once, and confirm it works. Then watch `korail.com` for the Chuseok notice rather than a date someone reposted.

The merger is genuinely good news for visitors — one app, one search, more seats and a real fare cut. It just happens to land three weeks before the hardest week of the year to get a train, which is why the boring account admin is the part that decides whether you get a seat.
