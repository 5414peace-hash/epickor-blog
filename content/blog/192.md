Olive Young is not just a beauty store in Korea. It is the default: about 1,300 branches nationwide, and for most visitors it is where the K-beauty trip actually happens.

What almost every English guide tells you is **what** to buy. What none of them tells you is **when**, and that is the part that changes the bill. **Olive Young discounts on a published calendar** — a roughly week-long 빅세일 (big sale) four times a year and a short 올영데이 (Olive Young Day) every month. Land inside one and the same basket costs noticeably less. Land four days after one and you pay list.

If EpicKor's [Korea tourist shopping route](/blog/173) is the map for a whole Seoul shopping day, this page is the in-store Olive Young checklist: when to go, what things cost, what is worth buying, and what to leave on the shelf.

The scale of the tourist side is worth knowing too. **Foreign customers were 2% of Olive Young's domestic offline sales in 2022. They are now about 33%.** Cumulative foreign spending in its Korean stores passed **₩1 trillion in August 2026**, three months earlier than the company expected. At the 센트럴 명동 타운 (Central Myeongdong Town) branch, **roughly 95% of sales come from foreign customers**, and it was the highest-grossing Olive Young in the country last year.

## Quick Answer: What Should You Buy At Olive Young?

Buy sunscreen, one hydrating step you will actually use, pimple patches, and a lip tint or two. Skip full routines, strong actives you have not tested, and anything large and liquid.

If you only have one visit, this basket works:

- one sunscreen you tested on the back of your hand
- one cleanser or toner pad, if you genuinely need it
- one sheet-mask pack or pimple-patch set
- one lip tint or hand cream as a gift
- one small tool

That gives you the fun of shopping without creating a suitcase problem.

## Why Olive Young Became A Tourist Stop

Myeongdong is the clearest illustration. **There are nine Olive Young branches in Myeongdong alone**, some of them a one- to three-minute walk apart, each given a different job — K-beauty experience zones, K-pop tie-in areas, and so on. About 4.5 million foreign visitors came to Myeongdong in the first half of 2025, more than any other Seoul commercial district, and Olive Young built for that rather than around it.

For a traveler this has a practical consequence: **the branch you walk into is not necessarily the one you want.** The tourist-heavy flagships have the widest stock, multilingual staff and till-side tax refund. A small neighbourhood branch has narrower stock and staff who process refunds less often.

## How To Read The Store Without Getting Overwhelmed

Olive Young is organised by category, not by brand story. Skincare, suncare, masks, makeup, hair, body, health, and a snack section that has nothing to do with your face.

![A skincare aisle inside an Olive Young branch: shelves of toners, serums and cleansers with Korean price tags and promotional flags, with Dr.Jart+, BOH and isoi brand signage on the facial-care wall behind.](/assets/images/posts/192/oliveyoung-skincare-aisle.jpg)
*Look at the shelf edges, not the shelf. Those coloured tags are the promotion markers — 1+1, 2+1 and event pricing — and they change with the sale calendar rather than with the product. Photo: Pkccccj / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.*

Three habits make the store manageable:

1. **Decide your category before you walk in.** "Sunscreen and one tint" survives contact with the shelves. "K-beauty" does not.
2. **Read the shelf-edge tags, not just the price.** 1+1 and 2+1 offers are common and are how the basket quietly doubles.
3. **Use the rankings as a filter, not an instruction.** The in-store ranking displays tell you what is selling in Korea right now, which is useful signal and terrible reason to buy something for your own face.

## Buy: Korean Sunscreen

This is the category where Korea genuinely earns the trip. Textures are lighter than most Western equivalents, finish options are wider, and the price band is friendly — a mid-tier bottle sits around ₩20,000.

Test on the back of your hand in-store: white cast, stickiness, and smell are the three things that decide whether a sunscreen gets used or abandoned. If you burn easily, check that the label carries both SPF and a PA rating.

## Buy: Toner Pads, But Only If You Will Use Them

Toner pads are convenient and Korea does them well. They are also bulky, heavy, and the single most common thing left half-used in a bathroom cabinet six months later.

A 70-sheet tub lists around ₩28,000 and frequently sells for a third less. If you already use a toner you like, this is a trend purchase, not an upgrade. If you travel a lot or hate decanting liquids, it is a real convenience.

## Buy: Pimple Patches And Small Tools

Patches are the easiest thing to recommend: light, cheap, flat, and they work. Buy two sizes if the brand offers them. Small tools — tweezers, spatulas, silicone applicators — pack flat and cost little.

## Be Careful With Actives

Retinol, high-percentage vitamin C, and strong exfoliating acids are where holiday shopping causes actual damage. You are travelling, your skin is already dealing with a new climate and a different water supply, and you will not know for two weeks whether a product suits you.

Buy at most one active, in the smallest size offered, and start it after you get home.

## Makeup: Lip Tints Are The Easy Win

Tints are Korea's most exportable makeup category: small, light, inexpensive at around ₩10,000, and forgiving of a shade mistake in a way foundation is not. They are the correct answer to "I need to bring back six small gifts."

Foundation is the opposite. Shade ranges are narrower than in Western markets and matching under store lighting is unreliable. Skip it.

## Tax Refund: The Rules, And The One Document A Photo Will Not Replace

Olive Young does **immediate refund at the till** in tourist-facing branches, which is why the counters carry those lit signs. The refund is roughly **5–8% of the purchase price**, deducted on the spot.

Both immediate-refund caps were **doubled on 1 January 2024**, from ₩500,000 per transaction and ₩2.5 million total. Any guide written before that understates the ceiling by half.

**Not everything in the basket qualifies.** Hygiene products, shopping bags, and dried fruit are excluded — which matters because Olive Young sells all three, and the snack aisle is right there.

Two more practical notes. **Go between 10:00 and 12:00 if you can.** The tourist branches are busiest from about 14:00 to 20:00, and the queue at a fourteen-till Myeongdong checkout is a real part of your afternoon. And keep receipts until you leave the country — if you shop near the end of a trip, check that liquids and aerosols fit airline rules and your luggage weight before you commit.

The boring packing rule is the one that saves you: **do not buy more liquid skincare than you can comfortably check in.**

## Best Olive Young Strategy For A Short Korea Trip

If you have one visit, work in this order:

1. **Check the date against the sale calendar** before you decide how much to buy.
2. **Go in the morning**, to a tourist-facing branch, with your physical passport.
3. **Buy the basket above**, testing sunscreen on your hand first.
4. **Keep the single payment under ₩1,000,000** so the refund happens at the till.
5. **Stop.** The best haul is the one you finish.

## What To Skip At Olive Young

Skip anything that depends on fantasy instead of need.

Do not buy an entire routine because one influencer posted a shelf photo. Do not buy a strong product because it is on sale. Do not buy a huge set because the per-item price looks good if you cannot use it.

Also be careful with:

- fragrance-heavy products if your skin is sensitive
- multiple exfoliants
- full-size shampoo and body wash if luggage is tight
- foundation without shade testing
- "limited" sets that include products you do not want

The best Olive Young haul is not the biggest one. It is the one you actually use.

## FAQ

**Q: When does Olive Young have sales?**

Four big sales a year, roughly a week each, straddling the month boundary — late February into March, late May into June, late August into September, and late November into December. Plus a shorter 올영데이 every month around the 25th to 27th, which is coupon-led through the membership app. Confirm the exact dates on Olive Young's site; the pattern is stable but the days shift slightly.

**Q: Can I get a tax refund at Olive Young?**

Yes, immediately at the till in tourist-facing branches, at roughly 5–8% of the purchase. You need a minimum of ₩15,000 in one store, a single payment under ₩1,000,000, and no more than ₩5,000,000 in total during your stay.

**Q: Do I need my actual passport, or is a photo enough?**

Your actual passport. A phone photo or a photocopy is not accepted. Olive Young's Myeongdong store prints "PLEASE PREPARE YOUR PHYSICAL PASSPORT" in English and Chinese along every checkout counter, which tells you how often people try.

**Q: Which Olive Young branch should I go to?**

A tourist-facing one — the Myeongdong branches, or a large flagship. They carry the widest stock and process refunds constantly. There are nine Olive Young stores in Myeongdong alone, some one to three minutes apart. Small neighbourhood branches have narrower selection.

**Q: What is the best time of day to go?**

Between 10:00 and 12:00. The tourist branches are busiest from about 14:00 to 20:00.

**Q: How much does Korean sunscreen cost at Olive Young?**

A mid-tier bottle sits around ₩20,000 — a 50 ml madecassoside sun serum lists at ₩22,000 as this was written. Prices drop during the quarterly sale.

**Q: Is everything cheaper at Olive Young than at home?**

Not automatically. Korean brands with international distribution are often close in price abroad, and sometimes cheaper on sale there. What Korea gives you is **range and freshness** — new releases, textures and formats that have not been exported yet — rather than a guaranteed discount.

**Q: What should I not buy?**

Foundation without a shade test, strong actives you have not used before, full-size shampoo and body wash if luggage is tight, and any "limited" set containing products you do not want. Hygiene items, shopping bags and dried fruit also do not qualify for the tax refund.

## Final Takeaway

Olive Young rewards planning more than enthusiasm. **Check the date against the sale calendar, go in the morning, take your physical passport, and buy a basket you can finish.**

The rest of the store will still be there. So will the next quarterly sale.
