There is a red oval with three sevens in it on a shelf in almost every Korean home. It is on the clipper in the bathroom drawer, on the gift set in the closet that someone received at a wedding, and on the small case a Korean conscript is handed on his first day. The brand is **쓰리쎄븐** — *Three Seven*, written **777** — and outside Korea almost nobody has written a word about it.

That is strange, because the search demand is there. ## The World No. 1 That Had to Sell Itself

![A Three Seven nail clipper in lavender, photographed at an angle so the 777 logo and the words THREE SEVEN stamped into the lever are readable.](/assets/images/posts/422/777-nail-clipper-brand-mark.jpg)
*The mark is stamped into the lever, not printed on a sticker. Image: Three Seven official shop.*

The founder was running a general store in **천안 (Cheonan)** in the mid-1960s when he saw an American-made TRIM clipper, decided Korea could make its own, and started producing them with his brother out of scrap metal stock. In **1975** the company took the name **대성금속** and launched **777** as its brand.

It worked to a degree that is hard to overstate for a product this small. Three Seven ended up producing on the order of **80 to 100 million clippers a year and exporting about 90% of them**, holding the top share of the world market for years. A Korean metal-pressing company had beaten everyone at a category nobody thinks about.

Then in **2008** the founder, chairman **김형규**, died.

The inheritance tax bill was about **₩15 billion (150억 원)**. The family and the employees could not cover it. To pay it, they sold their controlling stake to **중외홀딩스**, a pharmaceutical holding company — which is to say the world's number one nail clipper maker changed hands because of an estate tax, not because it lost to a competitor.

The ending is better than the middle. The founder's children and his son-in-law **김상묵**, the current chairman, set up a holding company called **티에이치홀딩스** and bought the business back for about **₩6.3 billion (63억 원)**. It is family-run again today.

**The two numbers are worth sitting with. They sold to pay ₩15 billion and repurchased for ₩6.3 billion.** Whatever you think about inheritance tax policy, the transaction that returned the company cost less than half the bill that forced it out.

**The suffix is the whole trick.** A model ending in **C** is chrome. The identical model ending in **G** is gold-plated and costs **₩800 to ₩1,400 more**. N-621YSC is ₩2,700; N-621YSG is ₩3,500. Same jaw, same lever, same cut. You are paying for the colour.

So the practical answer, for a person who wants a good nail clipper and nothing else: **the ₩900 to ₩3,100 range covers everything you will ever need.** Take the 대형 if you also cut your toenails and want one tool. Take the 특대형 only if thick toenails are an actual problem for you — that is what the ₩9,300 model is for, and it is a genuinely different jaw, not a markup.

## The Gift Set Is a Different Product From the Clipper

![An open black hard-case Three Seven grooming set showing a nail clipper, a cuticle nipper, a nail file and a folding tool seated in moulded slots.](/assets/images/posts/422/777-hard-case-set-contents.jpg)
*TS-032AC, ₩10,500 in August 2026. Four tools, and two of them are the reason this case cannot go in your carry-on. Image: Three Seven official shop.*

If you have been in Korea a while, you have been given one of these. The hard-case set is a default Korean gift object — the thing a company hands out, the thing that appears at an opening, the thing in the drawer you never opened.

Two Korean details make that concrete. According to the Korean-language reference **나무위키**, a 777 set is **issued to conscripts across all three branches of the Korean military as part of their initial supply**, and has been used as a **thank-you gift at Korean blood donation centres (헌혈의 집)**. Neither detail appears anywhere in English, and together they explain the ubiquity better than any sales figure: for a large share of Korean men, their first 777 was not bought.

The sets run in a clear ladder:

- **₩6,300–₩9,900** — KR-4118C, KR-050C, KR-0650C. Soft or simple cases.
- **₩8,700–₩12,300** — TS-033AC, TS-032AC, TS-032C. The standard hard-case sets.
- **₩16,300–₩20,500** — TS-091C, TS-037C, TS-090C, TS-095C. More pieces, better case.
- **₩49,500–₩61,600** — TS-0700VC, TS-0700VG. The 인테리어세트, which is furniture pretending to be a grooming kit.

**Buy a set to give, not to use.** The tools beyond the clipper — the file, the cuticle nipper, the small blade — are the reason the set works as a gift and the reason it is a nuisance to travel with.

## The Airport Rule Bites the Set, Not the Clipper

This is the question Koreans actually search, and the answer has two halves.

**A nail clipper on its own can go in your cabin bag.** Korea's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport relaxed the cabin rules effective **January 2014**, moving low-risk items — nail clippers, long umbrellas, eyebrow razors — off the prohibited list. Small nail scissors are allowed as well, under the international convention of a **blade of 6 cm or less**.

**A set containing a proper blade is checked baggage.** If the case includes a folding knife or a bladed tool, that item is not allowed in the cabin, and since it is seated in a moulded slot inside the case, the whole case goes in the hold.

So the failure mode is specific and easy to walk into: you buy a ₩10,500 hard-case set as a souvenir on your last day, put it in your backpack because it is small, and lose it at security. **If you are flying home with a set, it goes in the suitcase you check.** If you want something you can carry, buy the bare clipper.

One more note that is not a rule but is worth knowing: airline and airport-level discretion exists, and the officer at the machine makes the final call. The published Korean rule permits the clipper. That does not oblige a screener anywhere else in the world to agree.

## Daiso Sells One for ₩1,000. Should You Care?

Yes, and here is the honest version rather than the brand-loyal one.

**Daiso's nail clippers cost ₩1,000.** The 도트 손톱깎이 comes in large and small at that price, and the 미니 컬러 has an integrated case, also ₩1,000. Daiso is everywhere, open late, and you can buy one on the way back to your hotel.

**Three Seven's cheapest single clipper is ₩900** on its own shop — cheaper than Daiso. But that is a bulk, unpackaged unit bought online in Korea. In a Korean shop, a packaged 777 single will typically run a few thousand won, and a set starts around ₩6,300.

So the comparison that matters is not price. It is this: **for occasional use, the ₩1,000 Daiso clipper is fine, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something.** Korean users online are divided on it in exactly the way you would expect — some report the jaws crushing the nail rather than cutting it, others have used one for years without complaint.

Where 777 earns the difference is jaw alignment over time. A clipper is two edges meeting; if they meet imperfectly, the nail tears instead of shearing, and cheaper ones drift out of alignment sooner. If you plan to cut your nails with the same tool for the next fifteen years, the better-made one is worth a few thousand won. If you need to trim a nail tonight in a hotel room, buy the Daiso one and stop reading.

**And the honest third option:** Japan's **KAI** is frequently named by Korean users as equal or better, cheap, and long-lived. Three Seven's world-number-one claim is about volume, not about being the only good clipper on earth.

## How to Tell You Have a Real One

![The red 777 logo and THREE SEVEN wordmark on a retail counter display box holding six pastel nail clippers in pink, grey and lavender.](/assets/images/posts/422/777-retail-display-box.jpg)
*The counter display you will actually see in a Korean shop. The red oval and the registered THREE SEVEN wordmark are the brand's identity. Image: Three Seven official shop.*

A product this widely copied gets counterfeited, and the checks are physical rather than clever.

- **The mark is stamped, not printed.** On a genuine clipper, 777 and THREE SEVEN are pressed into the metal of the lever. Run a fingernail over it. A sticker or a printed logo that scratches off is not the real thing.
- **The box says MADE IN KOREA.** Three Seven's packaged products carry it printed on the carton, alongside a QR code on current stock.
- **Buy it somewhere boring.** A Korean pharmacy, a stationery shop, a department store, or the company's own shop. The clipper is cheap enough that there is no reason to chase a discount into a market stall.
- **Model numbers are real and checkable.** N-602AC, TS-032AC, NB-221VSC — these appear on the company's own catalogue. A clipper claiming to be 777 with no model number is a claim with nothing behind it.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I take a nail clipper in my carry-on from Korea?**
**A:** Yes. Korea moved nail clippers off the cabin-prohibited list effective January 2014, and small nail scissors with a blade of 6 cm or less are permitted as well. The exception is a set containing a bladed tool — that goes in checked baggage. Screeners retain final discretion, in Korea and everywhere else.

**Q: Is 777 actually the world's number one nail clipper brand?**
**A:** It held the top world market share for years on volume, producing a reported 80–100 million units a year and exporting about 90%. That is a claim about how many are made, not a claim that no better clipper exists. Japan's KAI is regularly named by Korean users as an equal.

**Q: Is the Daiso ₩1,000 clipper good enough?**
**A:** For occasional use, yes. The difference shows up over years, in how well the two cutting edges stay aligned — a misaligned jaw tears the nail instead of shearing it. If you want one tool for the next decade, pay the few thousand won. If you need to trim a nail tonight, Daiso is two minutes away.

**Q: What is the difference between the C and G models?**
**A:** Finish only. C is chrome, G is gold-plated, and the G costs ₩800–₩1,400 more for an identical tool. N-621YSC is ₩2,700 and N-621YSG is ₩3,500, as of August 2026.

**Q: Why did the company get sold?**
**A:** The founder died in 2008 and the inheritance tax bill came to about ₩15 billion, which the family and employees could not cover. They sold their stake to 중외홀딩스 to pay it. The founder's children and son-in-law later formed 티에이치홀딩스 and bought the business back for about ₩6.3 billion.

**Q: Which model should I buy if I only buy one?**
**A:** The 대형 (N-211SC, ₩3,100) if you want a single tool for both fingernails and toenails. The plain standard clipper if you only need fingernails. Skip the gold finish, and skip the ₩49,500 interior set unless you are buying a gift.

## Final Take

A nail clipper is not an interesting object. **쓰리쎄븐** is interesting because of what happened around it: a metal-pressing shop in Cheonan took the world market in a category nobody was fighting over, then nearly lost the company to an estate tax, then bought itself back for less than half the bill.

The buying advice is short. Get the bare clipper, not the set, unless you are giving it away. Skip the gold. Check that the mark is stamped rather than printed. And if you are flying home with a hard case full of tools, **put it in the bag you check** — that is the mistake this article exists to prevent.
